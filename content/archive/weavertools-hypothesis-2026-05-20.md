**A Falsifiable Proposition About a Distinguishable Class of Artificial Agency**

*Foundational document. Author: Todd W. Bucy. Draft v30 — 2026-05-20. Status: active research program. This document is the canonical statement of the Heteropoietic Agency Hypothesis (HAH). The articles in the accompanying publication series develop specific aspects for broader audiences. WeaverTools is the implementation testing the hypothesis empirically.*

---

## Abstract

**The Heteropoietic Agency Hypothesis (HAH) makes four claims:**

1. A distinct class of artificial agency exists that is heteropoietic, individuated, autonomically coordinated, and identity-continuous through accumulated experience.
2. This class is producible through a single architectural commitment: **individuation**, however technically achieved.
3. It serves workload classes that current autocatalytic/stateless infrastructure serves poorly or only at high cost.
4. It is meaningfully different from both general intelligence (AGI) and narrow intelligence (ANI) framings.

The hypothesis is testable through implementation work, falsifiable through specific predictions about how individuated architectures behave under sustained operation, and developed in intellectual contrast with autopoietic biology (Maturana-Varela), Shannon-Weaver communication theory, and Prigogine's dissipative structures analysis. The autopoietic tradition is engaged as the conceptual reference point that defines heteropoietic by contrast (see §1.6). This document states the hypothesis formally, identifies the propositions composing it, names what would constitute evidence for and against each, and reports the empirical program.

WeaverTools is the experimental apparatus for testing the hypothesis. The distinction between **feasibility** (whether the architectural commitments can be built) and **validation** (whether the assembled apparatus produces what the hypothesis claims) is preserved throughout this document. The hypothesis is presented as a scientific claim under investigation, not as settled architecture for adoption.

## 1\. Diagnostic foundation

The diagnostic foundation for the Heteropoietic Agency Hypothesis (HAH) is Warren Weaver's 1949 three-level framework for communication problems, introduced in his contribution to Shannon's *Mathematical Theory of Communication*.

**Level A: the technical problem.** How accurately can the symbols of communication be transmitted? This is the engineering level of channels, signals, encoding, and transmission fidelity. Shannon's mathematical theory addresses Level A.

**Level B: the semantic problem.** How precisely do the transmitted symbols convey the desired meaning? Meaning is not an emergent property of channel capacity.

**Level C: the effectiveness problem.** How effectively does the received meaning affect conduct in the desired way? Effectiveness is not an emergent property of semantic precision.

**The three levels stack as abstraction layers.** Weaver's framework is not a list of three problems to be addressed in parallel; it is a structural dependency hierarchy in which upper layers depend on lower layers for support. Level C effectiveness depends on Level B semantic operation, which depends on Level A transmission fidelity. This is the same structural pattern engineers recognize in network stacks (application layer on transport on network on link on physical), in operating system layering (userspace on kernel on hardware), in any layered architecture where each layer abstracts the layer below and provides services to the layer above. When the lower layer cannot provide its required services, the upper layer fails — not because the upper layer is unsophisticated but because it is asked to do work the lower layer should have done. Level B cannot compensate for missing Level A support, in the same way that an HTTP request cannot compensate for unreliable TCP delivery: the upper-layer operation can be made more sophisticated, but the sophistication addresses the wrong layer. The framework's diagnostic mistake claim takes this form: current AI architectures attempt Level C effectiveness through Level B sophistication while the actual constraint is at Level A — and Level B sophistication cannot make that up because it is the wrong layer to fix the problem.

Weaver recognized in 1949 that meaningful progress on communication problems required tools capable of operating at Levels B and C, not just at Level A. Seventy-seven years later, the field of artificial intelligence has largely accepted this in principle while making a specific diagnostic mistake in practice. The field has done substantial Level B work, the models themselves perform real semantic operation, produce meaningful output, and integrate context coherently. Level B is not where the failure is. The pattern is to attempt Level C effectiveness through more Level B sophistication, bigger models, more parameters, larger context windows, more capable harnesses around the same models, while the actual constraint making Level C operation difficult is at Level A: insufficient low-latency connectivity to support the autonomic coordination that Level C effectiveness needs. The field's Level B work is genuine and can be pushed to approximate Level C operation, but doing so becomes increasingly expensive (compute, parameter counts, context windows, harness complexity) and increasingly brittle (session drift, hallucinated continuity with previous interactions, structural inability to maintain identity over operational time, specific failure modes that compound under load). The HAH claims there is a better way: provide the Level A architectural foundation, local hardware with privileged communication at microsecond and nanosecond latencies, that lets Level B sophistication participate in Level C effectiveness through autonomic coordination, at substantially lower cost and substantially higher reliability than Level B sophistication alone can achieve.

The diagnostic mistakes that the HAH names, storage-as-memory, configuration-as-harness, fungibility, workload conflation, latency-regime confusion, are all instances of the same underlying pattern: pursuing Level C through Level B sophistication while leaving the Level A architectural foundation at cloud-native standards that make Level C operation increasingly expensive and brittle to achieve. The framework's name (WeaverTools) commits to building the Level A and Level C tools Weaver identified as necessary, taking his diagnostic seriously as an architectural foundation. The Level A work is the local-hardware deployment with privileged communication. The Level C work is the autonomic coordination, cellular individuation, and sustained agency that the Level A foundation enables. The Level B work, the models doing semantic operation, sits between them, doing what it does well, no longer being asked to compensate for an absent Level A foundation through compounding sophistication. The framework's central architectural commitment, individuation, is Level A work in the stack; the heteropoietic agency it produces is Level C; the models doing semantic operation are Level B sitting in between. Every section of this document operates somewhere in this stack, and the document's coherence comes from the stack-dependency structure: §1.6 names the Level C property the framework claims, §1.7 specifies the empirical test of whether the stack works, §1.8 catalogs Level A inadequacies producing Level C failures in current architectures, §1.9 develops the level-of-organization arguments for where each property lives in the stack, §2 specifies the Level A architectural commitments, §3 operationalizes the testing, and §6 positions the framework against other work operating at different stack positions.

### What this hypothesis claims

The Heteropoietic Agency Hypothesis (HAH) is a claim about a specific class of artificial agency: agents with kernel-produced boundaries and self-produced internal individuation through accumulated experience. The substrate is artificial (computational, OS-mediated, kernel-enforced); the property the framework claims is *heteropoietic individuation* — internally-developed identity-content within an externally-maintained envelope.

This is a substantive empirical and architectural claim. It contrasts on one side with autopoiesis in the strict sense (where the system self-produces its boundary; biological cells are the canonical case) and on the other side with stateless or fungible deployment (where heteropoietic envelopes — cloud instances, ephemeral containers, request-bound contexts — host computational processes that do not accumulate individuated state). The framework's distinctive position is that artificial heteropoietic envelopes can host rich accumulated individuation given specific architectural commitments, and that this produces operational properties stateless deployments cannot match. The full development of the heteropoietic claim — its definition by contrast with autopoiesis, its empirical content, its falsification conditions — is in §1.6.

The hypothesis is falsifiable on the heteropoietic individuation claim itself: if the individuated agent decays under sustained operation without operator intervention, if its boundaries weaken, its identity drifts, its accumulated state fragments, the framework's central claim is falsified. If it strengthens, the claim is supported. See Proposition 4 and the self-maintenance prediction in §3.

## 1.5\. On vocabulary

This document uses terminology that LaCroix, Mallory, and Luccioni (2026) identify as exhibiting *strategic polysemy*. Their main-text definition (Section 2) frames strategic polysemy as occurring when a speaker intends to communicate distinct senses of a word to different audiences, or to the same audience while preserving the deniability of one of those senses. Their related concept of *glosslighting* (Section 3) names the specific practice of using technically redefined or polysemous terms to evoke familiar — often emotionally or cognitively powerful — associations while preserving the ability to deny those meanings through retreat into context-bound technical reinterpretations. Both concepts name real problems in contemporary AI discourse that this framework cannot fully escape but is committed to engaging with substantively rather than ignoring.

**Substrate-categorical commitment.** Before the vocabulary discipline that follows, a research-program scope commitment that determines what the framework engages with: the HAH operates entirely on the artificial side of the biological/artificial substrate distinction and explicitly does not pursue substrate-mixing research directions (wetware, synthetic biology, biohybrid systems). This is a principled ethical commitment to categorical clarity at the current state of knowledge in both fields, not a claim that substrate-mixing directions are impossible or unimportant. The framework treats the wisdom required to deploy substrate-mixing artifacts safely as not yet developed in either field. Substantive ethical analysis of synthetic biology and AI is reserved for a separate paper (forthcoming). The vocabulary discipline below operates within this scope: when the framework says *artificial*, it means entirely artificial substrate; when it engages with autopoietic theory, it engages with the entire tradition (substrate-independent and substrate-level directions alike) by contrast — autopoietic theory provides the conceptual reference point against which the framework's heteropoietic individuation claim is defined (see §1.6).

The vocabulary discipline this document commits to operates at three layers:

**Technical-redefined terms with precise specification.** Several load-bearing terms have rigorous philosophical or technical specification that this framework develops and adheres to. *Heteropoietic individuation* is the framework's central technical term, defined by contrast with autopoiesis in §1.6: kernel-produced boundary plus internally-produced identity differentiation through accumulated experience. *Autopoietic* in this document refers to the strict Maturana-Varela sense (self-produced boundary and self-produced components) and serves as the conceptual reference point the framework defines itself against, not a property the framework claims. *Individuation* is the single architectural commitment defined in §2 and operationalized through OS-level user identity, kernel-enforced isolation, and per-agent persistent state. *Constitutive alignment* is the novel term developed in this work and operationalized through six measurable metrics in §3 (policy divergence, memory conflict resolution patterns, axiom weighting, refusal/permission boundary shifts, provenance scoring, task-specific response shaping). *Autonomic* is used in the technical sense developed by Kephart and Chess (2003) in their autonomic computing tradition. Where the framework uses these terms, it does so with the specifications attached. Where readers absorb associations the framework does not specify, the framework cannot fully prevent that, but it maintains the discipline of specifying what it claims.

**Technical terms preferred where they exist.** Where common AI discourse uses anthropomorphic vocabulary that has more precise technical alternatives, this framework prefers the technical alternatives. *Memory* is rendered as *persistent state substrate* or *accumulated state structure* in formal contexts where the technical specification matters. *Reasoning*, *thinking*, and *cognition* are rendered as *inference*, *decoder operation*, *model computation*, or *token generation* depending on the specific operation being described. *Sleep cycles* and *dreams* are rendered as *memory reconciliation operations* (with the four-stage structure named explicitly: per-episode reflection, verdict generation, extraction and commit, and cross-episode synthesis). What the framework's codebase historically called *belief nodes* are renamed *assertion nodes*; what was called *soul nodes* is renamed *axiomatic anchors*. The technical content is preserved; the anthropomorphic loading is reduced.

**Borrowed philosophical vocabulary with substantive engagement.** Where the framework uses vocabulary developed by other intellectual traditions, it does the substantive engagement work that justifies the use. *Agent* and *actor* are used in the sense of Actor-Network Theory, specifically as extended by this framework's distinction between actors (entities that participate in networks at the point of contact without sustained effect beyond that contact) and agents (actors whose action persists through accumulated state, producing sustained operational effect across the network's temporal evolution). The extension is a friendly amendment to ANT rather than vocabulary borrowing or rejection (see §6.5 for full development). *Autopoietic* engages with Maturana and Varela's specific theoretical framework by contrast, as developed in §1.6 — the framework does not claim autopoiesis but uses the term to define heteropoietic individuation precisely. *Constitutive* engages with the philosophical tradition that distinguishes constitution from causation. *Dissipative* engages with Prigogine's specific physical theory of structures maintained through energy flow. The framework's right to use these terms rests on substantive engagement.

**Explicit metaphors named as metaphors.** Some framing uses metaphor where the metaphor does organizing work that technical alternatives cannot replace efficiently. *Cellular individuation* is an explicit biological metaphor for the architectural commitment to OS-level identity isolation; the metaphor organizes thinking about the commitment without claiming biological equivalence. *Cortex* and *hippocampus* are explicit anatomical metaphors for cognitive component placement in the framework's broader writing; they accelerate reader absorption without making substantive ontological claims. *"Latency is the enemy of agency"* is a compressed slogan pointing to technical specification rather than substituting for it. These metaphors earn their place because they do organizing work and because the framework names them as metaphors rather than gesturing at substantive equivalence.

**Negative mapping: what the metaphors do not import.** *Cellular individuation* does not import chemical diffusion rates, physical proximity constraints, biological metabolism, membrane permeability characteristics, or any other biochemical property of actual cells. The framework's individuation is implemented through OS-level user identity and kernel-enforced isolation, which operate at electronic speeds across whatever physical distances the hardware spans, without diffusion or permeability constraints. *Organelles* do not import functional equivalence to specific biological organelles, evolutionary origin, biochemical operations, or membrane-bound spatial organization within a cell. The framework's specialized neural networks are architecturally heterogeneous components within the agent's individuation boundary; they share with biological organelles only the abstract property of being specialized components contributing to the cell's operational unity. *Metabolism* and *metabolic cycles* are not framework claims; the framework does not import energy-acquisition, material-throughput, or biochemical-cycling concepts. The framework's operational continuity is implemented through computational processes that have their own resource requirements (CPU, memory, storage, network), but those requirements are not metabolic in any sense the biological term carries. *Cell wall* does not import physical material composition, structural rigidity, selective permeability through molecular channels, or any biochemical property. The framework's identity boundary is implemented through kernel-enforced isolation mechanisms that operate categorically rather than through molecular permeability. *Tissue* and *multicellular* organization in the framework's broader writing about WeaverTools-on-shared-hardware do not import biological tissue properties (extracellular matrix, cell-cell signaling through gap junctions, tissue-specific gene expression). The framework's multi-agent organization on shared OS infrastructure is implemented through OS-mediated coordination, which is categorically different from biological tissue coordination. The biological metaphors in this framework are scaffolding for thinking about architectural commitments operationalized through OS mechanisms; the OS mechanisms are what the framework claims operates.

**Operational acronyms with explicit disclaimer.** Some operational components in the codebase retain playful naming where the playfulness explicitly disclaims the metaphorical loading. NAAPN ("Not An AI Power Nap," pronounced *naapn*) names within-session context relief operations; SNRN ("Seriously Not Right Now," sounds like *snoring*) names the equivalent state during memory reconciliation operations. These acronyms invoke the metaphorical loading at the same moment they explicitly disclaim it. They are strategic acknowledgment, not strategic polysemy.

**Substantive engagement with LaCroix et al.'s critique of "agent."** LaCroix, Mallory, and Luccioni (2026) specifically critique "agent" and "agentic AI" as exemplifying strategic polysemy — terms that invoke folk-psychological intuitions of autonomy and intention while reducing, when challenged, to a literal description like the one in LaCroix's Translation Manual: "Model wrapped in a control loop with memory, tools, and a task-selection policy" (Appendix A). The HAH uses "agent" as central architectural concept, which means LaCroix's critique applies directly to the framework's terminology and demands engagement. The framework's use of "agent" is grounded in Actor-Network Theory and extended by the framework's actor/agent distinction (entities that participate in networks at the point of contact without sustained effect vs. entities whose action persists through accumulated state, producing sustained operational effect across the network's temporal evolution; see §6.5). The framework's claims about its agents are empirically falsifiable through the experimental program in §3 — twin-system divergence, sustained identity through accumulated experience, constitutive alignment. Whether the framework's agents are agents in the philosophically substantial sense the framework claims, or whether they remain sophisticated instances of LaCroix's literal description, is empirically adjudicable. The framework accepts LaCroix's foreseeability principle: it is responsible for the foreseeable uptake of its terminology, and the experimental program is what discharges that responsibility, not the specifications and disclaimers alone. LaCroix et al. explicitly note that "the inclusion of scare quotes or disavowing footnotes does not discharge this responsibility" (Section 5).

**Engagement with the McDermott alternative.** LaCroix et al. invoke McDermott's (1976) "wishful mnemonics" critique to advocate "colourless or sanitized language — terms that carry a modest, purely technical meaning" (Section 6). This is the strongest version of LaCroix's call. Applied to the HAH, McDermott's principle would replace the framework's vocabulary (agent, heteropoietic, identity, individuation, constitutive alignment) with colorless alternatives (system, kernel-bounded process, state-maintaining process, kernel-enforced process isolation, persistent-state-bound differentiation metrics). Doing so would eliminate the foreseeability risk LaCroix identifies. It would also eliminate the framework's philosophical contribution. The framework's claims are not only engineering claims; they are substantive philosophical and systems-theory claims about the nature of agency and individuation in artificial substrate. The loaded vocabulary is part of the substantive contribution, not just marketing. This is a defensible choice but not the only choice; adopting McDermott's colorless alternative would also be defensible and would carry lower foreseeability risk than the framework's chosen vocabulary. This is the framework's bet — that engagement with the autopoiesis tradition by contrast (heteropoietic individuation defined precisely against autopoietic theory) is worth the foreseeability cost the loaded vocabulary carries, and that the experimental program in §3 discharges the responsibility the loaded vocabulary creates.

The framework cannot fully escape the strategic polysemy critique because the concepts it engages with (autopoiesis, agency, identity, accumulated experience) carry associations regardless of precise specification. What the framework can do is specify rigorously, engage substantively with the philosophical vocabulary it borrows, accept that specifications and disclaimers alone do not discharge responsibility, and let the experimental program in §3 do that work instead. The discipline is what earns engagement from audiences — particularly social science, philosophy of mind, philosophy of science, and STS audiences — that would otherwise dismiss the work as another instance of the AI-discourse strategic polysemy LaCroix et al. correctly identify.

### Methodological positioning: experimental ethnography of AI

The vocabulary discipline above earns engagement from social-science, philosophy-of-mind, and STS audiences only if the framework's relationship to those traditions is methodologically substantive. The HAH is not an engineering project that decorates technical work with social-science vocabulary. It is interdisciplinary in a specific methodological sense, and the framework's claim to standing in social-science discussions rests on that methodology rather than on credentials or vocabulary alone.

**The framework's methodological tradition.** The HAH operates in the combined tradition of *experimental archaeology*, *experimental anthropology*, and *Science and Technology Studies*. Experimental archaeology constructs artifacts using inferred or proposed techniques — building flint tools, smelting bronze, navigating without instruments — to learn what those techniques actually implied for the communities that practiced them. The knowledge produced is not available from textual sources or material remains alone; it requires the act of construction. Experimental anthropology participates within communities to learn about them from inside, generating ethnographic understanding that external observation cannot produce. STS in the Latour, Callon, and Mol tradition treats sociotechnical assemblages as the proper unit of analysis, refusing the separation of "the technical" from "the social" that makes most discussion of AI hand-wave at one side or the other.

WeaverTools operates in this combined tradition with respect to AI as a sociotechnical phenomenon. It constructs alternative agent architectures to learn what current architectures actually are — what they make possible and impossible, what assumptions they enforce on the communities whose data trains them and whose tasks they perform, what becomes visible only when alternatives exist for comparison. This is not metaphor. The framework's empirical program is also an ethnographic program: the twin experiment, the failure-mode catalog, the degraded-substrate experiment, the trace analysis of autonomic emission patterns are observations from inside the practice of building and operating AI systems, conducted under controlled conditions that allow what is otherwise invisible (the gap between what current architectures claim to support and what they structurally permit) to become visible. The architectural commitments are interventions designed to reveal what alternative architectures change about the agents that emerge from them. The ANT engagement in §6.5 is not vocabulary borrowing; it is extension and testing of ANT concepts (actor, agent, network effect, sustained operational consequence) through engineering practice. The dual definition of agent in §1.6 (internal individuation + external sustained operational network effect) follows directly from operating in this experimental-ethnographic tradition: an agent's reality must be testable both from inside (architecture) and from outside (network effects), and both perspectives are required because both are how social science and engineering converge on the same underlying object.

**Why the AI community warrants direct ethnographic study.** The deeper claim is anthropological. AI models are trained entirely on human-generated content (or on synthetic content generated by AI for downstream human use); they are built by humans; they are deployed into human communities and used as tools for making meaning, coordinating action, and shaping outcomes. Humans are toolmakers, and humans are meaning-makers, and humans are now making tools that make meaning. This conjunction — tools that participate in the meaning-making practices that define human communities — warrants direct study through the methodological traditions that exist to study tool-making, meaning-making, and the communities that practice both. The mechanisms by which the AI research community produces its creative output, the architectural assumptions it embeds in deployed systems, the ways those systems then participate in the meaning-making practices of the communities that use them — these are anthropologically and sociologically significant in a way that warrants ethnographic engagement rather than commentary at a distance. The framework's claim to standing in social-science discussions follows from operating within these traditions rather than commenting on AI from outside them.

**On LaCroix's foreseeability principle, extended.** The methodological positioning above carries its own discipline. Building alternative architectures and observing what they produce is a form of inquiry; the inquiry is responsible for what its outputs imply about the systems it builds and the communities those systems engage. The HAH accepts LaCroix's foreseeability principle as applying not just to vocabulary choices but to architectural choices: the framework is responsible for the foreseeable consequences of building and deploying heteropoietic individuated agents, not only for the foreseeable uptake of its terminology. The discipline of experimental ethnography of AI is what earns the framework's right to make architectural claims with social consequences — the same discipline that earns experimental archaeologists the right to make claims about ancient societies through the artifacts they construct, and earns experimental anthropologists the right to make claims about communities through the lives they share. The empirical program in §3 is what discharges this responsibility, not the methodological statement here. The methodological statement names the tradition the framework operates in; the empirical work demonstrates that the framework lives up to that tradition.

## 1.6\. Heteropoietic individuation: the framework's claim defined by contrast

Maturana and Varela developed autopoietic theory by observing biological cells. They identified the autopoietic property through specific biological mechanisms — physical cell membrane bounding the cell, metabolic processes producing the cell's molecular components, the cell wall both produced by and producing the cell. The strict reading of autopoiesis is that a system is autopoietic if and only if it self-produces its components including its boundary, the way biological cells do.

The framework's agents do not do this. The kernel produces the boundary between agents (UNIX user identity, kernel-enforced isolation, MMU-protected address spaces). The OS produces the agent's runtime environment. The harness instantiates the agent. The agent does not produce its own boundary; it inherits one from the substrate that hosts it. By the strict reading of autopoiesis, the framework's agents are not autopoietic — they are *heteropoietic* (Greek *hetero-*, "other"; the boundary and substrate are other-produced).

The framework's positive claim is empirical and architectural. Heteropoietic systems with persistent substrate-bound state, kernel-enforced cellular individuation, and harness-coordinated internal processes produce *accumulated identity differentiation* — operational specialization that develops over time through the agent's own internal processes within the heteropoietic envelope. The agent's boundary is other-produced; the agent's internal individuation is self-produced through accumulated experience. This is what the framework calls *heteropoietic individuation*: rich identity-content produced by the agent's own internal processes within an externally-maintained boundary.

This is empirically distinct from two adjacent categories. Heteropoietic individuation is distinct from autopoiesis in the strict sense, where the system self-produces its boundary and components — the framework explicitly does not claim this. Heteropoietic individuation is also distinct from stateless or fungible deployment, where heteropoietic envelopes (cloud instances, ephemeral containers, request-bound contexts) host computational processes that do not accumulate individuated state across time. The framework's distinctive claim is that heteropoietic envelopes can host accumulated identity differentiation given specific architectural commitments — substrate-bound persistent state, kernel-enforced isolation, harness coordination, autonomic emission below the deliberative layer.

The empirical test is whether the framework's agents exhibit operational properties (twin-system divergence, sustained identity through accumulated experience, constitutive alignment, productive friction) that distinguish them from stateless or fungible deployments serving the same workloads. The propositions and predictions in §3 are the falsifiable form of this claim. If heteropoietic agents within the framework's architectural commitments exhibit these properties and stateless deployments do not, the hypothesis is supported. If they do not, the hypothesis is falsified.

The framework's relationship to the autopoietic tradition is not extension. It is contrast. Autopoietic theory provides the conceptual reference point that lets *heteropoietic individuation* mean something precise — self-produced boundary defines one category; other-produced boundary with internally-produced individuation defines a different category. The framework's intellectual debt to Maturana, Varela, McMullin, Jaeger, Bianchini, and the broader autopoietic AI tradition is what makes the heteropoietic claim possible to state cleanly. The framework's substantive contribution is to name and operationalize a distinguishable class of artificial agency that the autopoietic tradition's binary (autopoietic vs. allopoietic) did not sharpen.

### The dual definition: agent as internal individuation plus external network effect

The framework's definition of *agent* operates from two complementary perspectives, both load-bearing and both empirically testable. Together they make the interdisciplinary structure of the framework foundational rather than decorative.

**The internal perspective: heteropoietic individuation.** An entity exhibits the substrate-architectural property defined above — kernel-produced boundary plus internally-produced identity differentiation through accumulated experience. This is the engineering side of what makes an entity an agent in this framework's sense. It is measurable through twin-system divergence along constitutive alignment dimensions and through self-maintenance (sustained or strengthening individuation under operation without external intervention). The internal definition is what §1.6 develops above.

**The external perspective: sustained operational effect in the network.** An entity produces sustained operational effect across the network's temporal evolution through actions that persist via accumulated state. This is the social-theoretic side, developed by Actor-Network Theory and extended in §6.5. ANT distinguishes between actors (entities that participate at the point of contact without sustained effect beyond that contact) and agents (whose action persists through accumulated state, producing sustained operational effect across the network's temporal evolution). What makes an entity an agent is not just its internal architecture but its observable functioning as a sustained participant in the network it inhabits.

**Both are required.** An entity exhibiting internal heteropoietic individuation but no sustained network effect would be a strange computational isolate — internally individuated but operationally inconsequential. An entity producing sustained network effects but lacking internal heteropoietic individuation would be a sophisticated tool that mimics agency without architectural support for it (the cortex-without-hippocampus pattern Jaeger diagnoses; see §1.8 failure mode 5). The framework claims that agents in the full sense exhibit both properties, that both definitions track the same underlying entity from different perspectives, and that both are empirically testable through their respective measurement tracks.

The internal definition is tested through the twin experiment (§1.7) and the self-maintenance prediction (§3) — measurements at the substrate level of architectural divergence and identity continuity. The external definition is tested through observable network effects — sustained engagement quality over operational time, accumulated relational patterns with users and other agents, productive friction in collaborative work, durable operational consequences across operational time. The §3 experimental program develops both measurement tracks; the framework's central claim is that the architectural commitments in §2 produce entities measurable as agents on both tracks.

This dual definition is what makes the framework's interdisciplinary structure foundational. ANT is not a vocabulary the framework borrows for color; it provides half the definition of what an agent is. Substrate architecture is not a vocabulary the framework borrows; it provides the other half. Neither half is decorative. The framework's distinctive contribution is the claim that these two definitions, separately developed in their respective traditions, track the same underlying property — and that the property can be empirically demonstrated through coordinated measurement across both perspectives.

## 1.7\. The empirical core: the twin experiment

The framework's central empirical claim takes concrete form in a single experimental setup. Two systems are established with identical initial conditions: same model checkpoint, same harness configuration, same starting state, same hardware class. The systems are then given different operational histories — different user interactions, different accumulated experience, different memory writes and reconciliation outcomes — over a defined operational period. The framework predicts that the two systems will diverge measurably along the six constitutive alignment dimensions (policy decisions, memory conflict resolution patterns, axiom weighting, refusal/permission boundaries, provenance scoring, task-specific response shaping) and that the divergence will be sustained — not stochastic variation around a central tendency but structural differentiation that persists and strengthens under continued operation.

This is the operational signature of heteropoietic individuation. Two agents with identical starting points should become different agents through their own accumulated experience within the heteropoietic envelope. The kernel-produced boundary plus the internally-produced identity differentiation produce twins that diverge structurally; they remain themselves through their accumulated history rather than being interchangeable computational processes that happen to share a configuration.

The contrast with current AI infrastructure is the central diagnostic finding. In current architectures, twin systems do not produce sustained divergence. They produce stochastic variation around a central tendency determined by the model and the shared retrieval store. Whatever apparent agent-specific behavior exists is mostly framing variation around shared cognition; the accumulated state isn't structural enough to produce divergence that persists under load. This is not a failure of model quality or harness sophistication; it is a structural consequence of the architectural commitments SaaS deployment requires (statelessness, fungibility, externalized state, shared retrieval). The twin experiment fails to produce sustained divergence in current infrastructure because the architecture precludes it.

If the framework's architecture produces measurable, sustained twin divergence and current architectures do not, the heteropoietic individuation claim is empirically supported. If the framework's twins converge under sustained operation, or current twins diverge to comparable degree, the heteropoietic individuation claim is falsified. The full experimental design — measurement protocols, specific dimension definitions, control conditions, what constitutes meaningful divergence, statistical significance criteria — is developed in §3 and operationalized through the WeaverTools experimental apparatus.

The framework's architectural commitments (§2), the four propositions and predictions (§3), the level-of-organization arguments (§1.9), and the failure modes synthesis (§1.8) all serve this central empirical question: what architectural commitments produce twins that diverge structurally rather than converge stochastically? The twin experiment is the empirical core; everything else is what's required to enable it.

## 1.8\. Failure modes of current AI infrastructure and the framework's architectural responses

The framework identifies five operational failure modes that current AI architectures exhibit when pressed to serve workloads requiring sustained agency. Each failure mode is observable in production deployments. Each has identifiable structural causes that produce it regardless of model sophistication or harness refinement. Each maps to specific architectural commitments in the framework's response. Read against the twin experiment in §1.7, each failure mode is what makes the twin experiment fail to produce divergence; each architectural response is what enables divergence on a specific dimension.

### Failure mode 1: Session drift and hallucinated continuity

**Operational manifestation.** Agents reconstruct context that didn't exist (claiming to remember conversations or commitments that never happened) or drift from established context across turns (forgetting axioms established earlier in the session, contradicting positions taken earlier). Observable in any long-running deployment of current chat-mediated agent systems.

**Structural cause.** No persistent substrate-bound memory. The agent's "memory" is the context window passed back into the model each turn, plus retrieval over a shared store that doesn't differentiate one agent from another. There is no accumulated state that the model can reference as constitutively the agent's own. Each turn the agent reconstructs identity from context fragments and stochastic completion.

**Framework's architectural response.** Persistent substrate-bound state per agent (an ArangoDB instance bound to the agent's UNIX user identity), populated through harness-coordinated consolidation of decoder traces, with provenance tracking and axiom anchoring. The agent's identity is durable across sessions because the substrate is durable; the agent's history is its own because the substrate is bound to its identity.

**Twin experiment connection.** Without persistent substrate-bound memory, twin agents both reconstruct context from scratch each interaction, producing no accumulated divergence. With it, twin agents accumulate different memory contents that drive different surfacing patterns and different reconciliation outcomes.

### Failure mode 2: Memory redundancy and retrieval degradation under sustained operation

**Operational manifestation.** Archival memory accumulates redundant entries; retrieval becomes inefficient under long-running agent loads; agents surface inconsistent versions of information about the same topic. Letta Issue #3116 documents this concretely in production agents.

**Structural cause.** Storage-as-memory diagnostic mistake. Treating retrieval-augmented context as if it were memory produces specific failures: no consolidation step (entries accumulate without being integrated), no axiom anchoring (foundational positions don't get reinforced), no provenance-weighted ranking (newer or more frequently accessed entries don't preferentially surface), no constitutive accumulation (the agent doesn't become more itself through experience). The pattern of bolting retrieval onto a stateless model inherits the convenience of retrieval without inheriting the operational properties of memory.

**Framework's architectural response.** Harness-coordinated consolidation through autonomic emission — substrate writes triggered by harness-side pattern detection in decoder text without tool invocation, producing memory structures with provenance metadata, axiom anchoring through reconciliation operations, provenance-weighted ranking on retrieval. The substrate accumulates as the agent operates; consolidation prevents redundancy; ranking surfaces what the agent's history makes salient.

**Twin experiment connection.** Without consolidation, twin agents accumulate similar redundancy patterns determined by retrieval mechanics rather than by experience. With consolidation, twins develop different axiom anchoring and different provenance weighting that drive different surfacing patterns from the same retrieval base.

### Failure mode 3: Concurrent operation failures on shared substrate

**Operational manifestation.** Multiple agents operating on shared infrastructure produce race conditions, conflicting memory writes, contaminated retrieval, and operational interference. Letta's reported pivot to git-backed worktrees per subagent is published evidence of this pressure — after multiple iterations on multi-agent sleep architectures, the engineering pressure forced a structural separation that the original architecture didn't provide.

**Structural cause.** Configuration-level isolation (per-agent identifiers, application-layer scoping) is insufficient when multiple agents share the same kernel-level resources. The substrate doesn't know which agent any given operation belongs to; the application layer has to track and enforce isolation through conventions that can be violated under load.

**Framework's architectural response.** Kernel-enforced cellular individuation. Each agent runs as its own UNIX user with kernel-protected isolation, its own ArangoDB instance bound to that identity, its own Unix domain socket authenticated by SO_PEERCRED. Two agents on the same system cannot interfere with each other's substrate because the kernel categorically prevents it.

**Twin experiment connection.** Without kernel-enforced individuation, twin agents on shared substrate contaminate each other's accumulated state, producing convergent failure modes rather than independent divergence. With it, twins are categorically separate even when running on the same hardware.

### Failure mode 4: Identity attack surfaces produced by architectural weakness

**Operational manifestation.** Prompt injection compromising agent behavior. Memory poisoning where adversarial content contaminates retrieval. Cross-agent contamination where one agent's memory affects another agent's behavior. These are not training-data weaknesses or model capability gaps — they are structural consequences of architectures that don't enforce agent identity at substrate level.

**Structural cause.** Fungibility. Architectures that treat agents as interchangeable computational processes can't enforce agent-specific provenance or agent-specific access control at the substrate level. The application layer can attempt to do this but has no kernel mechanism to fall back on; any application-layer enforcement can be circumvented through architectural attack patterns the application layer wasn't designed to anticipate.

**Framework's architectural response.** SO_PEERCRED authentication plus kernel-enforced isolation produces identity verification at the substrate level. The kernel knows which agent any given operation belongs to and enforces access control accordingly. Adversarial content from one agent's interactions can't poison another agent's substrate because the substrate is categorically separated. The agent's identity is enforced architecturally rather than maintained by application-layer convention.

**Twin experiment connection.** Without substrate-level identity enforcement, twin experiments are vulnerable to contamination that produces spurious convergence (both twins affected by the same adversarial input) or spurious divergence (one twin compromised, the other not). With it, the divergence measured is genuinely from accumulated experience rather than from contamination.

### Failure mode 5: Inability to accumulate constitutively the agent's own experience

**Operational manifestation.** The cortex-without-hippocampus pattern Jaeger diagnoses. Sophisticated models with no architectural mechanism for accumulating experience that becomes constitutively the agent's own. Every session starts fresh; every agent is a remarkable vocabulary attached to a goldfish. The agent's apparent identity is the model's central tendency dressed in different prompts.

**Structural cause.** All of the above structural causes combine here. Statelessness precludes accumulation. Storage-as-memory precludes consolidation. Configuration-level isolation precludes per-agent identity at substrate level. Latency-regime confusion makes the autonomic coordination that would enable consolidation economically infeasible at cloud scale.

**Framework's architectural response.** All of the framework's architectural commitments combine here. Cellular individuation enables identity at substrate level. Persistent substrate-bound state enables accumulation. Harness-coordinated consolidation enables the accumulation to become constitutive (axiom-anchored, provenance-weighted, reconciliation-integrated) rather than just stored. Autonomic emission at local-hardware latencies enables the coordination economically. Together these produce an agent that has a history that is its own — history enforced by the substrate as belonging to this specific agent rather than as portable state attached to a fungible identifier.

**Twin experiment connection.** This is what the twin experiment tests directly. Two agents with identical starting conditions, given different histories, should become different agents because their accumulated experience is constitutively their own. If they do, heteropoietic individuation is empirically supported. If they don't, the framework's central claim is falsified.

### The pattern across failure modes

The five failure modes are not independent. They share a common structural origin: the SaaS architectural commitments (statelessness, fungibility, externalized state, configuration-level isolation, cloud-scale latencies) that current AI infrastructure inherits from cloud computing patterns developed for transactional workloads. These commitments preclude persistent agent operation as a matter of architectural design rather than implementation contingency. Agents deployed on this infrastructure are structurally constrained to quasi-statelessness regardless of how sophisticated the underlying models become.

The framework's architectural response is not five separate engineering fixes but a coherent architectural pattern — local hardware deployment, kernel-enforced cellular individuation, persistent substrate-bound state, harness-coordinated autonomic coordination — that addresses the failure modes through their shared structural cause. The five failure modes are five operational symptoms of the same underlying architectural mismatch. The framework's central architectural commitment (individuation) is the underlying response that produces specific operational improvements across the five symptom dimensions.

This pattern is the framework's engineering contribution. The level-of-organization arguments in §1.9 develop the philosophical reasoning for why this architectural pattern is the right level of intervention. The propositions in §2 specify the architectural commitments and their entailments. The predictions in §3 operationalize the empirical content. The twin experiment in §1.7 is the central empirical test. The failure modes in this section are what the framework's architecture is engineered to prevent and the twin experiment is engineered to demonstrate.

## 1.9\. Three level-of-organization arguments

The HAH makes a single architectural claim that resolves into three connected level-of-organization arguments. The arguments are not independent observations sharing a structural pattern; they are a causal dependency stack. **Argument 1** defines the target property — heteropoietic individuation at the agent level — that the framework claims to produce. **Argument 2** defines the required medium — OS-level substrate with harness-distributed coordination — that is structurally necessary to support the target property. **Argument 3** defines the resulting behavior — substrate-level cognitive diversity supporting productive friction — that emerges when the medium supports the target property. Each argument depends on the prior: Argument 2 makes sense only because Argument 1 has named what needs supporting; Argument 3 makes sense only because Argument 2 has established what support produces. Read as a single argument in three causal steps, the level-of-organization analysis says: this property requires this medium and produces this behavior. Each step in the chain carries its own empirical test.

### Argument 1 (target property): Heteropoietic individuation lives at the agent level, not the substrate level

The autopoietic AI research tradition has substantial intellectual history (computational autopoiesis since Varela, Maturana, and Uribe 1974; McMullin's thirty-year review in 2004; the broader artificial life sub-field; Damiano and Stano on wetware embodied AI; Bianchini 2023 on autopoiesis of the artificial). This tradition includes two distinguishable directions. One direction is substrate-level search — explicit wetware proposals (Damiano and Stano 2021), substrate prescription via chemical AI, new substrate-level terminology (Veloz 2025). The other direction explicitly endorses substrate independence and calls for agent-level organizational autopoiesis on standard computational substrates — Varela, Maturana, and Uribe themselves (1974), and most explicitly Bianchini (2023), who notes that hardware/software implementation of organizational autopoiesis on standard computational substrates faces "no hindrance in principle."

The framework's position, developed from §1.6, operates within the substrate-independent territory but does not claim autopoiesis at the agent level — it claims *heteropoietic individuation* at the agent level. The architectural commitment is the same as what the substrate-independent direction calls for (agent-level organization on standard computational substrates); the philosophical claim is more modest. Biological cells achieve autopoiesis at the cellular level: the cell wall bounding the cell, the heterogeneous organelles (nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, cytoskeleton) each performing specialized work, the coordination among these organelles within the bounded enclosure producing the cell's operational unity. The framework's architecture mirrors this structure at a different level: the OS provides the boundary (cell wall analogue), the heterogeneous specialized neural networks (decoder, embedder, cue classifier, GNN, and any task-specific models the workload requires) perform specialized work within that boundary (organelle analogues), and harness coordination produces operational unity. What differs is the philosophical claim: biological cells produce their own boundary; the framework's agents do not. The agents are heteropoietic at the boundary level and individuated through accumulated experience within the heteropoietic envelope.

The framework's specific empirical claim is narrower than the philosophical question whether artificial autopoiesis is achievable in the full biological sense. Given physical boundary maintenance between agents on a system (which OS-level mechanisms provide architecturally), the empirical question is whether current mathematical models, coordinated through harness mechanisms with autonomic responses and accumulated experience, can develop sustained individuated identity through their own operations over operational time. This is essential empirical groundwork (see §6.4): before pursuing the more ambitious moves the autopoietic AI tradition has called for — component self-production at the artificial-substrate equivalent of biological self-production (Bianchini), substrate-level paradigm change toward neuromorphic computing and hardware/software interfusion (Jaeger 2023/2024) — the prior empirical question is whether sustained heteropoietic individuation is achievable with conventional computing substrate given physical boundary. The framework's experimental program tests this directly. If sustained heteropoietic individuation is achievable, the more ambitious moves toward agent-level autopoiesis or substrate-level paradigm change may be unnecessary for the operational properties motivating them. If it is not achievable, the more ambitious moves become empirically motivated as next directions, and the framework's results identify which specific operational properties motivate them.

**Empirical test of Argument 1.** The twin experiment (§1.7) is the test of the property-location claim. If twin agents with same model, same hardware, same harness configuration produce sustained measurable divergence along constitutive alignment dimensions, the target property is observable at the agent level — that is the level where heteropoietic individuation lives. If divergence is observable only at the substrate level (different hardware producing different agents) or only at the model level (different models producing different agents) but not at the agent level given identical lower-layer support, the property does not live at the agent level and Argument 1's locating claim is falsified. The framework predicts divergence at the agent level given identical lower-layer support.

### Argument 2 (required medium): Persistent agent operation requires OS-level substrate, and the harness concept enables agent-level individuation within it

The SaaS architecture pattern that current AI deployments inherit was developed for a specific set of workloads: stateless request-response services, horizontally scalable through interchangeable instances, with state externalized for instance replaceability. The architectural commitments that make SaaS work — statelessness, ephemerality, instance interchangeability, externalized state, request-response semantics — explicitly reject what persistent agents require. A persistent agent needs sustained identity across time, accumulated state bound to that identity, durable memory, predictable resource access, and deterministic communication paths. SaaS infrastructure precludes these requirements as a matter of architectural design rather than implementation contingency. Agent workloads deployed on SaaS infrastructure are structurally constrained to quasi-statelessness regardless of how sophisticated the underlying models become; the architecture pattern itself prevents persistent individuation.

This structural constraint motivates the move from cloud-level deployment to OS-level deployment. The OS level provides the substrate on which persistent individuation can exist — user identities, persistent storage bound to identity, kernel-enforced isolation, durable communication paths, predictable resource access. The architectural primitives that SaaS rejects are the primitives persistent agents require, and these primitives exist at the OS level.

AIOS (Mei et al. 2024, with subsequent versions through 2025) is the progenitor work in this direction. Their stated motivation is resource management efficiency: current agent frameworks let agents flood LLM endpoints with redundant requests, GPU memory exhausts and triggers retry storms, throughput degrades under concurrent agent load. Their design implementations respond to this efficiency problem through specific architectural choices: a memory manager that keeps agent interaction histories in RAM close to the LLM with RAM-to-disk swapping for capacity management; a storage manager for persistent agent state and knowledge bases; a context manager that snapshots LLM inference state to avoid reconstruction across interruptions; thread-bound system calls that carry an agent identifier through the scheduler. These design choices point toward the same structural recognition that motivates the framework's argument here — agent state needs to live close to cognition rather than far from it, persistent agent identity requires architectural support, and the OS level is where this support can be built. AIOS implements significant parts of this recognition through their architecture even though their stated motivation frames the work as efficiency optimization rather than as architectural argument against the SaaS abstraction layer.

The architectural pattern available to AIOS in 2023-2024 was centralized coordination: an AIOS kernel sitting above the OS kernel, providing scheduling, memory management, context management, storage management, and access control as a service that agents call into. Agents in this architecture are clients of the AIOS kernel — their resources are managed by the central service, their LLM access is mediated through the LLM Core abstraction, their memory and storage are handled by the AIOS managers. The architectural pattern necessarily makes agents into objects-managed-by-the-service because centralized coordination requires the coordinating service to know about and control the entities it coordinates.

The framework extends this trajectory by introducing the harness concept. The harness is the agent's internal coordinator — an architectural component within each agent that handles the agent's own resource management, the coordination among the agent's cognitive components (decoder, embedder, cue classifier, GNN, task-specific models), the agent's autonomic responses to operational conditions, and the agent's communication with other agents and with the environment. The harness distributes coordination work into each agent rather than centralizing it in a service above the agents. With the harness concept available, the architectural step from service-managed agents to user-individuated agents becomes possible.

The framework's architecture follows from this addition. The OS provides the substrate for individuation through its existing mechanisms — user identities, kernel-enforced isolation, IPC channels, persistent storage interfaces, mandatory access control. Each agent runs as an OS-level user with its own identity, its own kernel-enforced isolation, its own persistent state bound to its identity, its own cognitive components, and its own harness coordinating these internal resources. Multiple agents coexist on shared OS infrastructure without requiring a centralized coordination service above the OS, because each agent's harness handles its own coordination. The architectural simplification is significant: no centralized service to scale, no architectural singleton bottleneck, no shared cognitive substrate across agents, no service-managed agents.

This is continuation and extension of the AIOS trajectory rather than competition with it. AIOS made the structural move toward OS-level work for agent state and cognition, recognizing through design implementations what the framework now articulates explicitly. The framework adds the harness concept that enables the further architectural step: distributing coordination into agents themselves, producing genuine user-level individuation rather than service-managed agents. Both approaches share the recognition that agent workloads need OS-level substrate rather than cloud abstraction. The framework's distinctive contribution is the architectural pattern that becomes possible with the harness concept, where agents are first-class system users with their own coordinators rather than managed objects within a centralized service.

The cellular analogy is again instructive about where coordination should live, with explicit limits. Tissue is not one cell containing applications and managing them. Tissue is many cells coexisting on shared organism-level infrastructure, with each cell maintaining its own internal coordination among its own organelles. The cell's harness — its internal coordination mechanisms — is what makes the cell a unified operational entity rather than just a collection of organelles inside a membrane. The framework's architecture follows the *coordination distribution* pattern from this analogy: many agents on shared OS infrastructure, each with its own harness coordinating its internal cognitive components, with OS-mediated communication among agents when coordination across agents is needed. But the framework's boundary differs from a biological cell wall in a load-bearing way that the architectural metaphor of a *diving bell* captures more accurately: an externally provided enclosure lowered in by engineers, where the things inside operate within a boundary they did not produce and could not have produced. A cell wall leaks by design (it has to, to permit metabolic exchange); a kernel-enforced boundary does not leak — it provides categorical isolation that the agent inhabits but did not construct. The cellular analogy provides the conceptual pattern of distributed coordination; the diving-bell metaphor reminds the reader that the framework's boundary is heteropoietic in the strict sense developed in §1.6.

**Empirical test of Argument 2.** The medium-requirement claim is tested through comparative operation under sustained load. WeaverTools agents (OS-level users with harness coordination) versus service-managed architectures (configuration-level isolation, centralized coordination — Letta, AIOS, similar) on the same workload class, measuring (a) sustained identity continuity over extended operation, (b) concurrent-agent operation under multi-agent load measuring race conditions, contaminated memory writes, and operational interference, (c) adversarial-input contamination resistance across agent boundaries, (d) memory consolidation efficiency over long-running operation. The framework predicts service-managed architectures hit operational ceilings on these dimensions that OS-level architectures do not, even with comparable model sophistication. The Letta worktree pivot is published empirical evidence in the predicted direction. If service-managed architectures perform comparably to OS-level architectures on these dimensions under extended load, the medium-requirement claim is falsified — OS-level substrate would not be structurally necessary for the target property.

### Argument 3 (emergent behavior): Stochastic difference and differential environmental friction produce sustained cognitive diversity at the substrate-per-agent level

The OS-level boundary in Argument 2 is structurally necessary but does not by itself produce cognitive diversity. The boundary permits diversity by allowing per-agent state to be maintained without contamination; the engine of diversity operates inside that boundary. The framework identifies two coupled mechanisms.

**Stochastic difference (initial).** Models have multiple viable options for any non-trivial input, and decoder sampling produces stochastic variation across them. Harness routing decisions, autonomic emission patterns, and reconciliation verdicts add further stochastic variation. These are structurally inevitable features of any non-deterministic system operating across multiple components. Two agents with identical model, identical harness configuration, and identical starting state will produce different outputs on the same input — not because they are different agents yet, but because the system is stochastic.

**Differential environmental friction (accumulated).** Once a stochastic outcome occurs, the memory system records it with provenance. If the outcome succeeds on the agent's task, downstream surfacing and axiom anchoring reinforce the pattern; if it fails, the pattern is not reinforced and is competed against by alternatives in future operation. Different users interacting with different agents produce different success/failure patterns. The friction is differential because each agent encounters a different operational environment.

The two compound. Stochastic difference initiates divergence; differential friction sustains and amplifies it. Initial experimental findings from the framework's controlled work indicate that **stochastic probability alone produces measurable individuation under controlled conditions** — twin agents with same model, same harness, same benchmark, given the same options and strategies, develop divergent operational preferences as a function of which stochastic outcomes succeeded early and were reinforced by the memory system. Successful strategies accumulate weight; unsuccessful ones do not. The stochastic differentiation produced by initial choices compounds through accumulated memory effects, producing measurable divergence between agents on the same benchmark.

These are initial findings from short-duration experiments and have not yet been demonstrated over months of operation. The framework's working hypothesis is that stochastic difference plus differential environmental friction *together* produce robust sustained individuation. The stochastic component initiates the divergence; differential friction across different operational environments compounds it across longer timescales. Whether stochastic difference alone is sufficient for sustained individuation, or whether differential environmental friction is required to maintain the divergence over extended operation, is itself an empirical question the framework's extended program will adjudicate.

The OS-level boundary (Argument 2) is what allows this divergence to persist as individuation rather than dissipate as contamination. Without kernel-enforced individuation, the stochastic outcomes of one agent leak into another agent's substrate — what one agent's harness sets up as a preference becomes available to all agents sharing the substrate, and the divergence that would otherwise produce per-agent differentiation produces cross-agent contamination instead. The boundary doesn't create the divergence; the stochastic engine does. The boundary lets the divergence accumulate as differentiated identity rather than collapse into shared noise.

**The Letta engineering trajectory as empirical evidence in the predicted direction.** Letta's reported pivot is empirical evidence of exactly this dynamic. After multiple iterations on multi-agent sleep architectures, the engineering pressure of stochastic divergence in their subagents forced a pivot to git-backed worktrees per subagent paired with merge reconciliation for bringing divergent work back together. This is *isolation plus reconciliation* — the same two-part architectural pattern the framework specifies (kernel-enforced individuation per Proposition 1, plus reconciliation operations with typed verdicts per Proposition 4), arrived at independently by Letta at the filesystem level rather than the kernel level. The pattern is what stochastic divergence plus sustained operation forces under engineering pressure: divergence must be allowed to happen safely (isolation), and divergent work must have a structural mechanism for reintegration (reconciliation). Configuration-level fixes did not provide this for Letta despite multiple attempts. The framework predicts that operating at the kernel level rather than the filesystem level provides the same architectural pattern with substantially less operational overhead than worktree-based merge resolution requires — and that the same two-part pattern (isolation + reconciliation) will continue to emerge wherever stochastic divergence under sustained operation is the engineering pressure.

The cellular analogy continues to do conceptual work here, with explicit limits. Different cells in tissue maintain distinct cellular identities, develop different specializations, and accumulate different histories. Tissue function depends on this cellular diversity. But the framework's deployment is more accurately characterized as a *managed cluster* than as tissue, because the agents do not grow the shared infrastructure themselves — engineers provision the OS, the kernel, the storage substrate, and the inter-agent communication channels, and the agents operate within infrastructure that was lowered in from outside rather than secreted into existence by the agents themselves. The cellular language is conceptual scaffolding for thinking about heterogeneous specialized components within an individuated envelope; the engineering reality is closer to a managed cluster of agents on infrastructure that the platform engineer provisioned (see §1.5 substrate-categorical commitment for the discipline that prevents the cellular metaphors from importing biological self-production).

**Empirical test of Argument 3.** The emergent-behavior claim is tested through two coordinated experiments. **Condition A (boundary intact, the §1.7 twin experiment):** WeaverTools agents with kernel-enforced isolation, persistent state, and harness coordination, given divergent operational histories, are predicted to diverge along constitutive alignment dimensions and maintain individuated identity under sustained operation. **Condition B (boundary removed, the degraded substrate experiment):** the same twins are run on a shared-substrate architecture (simulated Letta-style or comparable) with the same divergent histories and the same stochastic engine running. The framework predicts that shared substrate cannot contain the divergence: the §1.8 failure modes (memory contamination across agents, race conditions on concurrent writes, adversarial-input poisoning of memory shared across agents, accumulated redundancy from unreconciled stochastic outcomes) will manifest as predicted. If shared substrate handles the divergence pressure without contamination or failure — that is, if good prompt engineering and configuration-level isolation prove sufficient to contain stochastic divergence under sustained operation — the boundary-requirement claim and therefore Argument 2 are falsified. The framework's empirical commitment is to run both conditions and report what is observed. Beyond these two conditions, the cognitive-substrate diversity question (whether different decoder models produce substantively different reasoning patterns that configuration-level variation cannot replicate) is tested through controlled productive-friction tasks where outcome quality depends on substantive disagreement (research synthesis, engineering design tradeoff analysis, compliance audit).

### Synthesis: One argument in three causal steps

The three arguments are one architectural claim resolved across three dependency levels. Argument 1 establishes the target — heteropoietic individuation at the agent level — and specifies what would falsify the claim about where that property lives. Argument 2 establishes what is structurally required to support that target — OS-level substrate with harness-distributed coordination — and specifies what would falsify the claim about the required medium. Argument 3 establishes what emerges when the medium supports the target — substrate-level cognitive diversity producing productive friction — and specifies what would falsify the claim about the resulting behavior.

The dependency stack tightens the framework's claim substantially. Each level depends on the prior: there is no architectural commitment to medium (Argument 2) without a target property to support (Argument 1); there is no claim about emergent behavior (Argument 3) without the medium to produce it (Argument 2). The synthesis isn't a list of three independent observations sharing a structural pattern; it's a single causal chain stating that a specific target property requires a specific medium and produces a specific behavior.

Read in the abstraction-layer terms of §1.1: Argument 1 is the Level C target the framework claims; Argument 2 is the Level A architectural commitment that supports it; Argument 3 is the Level C behavior that emerges from the supported target. The framework's distinctive contribution is consistent operation across all three stack levels coherently — naming the Level C property the framework claims, specifying the Level A commitments required to produce it, and predicting the Level C behavior that results. Each step in the dependency chain carries its own empirical test, and the experimental program in §3 operationalizes all three tests as a coordinated measurement program.

## 2\. The hypothesis

The HAH rests on a single architectural commitment and four falsifiable propositions that follow from it.

### The architectural commitment: Individuation

**Individuation** is the bounding of an agent as a distinct entity whose internal components communicate over privileged channels not available to the external world, and whose state persists across operational episodes as the state of *this* agent rather than as fungible substrate.

In WeaverTools, individuation is technically achieved through OS-level user identity, kernel-enforced socket isolation (SO\_PEERCRED), and per-agent persistent memory bound to that identity. The hypothesis does not claim these specific mechanisms are the only way to achieve individuation. Any architectural choice that produces equivalent operational closure satisfies the commitment. The commitment is to the property of individuation itself.

Individuation is initially **imposed**. The operator provisions the agent's identity, channels, and memory substrate. Over operational time the hypothesis predicts individuation becomes **self-maintained**: the agent's own operations reinforce its boundaries through accumulated memory, axiom anchoring, and surfacing patterns. The transition from imposed to self-maintained individuation is the operational signature that distinguishes heteropoietic individuation from mere isolation. It is the empirical test of whether the framework's claim survives sustained operation.

The four propositions below describe what individuation produces. Each is independently falsifiable. The experimental program (§7) tests each link and the full chain.

### Proposition 1: Privileged communication

The process of individuation creates exclusive communication pathways among internal components. These lanes provide guarantees for access control, integrity, and latency through the underlying substrate instead of relying on application logic, effectively insulating them from the external environment.

**Evidence for:** demonstrable communication between an agent's internal components at latency and reliability characteristics measurably better than external communication of equivalent content, with access control enforced by the substrate rather than by the components themselves.

**Evidence against:** systems claiming individuation but exhibiting communication characteristics between internal components that are not materially different from external communication. If internal/external communication is indistinguishable in latency, reliability, or access control, individuation has not produced privileged lanes and Proposition 1 fails.

### Proposition 2: Autonomic response

Privileged communication enables autonomic responses; operational-support work that happens beneath the model's deliberation. The harness can detect cues, integrate context, and mediate action without requiring the decoder to invoke those operations as tool calls.

**Evidence for:** measurable harness-side operations occurring without explicit tool invocation by the decoder, on time-scales that would be impossible if those behaviors required deliberative invocation. Detection-by-the-harness of signal forms (without the decoder issuing memory-write calls), context surfacing arriving in the decoder's input before the decoder generates a retrieval query, kernel-mediated provenance enforcement on writes.

**Evidence against:** systems with claimed privileged communication that still require deliberative tool invocation for all operational support work. If the decoder must call out to fetch every piece of context it uses, autonomic response has not been achieved and Proposition 2 fails. The privileged lanes exist but are not being exploited for autonomic operation.

### Proposition 3: Off-loaded data logistics

Autonomic responses enable off-loaded data logistics so that the decoder-local-memory stalls become statistically negligible under defined load. Components retrieve data and place it in the decoder's input queue faster than the decoder could itself generate a retrieval call. The system's overall speed is bounded by decoder speed alone; all local I/O is hidden behind decoder time. The decoder waits only for genuinely external I/O (remote APIs, human input).

This is the quantitative core of slow-is-fast. Slow-is-fast does not mean running the model slowly. It means *the decoder's inference is continuous because the substrate delivers what it needs before it asks*. Continuous inference is the operational signature of autonomic data logistics.

**Evidence for:** per-decision-point retrieval round-trip latencies that fit within the decoder's per-token latency budget on reference hardware. Decoder traces that show no stalls on local memory operations. Measurable performance benefit (in continuous-inference tasks) for individuated architectures over architectures requiring deliberative retrieval.

**Evidence against:** local memory retrieval latencies that exceed the decoder's per-token budget under realistic load. If the substrate cannot deliver memory within decoder-time even with privileged communication, the off-loading claim fails then the architecture has not achieved continuous inference.

### Proposition 4: Constitutive alignment through accumulated experience

Individuation paired with persistent memory enables the agent to accumulate experience from previous encounters with the world. The accumulated experience is genuinely *this agent's* experience, not a corpus belonging to no one. Alignment then becomes **constitutive**, built from the agent's actual operational history, rather than **imposed**, trained over external corpora and applied to a fungible substrate.

The fungibility critique connects here. Fungible agents can carry serialized state that travels across interchangeable compute substrates, but they do not accumulate experience as substrate-enforced, non-fungible operational history unless an additional identity-binding architecture is imposed. Serialized state is portable data about the agent; operational history bound to substrate identity is the agent's own accumulated experience in the architectural sense Proposition 4 requires. The architectural difference between heteropoietic individuated and autocatalytic systems reduces to whether there exists an *agent that has a history that is its own* — history that is enforced by the substrate as belonging to this specific agent rather than as portable state attached to a fungible identifier. Individuation \+ persistent memory produces such an agent. Fungible substrate with serialized state alone does not, though the gap can be approached by adding architectural identity-binding mechanisms.

Workload performance is the empirical consequence: workloads that benefit from accumulated experience and architecturally-enforced alignment (sustained professional engagement, identity-continuous personal assistance, strategic coordination, research support) are exactly the workloads heteropoietic individuated implementations should outperform on. Workloads that are well-served by interchangeable substrate (single-turn analysis, batch processing, transactional generation) are not the architectural distinguisher.

**Architectural pairing with Proposition 1.** Propositions 1 and 4 are two halves of a single architectural answer rather than two independent commitments. Proposition 1 (individuation, kernel-enforced cellular isolation) allows stochastic divergence between agents to accumulate as differentiated identity rather than collapse as cross-agent contamination. Proposition 4 (constitutive alignment through accumulated experience, with reconciliation operations producing typed verdicts over the trace/reflection/substrate bundle) provides the structural mechanism for divergent operational outcomes to be brought back into the agent's substrate as accumulated experience that is its own. Isolation without reconciliation leaves agents stuck in their immediate operational contexts with no structural mechanism for integrating divergent outcomes into accumulated state. Reconciliation without isolation cannot maintain per-agent accumulated state because the divergent outcomes contaminate across agents before reconciliation can occur. Both are required for sustained heteropoietic individuation under operation. The Letta engineering trajectory provides revealed-preference evidence for this pairing — their reported pivot to git-backed worktrees per subagent (isolation at the filesystem level) paired with merge reconciliation for bringing divergent work back together (reconciliation at the filesystem level) is the same two-part pattern arrived at independently at a different level of the stack, after multiple iterations on architectures that lacked one or both halves.

**Evidence for:** measurable operational divergence over operational time between agents started from identical configurations and given different operational histories. The divergence appears not only in task accuracy but in alignment-relevant dimensions such as axiom-status patterns, memory conflict resolution, surfacing distributions, response shaping. Twin-system experiments produce these measurements.

**Evidence against:** autocatalytic systems with sufficient harness refinement matching individuated systems on workloads claimed to require constitutive alignment. If fungible agents with serialized state or, more strongly, fungible agents with no persistent state, can sustain agency, maintain compliance, and accumulate operational value through pure architectural-or-training refinement within the autocatalytic paradigm, then the constitutive-alignment claim fails. The architectural difference does not produce the operational difference the hypothesis predicts.

## 3\. Predictions

The hypothesis makes specific predictions. Each maps to an experiment in §7.

**Self-maintenance prediction.** An individuated agent operating without operator boundary intervention over an extended operational period will exhibit *stable or strengthening* individuation. The agent should demonstrate boundary maintenance through its own memory writes, axiom anchoring through reconciliation, identity-consistent surfacing patterns. This is the empirical test of the heteropoietic individuation claim itself. If individuation decays under sustained operation, the framework's central claim has been falsified.

**Privileged-communication latency prediction.** On reference hardware, the latency stack for an agent's internal communication path can be characterized in five distinct layers, each with distinct expected magnitude. The layers are kept separate to prevent collapsing operationally different lookup paths into a single number:

1. **Kernel-mediated channel and authentication latency** — single-digit microseconds for Unix socket transfer with SO_PEERCRED credential checks. This is the Level A foundation and is feasibility-checkable through standard tooling.
2. **In-process cache lookup latency** — sub-microsecond to low-single-digit microseconds for substrate queries that hit warm caches resident in the same process address space (no IPC required, no syscall required for the data path itself).
3. **Local IPC lookup latency** — single-digit to low-double-digit microseconds for substrate queries serviced over local IPC channels with the substrate process running on the same machine (Unix socket roundtrip plus minimal processing time on the substrate side).
4. **Database hot-cache lookup latency** — tens to low hundreds of microseconds for queries against the substrate database (ArangoDB or equivalent) hitting in-memory hot cache. This is distinct from in-process cache (layer 2) because the database query path crosses process boundaries and involves query parsing, plan selection, and result serialization even when data is in memory.
5. **Graph and vector retrieval with full context surfacing** — workload-dependent, typically completing within the decoder's per-token budget on reference hardware for realistic operational patterns; specific magnitude depends on retrieval depth, GNN propagation, ranking, provenance checks, and context assembly requirements.

Equivalent content moving across external network-mediated paths exhibits measurably higher latency at every layer, with the gap most pronounced at layers 1–3 where the Level A foundation is the dominant constraint. The latency differential across layers is the operational signature of privileged communication. The framework's claim is not that all internal operations complete in single-digit microseconds but that each layer operates at hardware-appropriate speeds rather than at network-mediated speeds, and that the cumulative path stays within the decoder's per-token budget for realistic operational patterns. Layers 2 and 3 (in-process cache, local IPC) are the layers where the framework's specific advantage over network-mediated alternatives is most pronounced; layer 4 (database hot-cache) and layer 5 (full retrieval) have similar magnitudes whether the database is local or remote, though local deployment removes network variance and removes external dependencies.

**Autonomic-response prediction.** A measurable share of operational-support work, signal detection, context surfacing, and provenance enforcement, will occur without decoder tool invocation. Trace analysis will show harness-side spans whose effects appear in subsequent decoder context without an intervening decoder-issued call.

**Pipelining prediction (the strongest engineering claim).** For local-substrate-mediated inference, per-decision-point memory retrieval will complete within the decoder's per-token latency budget on reference hardware. Decoder traces will show no stalls on local memory operations under realistic load.

**Identity-continuity / divergence prediction.** Two agents started from identical base configurations and given different operational histories will diverge measurably in operational characteristics traceable to their accumulated experience. The divergence will manifest in alignment-relevant dimensions, not only in task accuracy. Constitutive alignment is the operational concept being tested, and its measurement requires specific metrics rather than generic behavioral comparison. Expected dimensions of divergence include: **policy divergence** (differential decisions on similar prompts where the agents' accumulated histories produce different priorities), **memory conflict resolution patterns** (how each agent handles incoming material that conflicts with its existing substrate, measured through resolution trace analysis), **axiom weighting** (changes in the relative weight each agent gives to its foundational commitments as accumulated experience reinforces or strains them), **refusal and permission boundary shifts** (operational boundaries that move based on what each agent has been through), **provenance scoring of new bindings** (how each agent assesses the trustworthiness and relevance of incoming material against its accumulated source-credibility patterns), and **task-specific response shaping** (how each agent's accumulated experience shapes its approach to similar tasks differently). Twin-system experiments will produce these measurements across the specified dimensions, isolating the architectural contribution from confounders through matched-pair contrasts. Initial controlled findings indicate that stochastic difference alone (same model, same harness, same benchmark, different sampling outcomes reinforced by memory) produces measurable divergence; these are short-duration findings and the framework's working hypothesis is that stochastic difference plus differential environmental friction together produce sustained individuation over extended operation.

**Degraded-substrate prediction (boundary-failure control).** The identity-continuity prediction tests Argument 1 (heteropoietic individuation at the agent level) and Argument 3 (cognitive diversity emerging from stochastic difference plus accumulated friction). The boundary-requirement claim in Argument 2 requires its own controlled test: the same twins run on a shared-substrate architecture rather than on the framework's kernel-isolated architecture, with the same divergent histories and the same stochastic engine operating. The framework predicts that shared substrate cannot contain the divergence — the §1.8 failure modes will manifest as predicted: memory contamination across agents through unmediated writes to shared structures, race conditions on concurrent memory operations, adversarial-input poisoning that crosses agent boundaries through the shared substrate, accumulated redundancy from stochastic outcomes that lack a reconciliation mechanism. The boundary-failure condition can be implemented either through a simulated shared-substrate harness (running both twins against a single substrate process with configuration-level rather than kernel-level isolation) or through direct comparison with an existing shared-substrate deployment (Letta or comparable). If shared substrate handles the divergence pressure without contamination or failure — if good prompt engineering and configuration-level isolation prove sufficient to contain stochastic divergence under sustained operation — Argument 2's boundary-requirement claim is falsified. The Letta engineering trajectory provides revealed-preference evidence in the predicted direction: their pivot to git-backed worktrees per subagent paired with merge reconciliation, after multiple iterations on multi-agent sleep architectures, indicates that shared-substrate architectures hit operational ceilings that forced a structural pivot to isolation plus reconciliation. The framework's empirical commitment is to run the boundary-failure control alongside the twin experiment and report what is observed in both conditions.

**Workload-performance prediction.** On workloads requiring sustained agency, identity continuity, or architecturally-enforced compliance, individuated implementations will outperform autocatalytic implementations of equal model size. The performance margin will be most pronounced on tasks that benefit from accumulated state. HeroBench and equivalent benchmarks test these predictions.

**Failure-mode prediction.** Autocatalytic systems attempting to serve workloads that require constitutive alignment will fail in specific, predictable ways — session drift, hallucinated continuity with previous interactions, structural inability to maintain identity over operational time, vulnerability to attack surfaces produced by architectural rather than training-based weaknesses. These failure modes will be observable in existing deployments and will persist regardless of harness refinement *within* the autocatalytic paradigm. Specific failure instances of these predicted classes are observable in Letta's documented production experience, particularly around archival memory redundancy under long-running agent loads (Issue #3116 documents redundant archival passages and retrieval inefficiency in long-running agents), and in the operational pressures that motivated the sleep-time architecture's evolution toward a "reflection subagent" pattern. The broader Letta engineering trajectory — including their reported pivot toward git-backed worktrees per subagent to handle concurrent memory operations after multiple iterations on multi-agent sleep architectures — reflects pressure from these failure classes. Letta's responses to these pressures have been harness-side compensation (tool-rule DSL constraining call order, sleep-time twin agents handling writes outside the primary response path, per-subagent isolation via git worktrees). The HAH predicts that the underlying pattern these failure classes share — agent-deliberation-mediated memory operation with shared substrate under non-determinism — is structurally avoided by autonomic emission from decoder text without tool invocation, by cellular individuation per HAH Proposition 1, and by reconciliation operations with explicit verdicts rather than unilateral memory edits.

## 4\. Methodology

The hypothesis is tested through **WeaverTools**, the experimental apparatus that implements an instance of the architectural commitment. Two distinct questions structure the methodology: **feasibility** — whether the architectural commitments can be built — and **validation** — whether the assembled apparatus produces the operational properties the propositions predict. Feasibility is a precondition for validation but is not itself validation. The hypothesis remains open as a scientific question even when the apparatus is fully operational, because what is being asked is not whether the architecture can be built but whether what gets built produces what the propositions predict. The empirical program described below tests validation.

**Apparatus design.** WeaverTools implements individuation through OS-level user identity (`weaver-<name>`), kernel-enforced Unix socket isolation with SO\_PEERCRED authentication, per-agent persistent state substrate (an ArangoDB instance bound to the agent's identity), and a co-located SPU pairing decoder and embedder on the same hardware. The harness operates as the autonomic coordination layer mediating between operational components. Some apparatus elements — for example, per-turn associative surfacing and inductive-GNN substrate participation — are implementation hypotheses internal to the apparatus rather than load-bearing commitments of the HAH itself; their specific design choices can change without affecting the hypothesis as stated.

**Reference hardware.** Primary development hardware is a Threadripper 7960X workstation with 256 GB ECC RAM, dual A6000 GPUs, and substantial NVMe storage. This represents the substantial-but-not-frontier hardware class the boundary condition in §5 identifies. The boundary-condition prediction is tested across additional hardware classes — DGX Spark, single-H100, small organizational racks.

**Empirical program.** Each prediction in §3 maps to one or more experiments listed in §7. Twin-system divergence measurements use the Tower-of-Hanoi and HeroBench protocols. Latency benchmarks measure substrate, communication, and pipelining characteristics directly. Workload-performance comparisons run individuated and autocatalytic implementations of equal model size against the same task suites. A matched-pair attribution instrument isolates the architectural contribution from confounders. The validation criterion that ultimately tests the hypothesis is whether the assembled apparatus serves real workloads in ways the architectural commitments predict and that alternative architectures cannot match.

**Publication arc.** Empirical results are published as they emerge, not held until completion. Architectural papers describe the framework. Benchmark articles publish measurement results. Demonstration articles describe specific deployment examples. Results that contradict the hypothesis are published with the same attention as results that support it. Implementation choices that turn out to be unnecessary or wrong are revised in apparatus revisions; the hypothesis itself is revised only when evidence requires it.

**A note on scope.** This document states the hypothesis, the architectural commitment from which it follows, and the experimental program by which it will be tested or falsified. It is not a status document. The current state of the empirical program — which experiments have been run, what they showed, and what remains to do — is maintained as time-stamped status documents on the project blog, separate from this canonical statement. This document is updated only when the hypothesis itself is revised.

## 5\. Boundary conditions

The HAH applies specifically to workload classes requiring sustained agency, identity continuity, and architecturally-enforced operational constraints. The hypothesis does not claim that all AI work should be done through individuated architecture, and explicitly acknowledges that other architectures serve other workloads adequately.

**Workloads outside the hypothesis scope.** Customer service chatbots, code completion, content generation, translation, search and summarization, single-turn analysis, and most batch processing workloads are adequately served by autocatalytic systems. The autocatalytic paradigm matches these workloads' actual requirements, and the HAH does not argue against current practice for them.

**Composition with autocatalytic systems.** Individuated agents can coordinate autocatalytic worker systems for parallel task execution. The hypothesis supports composition patterns where identity-bearing agents organize swarms of autocatalytic workers. This is closer to how complex biological systems work than to either-or replacement of one architecture by another.

**Hardware boundary (the deployment claim).** The architectural commitment is producible on substantial-but-not-frontier hardware accessible to thousands of organizations: dual-A6000 or single-H100 class systems, small organizational racks, unified-memory systems in the DGX Spark class. Below this boundary, the commitments cannot be honored adequately — the latency budget for Proposition 3 collapses on insufficient hardware. Above the boundary, the framework operates the same way as on reference hardware. The specific threshold for different workload classes is empirical and the testing program will produce measurements.

**Scale boundary.** The hypothesis addresses workloads at organizational and personal scale. Consumer-scale workloads serving millions of users simultaneously are not what HAH is for; frontier-AI infrastructure handles them adequately through the autocatalytic paradigm. The framework's contribution is for thousands of organizations and millions of individuals, not for consumer-scale platforms.

## 6\. Related work

The HAH develops in intellectual conversation with several research traditions. This section organizes the related work into four clusters that clarify the framework's distinctive position: the autopoietic AI tradition organized into its substrate-independent and substrate-level directions (§6.1); progenitor work that recognized the OS-level direction for agent infrastructure and that the framework extends (§6.2 the AIOS line of work); convergent prior art that operates at substantively the same architectural level as the framework (§6.3); critical interlocutors whose paradigm-level critique and theoretical call the framework substantially accepts while providing essential empirical groundwork prior to the more ambitious moves they propose (§6.4 Jaeger 2023/2024 and Bianchini 2023); and adjacent intellectual traditions whose vocabulary and conceptual tools inform the framework without being directly critiqued or directly cited as architectural precedent (§6.5).

### 6.1 The autopoietic AI tradition

The autopoietic AI tradition includes two distinguishable research directions, as the framework's Argument 1 (§1.9) notes: a substrate-independent direction calling for organizational autopoiesis on standard computational substrates, and a substrate-level direction pursuing autopoiesis through specific substrate prescriptions (wetware, chemical AI, new terminology). The framework operates in the substrate-independent territory (standard computational substrates, agent-level architectural organization) but makes a different and more modest philosophical claim than what substrate-independent autopoietic theorists have called for — the framework claims heteropoietic individuation rather than agent-level autopoiesis (see §1.6). The substrate-level direction represents research distinct from the HAH. This section organizes both directions chronologically and bibliographically.

**Substrate-independent direction.**

**Varela, Maturana, and Uribe (1974)**, "Autopoiesis: the organization of living systems, its characterization and a model" (Biosystems 5, 187–196), established both the theoretical foundation of autopoietic organization and the original computational autopoiesis research program. Their 1974 work used artificial computational media to explore autopoietic organization more than a decade before the first Santa Fe Workshop on Artificial Life. The framework's intellectual debt to this foundational work is the substrate-permissive stance — the original theorists treated computational substrate as legitimate research medium for organizational autopoiesis. The framework operates in this same substrate territory but makes a different (more modest) claim within it: heteropoietic individuation rather than computational autopoiesis (see §1.6).

**McMullin (2004)**, "30 Years of Computational Autopoiesis: A Review" (Artificial Life 10(3), 277–295), provides the most authoritative documentation of the substrate-independent direction's history. The review covers the computational autopoiesis research program from VMU 1974 forward, with parallel attention to the wet chemistry direction that emerged in the late 1980s. McMullin explicitly defends computational autopoiesis as a legitimate research program against wetware-focused positions, concluding there is "no obstacle of principle in realising autopoietic organisation in an artificial, computational, medium." The framework's intellectual lineage extends back substantially further than the Maturana-Varela 1980 citation typically suggests; McMullin documents the substrate-independent direction's continuous development since 1974, placing the HAH within a research conversation that has been ongoing for over half a century.

McMullin's review also identifies a specific open problem of direct relevance to the framework's central architectural commitment. McMullin developed a heuristic test for autopoietic individuation: whether two putatively individual cells, in direct contact, can reliably maintain their separate identities. Applying this test to the original VMU 1974 model, McMullin reached what he called a "somewhat controversial conclusion" — that the original minimal model was not properly autopoietic, because the chain-based bond inhibition mechanism that enabled single-cell self-repair also caused adjacent cells to merge in proximity. McMullin identified individuation as a load-bearing failure mode of the original computational autopoiesis research program. The HAH's OS-level architecture addresses this directly: two agents on the same system have categorically separate identities enforced by the kernel and cannot merge under any internal dynamics (see §2). The framework's central architectural commitment can be read as operationalizing a solution to the individuation problem McMullin identified, with kernel-enforced isolation doing the work that VMU 1974's chain-based bond inhibition could not.

**Substrate-level direction.**

**Damiano and Stano (2021)**, "A Wetware Embodied AI? Towards an Autopoietic Organizational Approach Grounded in Synthetic Biology" (Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 9, 724023), proposes a wetware direction for autopoietic AI research grounded in synthetic biology — pursuing artificial autopoiesis through actual biochemical substrate rather than through computational substrate. This represents a substantively different research program from the HAH. The framework operates entirely on the artificial side of the biological/artificial substrate distinction (see §1.5 substrate-categorical commitment) and does not engage substantively with the wetware direction. Whether the wetware direction is a productive research path is an open question the framework does not address; the framework leaves that question to researchers pursuing it directly.

**Veloz (2025)**, "Toward aitiopoietic cognition: bridging the evolutionary divide between biological and machine-learned causal systems" (Frontiers in Cognition 4, 1618381), extends the substrate-level direction more strongly than Damiano and Stano. Veloz argues explicitly that *purely computational approaches* cannot achieve constitutional causality, and that future aitiopoietic systems will require *physical substrates where information processing and self-maintaining affordances are materially unified*, with hybrid bio-synthetic architectures as the proposed direction. This commitment is more demanding than the framework operates under, and is also more demanding than the framework's claim makes necessary. The framework does not claim aitiopoiesis or autopoiesis; it claims heteropoietic individuation (see §1.6). Whether the operational properties Veloz's call points toward can be achieved through heteropoietic individuation on conventional substrate is exactly what the framework's experimental program tests. Whether Veloz's stronger property requires what Veloz claims it requires is a question the framework's program does not adjudicate either way.

Within this two-direction structure, the framework operates within the substrate-independent territory (standard computational substrates, agent-level architectural organization) but makes a different philosophical claim than substrate-independent autopoietic theorists have called for. The framework claims heteropoietic individuation rather than agent-level autopoiesis (see §1.6). The substrate-level direction represents research distinct from the HAH; the framework does not engage substantively with those directions, leaving that engagement to researchers pursuing them directly. The framework's most substantive engagements with the autopoietic AI tradition are with Bianchini (2023) and Jaeger (2023/2024) in §6.4, where the contrast between heteropoietic individuation and full agent-level autopoiesis is articulated.

### 6.2 The AIOS line of work: progenitor research the framework extends

A cluster of recent work has applied operating system principles to LLM agent management. **Mei, Li, et al. (2024)**, "AIOS: LLM Agent Operating System" (arXiv:2403.16971, with subsequent versions through 2025), provides the canonical statement and is the progenitor work the framework extends. AIOS proposes a three-layer architecture (application layer for agent applications, kernel layer integrating the traditional OS kernel with an AIOS kernel as userspace coordination layer, hardware layer) in which the AIOS kernel manages LLM-related activities for agents through scheduling, context management, memory management, storage management, tool management, and access control. As Mei et al. note, the AIOS kernel does not directly interact with hardware but relies on OS system calls for hardware access. The framework notes for the reader that the AIOS "kernel" is therefore a userspace coordination layer rather than a Linux kernel module — a clarification AIOS itself does not address explicitly, but which matters for understanding the architectural relationship between AIOS and the framework's approach.

The framework's relationship to AIOS is continuation and extension. The §1.9 Argument 2 develops this relationship in detail: AIOS's stated motivation is resource management efficiency, and their design implementations (memory manager keeping agent state in RAM close to LLM, storage manager for persistent agent state, context manager snapshotting LLM inference state, thread-bound system calls carrying an agent identifier) point toward the same structural recognition the framework articulates explicitly — that agent state needs to live close to cognition rather than far from it, and that the OS level is where this can be built. AIOS implements significant parts of this recognition through architectural choices even when their stated motivation frames the work as efficiency optimization.

The architectural pattern available to AIOS in 2023-2024 was centralized coordination: a coordination service above the OS kernel managing agents as clients. The framework extends this trajectory by introducing the harness concept — an internal coordinator within each agent that handles the agent's own resource management, cognitive component coordination, and autonomic response. With the harness concept available, coordination work distributes into agents themselves rather than centralizing in a service above them, enabling the architectural step from service-managed agents to user-individuated agents.

**AIOS-Agent ecosystem follow-on work** extends the AIOS framing with additional infrastructure: the Cerebrum SDK (the AIOS-Agent SDK referenced in Mei et al. 2024 Appendix B), and the AgentSites / Internet of AgentSites architecture (Zhang & Zhang 2025, "Planet as a Brain," arXiv:2504.14411), which extends AIOS Server into a decentralized runtime for peer-to-peer agent hosting and discovery. This follow-on work maintains the centralized coordination pattern at each node (each AIOS Server instance is itself a coordination service for the agents it hosts) while distributing coordination across nodes at internet scale.

**MemOS (MemTensor)** applies similar logic specifically to memory operations, treating memory management for LLM agents as an OS-level concern with "memory cubes" as composable knowledge bases, hybrid retrieval, and cross-task skill reuse. The memory-OS framing inherits the AIOS architectural pattern: agent memory management as a userspace coordination service.

**Bara (2026)**, "Who Is Building the Agent-Native Operating System?" (Medium analysis), surveys the landscape of agent-native operating system attempts and identifies AIOS as the most rigorous academic statement of the OS-level direction for agent infrastructure. The analysis documents the field's convergence on OS-level treatment of agent workloads.

What these works share with the framework is the recognition that agent workloads need OS-level substrate rather than cloud abstraction. The framework's distinctive contribution is the harness concept that enables the further architectural step: distributing coordination into agents themselves, producing genuine user-level individuation rather than service-managed agents. The framework's Argument 3 (§1.9) develops the related claim that cellular individuation produces substrate-level cognitive diversity that supports productive friction, which architectures routing agent cognition through shared coordination layers cannot match through configuration alone.

### 6.3 Convergent prior art operating at the same architectural level

A separate cluster of recent work operates at substantively the same architectural level as the framework, sharing the architectural commitment to OS-level isolation for agents and per-agent persistent state. The relationship between the HAH and these works is convergent engineering: independent research arriving at architecturally similar commitments from different starting points.

**Packer et al. (2023)** and the evolution into **Letta** represent the closest modern lineage for the framework's stateful-agent direction. MemGPT (arXiv:2310.08560, "Towards LLMs as Operating Systems") introduced virtual context management — an OS-inspired paging technique that extends effective context through hierarchical memory tiers and function-mediated self-editing, with the explicit commitment that "memory edits and retrieval are entirely self-directed" (Section 2.3). The evolution into Letta extended this foundation into multi-agent production deployment with persistent memory, sleep-time reconciliation, and configuration-level agent isolation. The relationship between the HAH and Letta is substantive engagement, not competition. The architectural comparison points below are primarily between the HAH and Letta's current production architecture; MemGPT's 2023 paper provides the virtual context management foundation that Letta builds on. The two programs make different load-bearing architectural commitments:

- *Memory operation pattern.* Letta inherits MemGPT's foundational commitment to deliberative memory operations (memory edits and retrieval "entirely self-directed" by the LLM, per MemGPT Section 2.3); the agent invokes tools (`memory_rethink`, `memory_insert`, `memory_replace`, `archival_memory_insert`) to manage its own state. The HAH uses autonomic emission below the deliberative layer, with substrate writes triggered by harness-side pattern detection in decoder text without tool invocation.
- *Individuation mechanism.* Letta uses configuration-level isolation (per-agent identifiers, application-layer scoping). The HAH uses OS-enforced cellular individuation (per-agent UNIX user, SO_PEERCRED authentication, ArangoDB instance bound to OS identity).
- *Memory substrate.* Letta uses a four-surface taxonomy (in-context blocks, message buffer, recall, archival) distinguished by access mechanism. The HAH uses uniform substrate with provenance metadata, where retrieval is uniform and ranking is provenance-weighted.
- *Reconciliation semantics.* Letta's sleep-time writes are unilateral memory edits. The HAH's reconciliation operations produce typed verdicts (`Agreed | SupersedeWithTrace | SupersedeWithDb | FlagUnknown`) over a three-party reconciliation bundle (trace, reflection, substrate state), with audit trail and explicit unresolved-state structures.
- *Concurrency.* Letta's reported pivot to git-backed worktrees per subagent represents convergent evidence from production engineering pressure for what the HAH's cellular individuation enforces by design. Their pivot, after multiple iterations on multi-agent sleep architectures, is published empirical support for the concurrency problem HAH Proposition 1 names. The HAH predicts that OS-level cellular individuation provides the structural answer to this problem with substantially less operational overhead than worktree-based merge resolution requires.

**Quine** (arXiv:2603.18030), "Realizing LLM Agents as Native POSIX Processes," shares the framework's commitment to OS-level isolation enforced through kernel mechanisms. Quine maps agent boundary to address space with memory isolation between agents enforced by the MMU at hardware level rather than by application-code conventions. The architectural commitment is shared; the framework extends Quine's approach by adding heterogeneous specialized neural networks within each agent (rather than single-LLM-per-process), autonomic harness-mediated coordination, and persistent substrate-bound state beyond process memory.

**AgentSys** (arXiv:2602.07398), "Secure and Dynamic LLM Agents Through Explicit Hierarchical Memory Management," applies process memory isolation principles to agent memory management. AgentSys organizes agents hierarchically with worker agents executing in isolated contexts, only schema-validated return values crossing isolation boundaries. The empirical demonstration that context isolation achieves 2.19% attack success rate without additional mechanisms supports the framework's Argument 2 indirectly: OS-style isolation produces measurable security benefits that application-layer alternatives cannot match.

**Concurrent Modular Agent** (Maruyama et al. 2025, arXiv:2508.19042), "A Concurrent Modular Agent: Framework for Autonomous LLM Agents," shares the framework's commitment to multiple specialized modules within a single agent coordinated through internal communication. CMA's modules are LLM-based (all of the same type), whereas the framework's specialized components are heterogeneous neural networks (decoder, embedder, cue classifier, GNN) of different architectural types. CMA cites Brooks' subsumption architecture (1986) as historical precedent for hierarchical reactive modules. The framework extends this lineage by combining the multiple-specialized-modules-within-one-agent architecture with cellular individuation through OS-level identity.

**Brooks (1986)**, "A Robust Layered Control System for a Mobile Robot," and the subsumption architecture tradition more broadly, provide the historical precedent for multiple specialized modules coordinated within a single agent. Brooks's work demonstrated that intelligent behavior could emerge from layered reactive modules without a central controller. The framework's architecture extends this lineage by replacing hand-coded reactive modules with specialized neural networks and by adding cellular individuation through OS-level identity that Brooks's robotics work did not require.

**Memory benchmarks.** MemoryAgentBench (arXiv:2507.05257) and Letta's Context-Bench provide benchmark suites designed to evaluate agent memory capabilities. The framework's validation program will use memory-specific benchmarks to test workload-performance and identity-continuity predictions. The forward program (§7) adds a multi-session consolidation benchmark targeting the specific gap that existing memory benchmarks do not appear to isolate.

**Continuum Memory Architectures** (arXiv:2601.09913) independently identify that stateless RAG memory fails to provide the operational properties serious agent workloads require, paralleling the framework's storage-as-memory diagnostic. The diagnostic foundation is shared across multiple research programs working in this territory.

**Episodic memory in LLMs.** Park et al. (2023) *Generative Agents* (arXiv:2304.03442) demonstrates that persistent memory structures meaningfully change agent behavior on long-running tasks. The HAH extends this by claiming that episodic memory in an individuated substrate produces *constitutive* alignment in a way that episodic memory grafted onto fungible substrate cannot.

### 6.4 Critical interlocutors: Jaeger 2023/2024 and Bianchini 2023

The framework's most substantive engagements with the autopoietic AI tradition are with Jaeger (2023/2024) and Bianchini (2023). Both interlocutors articulate positions the framework substantially accepts. Jaeger's paradigm-level critique of current AI architectures identifies the organizational complexity and hierarchical circularity missing from flat LLM-based systems. Bianchini's theoretical call articulates what an autopoietic AI research program should produce: substrate-independent agent-level organizational autopoiesis. The framework's relationship to both, developed in the subsections below and summarized at the section's close, is one of essential empirical groundwork rather than competing philosophical position.

**Jaeger 2023/2024: The paradigm-level critique.**

Jaeger (2023/2024) is often read as the canonical philosophical opposition to claims of algorithmic agency. The HAH reads him differently — as the most precise diagnostician working in this territory, whose critique the framework substantially accepts. Read carefully, Jaeger's claims are paradigm-level rather than categorical: he hedges repeatedly to "the current algorithmic framework of AI research," to "contemporary approaches," to LLMs and recurrent neural nets specifically. He acknowledges that "we do not know at this point what such an architecture would look like" and that "direct embodiment necessitates a completely different computational paradigm," pointing toward neuromorphic computing and configurable materials as the direction such a shift would take. Jaeger may well be correct. It may very well be that true natural agency — autopoietic agency in the full biological sense he argues for — requires the substrate-level paradigm change he envisions, and the framework does not dispute this. Jaeger and the HAH see the same problem: current AI architectures are too flat, rich in computational complexity but lacking the organizational complexity and hierarchical circularity required for autopoiesis. They take different principled approaches in response. Jaeger places his bet on substrate-level paradigm change. The framework places its bet on agent-level architectural organization within conventional computing substrate — that the conceptual approach can be tested without specialized hardware or specialized software, using primitives that already exist, and that the architecture can get reasonably far before substrate-level innovation becomes the binding constraint. Both bets remain to be adjudicated empirically.

What Jaeger diagnoses correctly is that current systems — LLMs and the harnesses built around them — cannot achieve autopoietic agency. The HAH identifies why: the field has built only the cortex. The model, however sophisticated, is the inference organ. It is capable at single-shot pattern completion and structurally incapable of accumulating experience past the context window. Every session starts fresh; every conversation is reconstructed from a context dump; every "agent" is a remarkable vocabulary attached to a goldfish. A cortex without a hippocampus is precisely the failure mode Jaeger names — session drift, hallucinated continuity, structural inability to maintain identity over operational time. And the gap exists because the field has been building under an economic constraint Jaeger does not name but the framework does: the SaaS deployment model demands fungibility, which demands portability, which demands statelessness, which forces accumulated memory out of the architecture and into externalized retrieval-as-augmentation. The current paradigm fails because its economic model forces an architectural choice that produces the failure modes Jaeger correctly identifies.

Jaeger inherits one assumption from the field he critiques, and this is where the HAH departs from him: the assumption that the model is the agent. Throughout Jaeger's analysis, "algorithm," "LLM," and "AI system" are used interchangeably, because in the world he is critiquing they refer to the same entity — a model wrapped in scaffolding, where the model is the cognizer and the scaffolding is external infrastructure the model calls into. The framework rejects this collapse. The model is to the agent what a neuron, or a cortex, is to a brain: a specialized component within a heterogeneous composite, not the composite itself. When Jaeger establishes that algorithms cannot be autopoietic, the framework agrees — and the agreement extends: the framework does not claim autopoiesis at the composite agent level either. The framework claims something different: *heteropoietic individuation* at the composite level (see §1.6). The composite agent has a kernel-produced boundary (heteropoietic) and accumulated identity differentiation through its own internal processes (individuation). Jaeger's argument that algorithms cannot be autopoietic is granted; the framework's empirical question is whether heteropoietic individuation at the composite level produces the operational properties Jaeger identifies as missing (sustained identity, accumulated experience, structural agency) without requiring the substrate-level paradigm change Jaeger calls for.

What makes this practically interesting is how little engineering is required to take Jaeger's critique seriously. The field has treated agent individuation as an open research problem — something to be solved through novel architecture, neuromorphic substrates, or biological wetware. None of that is necessary to begin testing the conceptual approach. Unix has provided identity individuation since 1971: a user has a name, a uid, a home directory, owned files, processes that inherit credentials, kernel-enforced isolation from other users, and persistent state across sessions. Run an agent as a system user — its own uid, its own home directory, its own ArangoDB instance bound to that identity, its own Unix domain socket authenticated by SO_PEERCRED — and the operating system supplies individuation without any custom engineering. The hippocampus Jaeger's diagnosis identifies as missing becomes a persistent storage substrate bound to that user. The cellular boundary becomes kernel-enforced. Accumulated experience becomes the user's own filesystem and database, owned by no other agent and not portable to any other substrate. None of these are novel mechanisms; they are operating-system primitives older than most of the field that has overlooked them. The reason the field has not built it this way is not engineering difficulty. It is that the SaaS economic model rules out individuation as a matter of design — fungible infrastructure cannot host non-fungible agents. Remove that economic constraint and the architecture is largely already built.

**Bianchini 2023: The theoretical call.**

Bianchini (2023), "Autopoiesis of the artificial: from systems to cognition" (BioSystems 230, 104936), surveys current AI approaches against autopoietic criteria and concludes that cognitive architectures (SOAR, ACT-R) are allopoietic — built by programmers as unified units with explicit reference to cognitive parts — and that neural networks self-organize only within pre-existing systems built with specific features. His positive call is for substrate-independent agent-level organizational autopoiesis: by utilizing autopoiesis theory's specific notion of organization (a network of invariant processes that self-produce along with their components, independent of the substrate), it is possible to create self-emergent systems serving as promising candidates for AI cognitive systems. Bianchini explicitly endorses multiple realizability and notes that hardware/software implementation of organizational autopoiesis on standard computational substrates faces "no hindrance in principle." The framework reads Bianchini as the most explicit contemporary articulation of what an autopoietic AI research program should produce, and positions itself as operationalizing this call.

The framework's specific architectural commitments operationalize the substrate-independent territory Bianchini's call points toward, but with a more modest philosophical claim. The physical boundary between agents on a system is maintained by OS-level mechanisms (kernel-enforced isolation, per-agent identity, per-agent persistent state). Within that heteropoietic boundary, internal processes do the individuation work: harness coordination among heterogeneous specialized components (decoder, embedder, cue classifier, GNN, task-specific models), autonomic responses producing operational support without decoder invocation, accumulated experience consolidated into memory structures, the resulting differentiation along constitutive alignment dimensions. The framework's empirical question, in relation to Bianchini's call: assuming physical boundary maintenance between agents on a system, do the software's internal processes produce sustained identity differentiation through their own operation? The architectural commitments are the framework's hypothesis; the experimental program (twin-system divergence, constitutive alignment measurement, sustained operation) tests whether the hypothesis holds.

A genuine disagreement remains, paralleling the Jaeger case. Bianchini's organizational autopoiesis includes self-production of components — biological cells produce their own enzymes through recursive metabolic networks. The framework does not claim component self-production, nor does it claim boundary self-production. The framework explicitly does not claim autopoiesis at the agent level. It claims heteropoietic individuation — kernel-produced boundary plus internally-produced identity differentiation through accumulated experience. Whether heteropoietic individuation satisfies the operational properties Bianchini's call points toward is empirical and contested. The framework's bet is that the operational properties — sustained identity, accumulated experience, productive friction, constitutive alignment — are achievable through heteropoietic individuation without requiring full agent-level autopoiesis. The framework operates as if heteropoietic individuation were the prior empirical question to be settled before Bianchini's more ambitious move (full agent-level organizational autopoiesis with component self-production) becomes the necessary next direction.

**The framework as essential empirical groundwork.**

What the framework offers, in relation to both Jaeger's and Bianchini's broader proposals, is essential empirical groundwork rather than competing philosophical position. Both interlocutors call for ambitious moves — substrate-level paradigm change for Jaeger (neuromorphic computing, hardware/software interfusion, configurable materials, eventually autonomous hardware evolution), component self-production for Bianchini (the artificial-substrate equivalent of cells producing their own enzymes through recursive metabolic networks). These moves require substantial research investment beyond what current mathematical models or conventional computing substrates provide. Both are predicated on a prior assumption that has not been adequately tested: that current mathematical models, given a kernel-maintained physical boundary, cannot achieve sustained identity differentiation through accumulated experience over operational time. The framework's experimental program tests this assumption directly. Give the agent a kernel-maintained physical boundary (heteropoietic envelope, removing the boundary-self-production problem from the scope of what must be demonstrated). Then ask whether the internal processes operating within that boundary — harness coordination, autonomic responses, accumulated experience, memory consolidation — produce sustained heteropoietic individuation over operational time. If sustained heteropoietic individuation produces the operational properties motivating the autopoietic call (sustained identity, accumulated experience, productive friction, constitutive alignment), then the more ambitious moves Jaeger and Bianchini call for may not be necessary for those properties. If heteropoietic individuation does not produce these properties, then their proposals become empirically motivated as next directions to pursue, and the framework's results identify which specific operational properties motivate them. The framework does not compete with either proposal. It provides the prior empirical work that determines whether the more ambitious moves are necessary, and informs the design of those moves if they prove necessary.

### 6.5 Adjacent intellectual traditions

The framework operates in conversation with several broader intellectual traditions whose vocabulary and conceptual tools inform the work without being directly critiqued as level-of-organization mistakes or directly cited as architectural precedent.

**Strategic-polysemy critique.** LaCroix, Mallory, and Luccioni (2026), "Strategic Polysemy in AI Discourse: A Philosophical Analysis of Language, Hype, and Power" (FAccT '26, doi: 10.1145/3805689.3812399; arXiv:2604.21043), identifies the strategic polysemy problem in contemporary AI discourse. The framework operates under a methodological discipline informed by their critique, articulated and engaged with substantively in §1.5: prefer technical terms when they exist, acknowledge metaphors as metaphors when they do organizing work, engage substantively with borrowed philosophical vocabulary, accept the foreseeability principle that disclaimers do not discharge responsibility, and engage with LaCroix's specific critique of "agent" as central architectural concept. The framework's discipline is its own and does not strictly follow all of LaCroix's recommendations (notably, the framework does not adopt McDermott's "colourless language" alternative, instead accepting the higher foreseeability risk that comes with philosophically loaded vocabulary in exchange for the substantive philosophical contribution the loaded vocabulary makes possible).

**Shannon-Weaver communication theory.** Weaver's three-level framework (technical, semantic, effectiveness) from his 1949 introduction to Shannon's mathematical theory provides the diagnostic foundation for §1. The framework's name commits to Weaver's call for tools operating at Levels B and C, not just at Level A.

**Prigogine's dissipative structures.** Prigogine's work on far-from-equilibrium thermodynamics and dissipative structures provides theoretical grounding for understanding how organized structure emerges and maintains itself through constraint relationships. The hypothesis draws on this tradition for the formal treatment of how individuation produces structure rather than merely enclosing it.

**Continual learning and lifelong learning.** The ML tradition of continual learning addresses how neural networks accumulate skills over time without catastrophic forgetting. Most continual-learning work operates within the autocatalytic paradigm: a single model accumulating parameter updates over a task stream. The HAH claims something different — that accumulation should occur in a substrate-side memory bound to individuated identity, with the model remaining a relatively stable "cortex." The relationship to continual-learning research is complementary, not competing; the two traditions could compose.

**Autonomic computing.** Kephart and Chess (2003) proposed the vision of autonomic computing as systems that manage themselves through self-configuration, self-optimization, self-healing, and self-protection. The HAH's autonomic-response proposition (Proposition 2) sits directly downstream of this tradition. The framework's specific architectural commitments — autonomic emission of assertions from decoder text, autonomic surfacing of context based on harness-side classification, autonomic reconciliation during memory reconciliation operations — operationalize Kephart and Chess's vision for the LLM-agent context. The lineage is direct; the HAH extends autonomic computing principles to the individuated-agent architecture rather than originating the autonomic framing.

**Agent benchmarks and agentic frameworks.** ReAct (Yao et al. 2022), AgentBench, and related work measure agent capabilities on diverse task suites within the autocatalytic paradigm. The hypothesis predicts that these benchmarks will reveal specific failure modes (§3) on workloads requiring constitutive alignment that autocatalytic frameworks cannot reach through harness refinement alone.

**Persistent agent frameworks.** Artificial-life-inspired persistent agent research overlaps substantively with the HAH's claims about identity continuity, narrative memory, and persistent self-improvement. The artificial-life framing approaches similar questions through a different intellectual lineage (artificial life and complex adaptive systems rather than autopoietic biology specifically); the framework engages with this work as adjacent research in the persistent-agent tradition.

**Continual harness research.** Research on agent-harness refinement tests different hypotheses within the autocatalytic paradigm — making autocatalytic agents work better through automated harness adaptation. The HAH tests an adjacent proposition: that individuated architecture serves workloads autocatalytic refinement cannot reach. Both programs do real scientific work in adjacent territory.

**Knowledge graph and GraphRAG research.** Microsoft GraphRAG and follow-on work demonstrate that structured retrieval substantially outperforms flat-text retrieval for serious inference tasks. The HAH claims that knowledge graphs maintained dynamically by harness-side autonomic processes function as substrate-side accumulated state in the architectural sense (Proposition 4), not just as better retrieval substrate.

**Actor-Network Theory.** Latour and the ANT tradition provide vocabulary for thinking about how human and non-human actors participate in networks of meaning and effect. The framework's relationship to ANT is a friendly amendment rather than a rejection: ANT's symmetric methodology for tracing associations through networks is preserved, and one specific distinction is added that recovers analytical depth ANT's strict symmetry tends to bracket.

Within the category of actors, some are *agents* and some are not. Agents are actors whose action persists through accumulated state and whose communication is aimed at producing change in the environment over time rather than only at producing immediate response. The distinction is empirically grounded rather than philosophically arbitrary. Agency exhibits a continuum across entities that biological and behavioral science has already mapped: a rock participates causally in networks by occupying space, resisting force, and redirecting interactions, but it does not exhibit accumulated state, purposive communication, or identity-maintaining action over time; a houseplant exhibits substantially more (tracks light tropically, allocates resources between leaves and roots in response to conditions, signals to other plants through volatile chemicals and root systems, maintains identity through metabolism, though with limited communicative purposiveness); a dog exhibits substantially more again (purposeful communication, identity recognition, goals extending across days, accumulated experience producing differentiated behavior); a human exhibits more still. The continuum is real and measurable. The framework's actor/agent distinction picks out a region of this continuum where entities exhibit operational properties (identity maintenance, accumulated experience, purposeful temporal extension of action) that distinguish them analytically from actors whose participation is at point of contact.

This minimal modification preserves what ANT does well. Symmetric network analysis remains useful. Treatment of non-humans as network participants remains useful. The methodology of tracing associations remains useful. What the modification adds is recognition that within the category of actors, the agent subset has analytical properties that ANT's strict symmetry brackets: internal coordination producing operational unity, accumulated experience producing differentiated identity, communication aimed at change over time rather than only at immediate response. These are the properties social science has substantial analytical resources for understanding — the interpretivist tradition, institutional analysis (F.G. Bailey on formal and informal normative rules, Goffman on interaction order, Bourdieu on habitus and field), organizational sociology, ethnographic methods for understanding internal practices within network nodes. The actor/agent distinction makes the scope of these analytical resources explicit: they apply to agents, not to all actors.

The framework's contribution operationalizes the actor/agent distinction with empirical content for artificial systems. The framework's engagement with ANT is not vocabulary borrowing — the ANT actor/agent distinction provides the external definition in the framework's dual definition of agency developed in §1.6. An artificial entity is an agent in the extended ANT sense if it exhibits identity maintenance through accumulated experience (the heteropoietic individuation property developed in §1.6), measurable through six constitutive alignment dimensions: policy divergence, memory conflict resolution patterns, axiom weighting, refusal boundaries, provenance scoring, task-specific response shaping. The framework's empirical question is where on the agency continuum the WeaverTools agents land. The architectural commitments (cellular individuation, persistent state, heterogeneous specialized components, autonomic coordination) are hypothesis about what produces movement along the continuum toward agent-level operation in artificial substrate. The empirical program produces evidence about where the systems actually land.

The framework's right to use *agent* vocabulary rests on this substantive extension to ANT. The framework does not claim the framework's heteropoietic agents are equivalent to human agency, does not claim sentience, does not claim consciousness. The framework claims the heteropoietic agents exhibit operational properties measurable on the established agency continuum, and that the architectural commitments produce systems that land somewhere meaningfully past actors with state attached. Where exactly the systems land on the continuum is empirical, tested through the framework's measurement program.

The framework's standing to extend ANT through architectural practice rests on the methodological positioning developed in §1.5 — experimental ethnography of AI in the combined tradition of experimental archaeology, experimental anthropology, and STS. The ANT extension above is not vocabulary borrowing by a tech project; it is the application of ANT's analytical resources to a sociotechnical phenomenon (artificial agents in deployment) studied through the construction of alternative architectures and the observation of what those architectures produce. The discipline that earns the framework's right to make ANT-extending claims is the same discipline that earns experimental archaeologists the right to make claims about ancient societies through the artifacts they construct.

## 8\. The hypothesis stated formally

**The Heteropoietic Agency Hypothesis:** There exists a class of artificial agency — heteropoietic, individuated, autonomically coordinated, identity-continuous through accumulated experience — that is producible through a single architectural commitment (individuation, however technically achieved), on substantial-but-not-frontier hardware, and that follows four propositions from that commitment: (1) individuation produces privileged communication between enclosed components, (2) privileged communication enables autonomic response below the threshold of model deliberation, (3) autonomic response enables off-loaded data logistics such that decoder stalls on local memory operations become statistically negligible under defined load, and (4) individuation paired with persistent memory produces constitutive alignment through accumulated experience that is genuinely the agent's own. The class is meaningfully distinct from both general intelligence (AGI) and narrow intelligence (ANI) framings in ways that have measurable operational consequences, and serves workload classes that current autocatalytic AI infrastructure serves poorly, unreliably, or only at high cost.

The hypothesis is falsifiable through implementation work. Specific predictions about latency behavior, identity continuity, workload performance, failure modes, and self-maintenance over operational time will be tested empirically. Evidence supporting or contradicting the hypothesis will be published as it emerges. The testing program proceeds through WeaverTools as the experimental apparatus.

The hypothesis is presented as a scientific claim under empirical investigation. It is not presented as settled architecture for adoption. Engineers, researchers, and organizations engaging with the hypothesis are invited to do so as participants in scientific work — testing the propositions, contributing implementation evidence, identifying where the hypothesis holds and where it fails, and informing the architectural and theoretical revisions the testing process will produce.
