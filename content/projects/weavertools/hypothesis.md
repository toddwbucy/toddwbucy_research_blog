**A Falsifiable Proposition About a Distinguishable Class of Artificial Agency**

*Foundational document. Author: Todd W. Bucy. Draft v05.3 — 2026-06-20. Status: active research program. This document is the canonical statement of the Heteropoietic Agency Hypothesis (HAH). The articles in the accompanying publication series develop specific aspects for broader audiences. WeaverTools is the implementation testing the hypothesis empirically.*

---

## Abstract

**The Heteropoietic Agency Hypothesis (HAH) makes four claims:**

1. A distinct class of artificial agency exists that is heteropoietic, individuated, autonomically coordinated, and identity-continuous through accumulated experience.
2. This class is producible through a single architectural commitment: **individuation**, however technically achieved.
3. It serves workload classes that current autocatalytic/stateless infrastructure serves poorly or only at high cost.
4. It is meaningfully different from both general intelligence (AGI) and narrow intelligence (ANI) framings.

The hypothesis is testable through implementation work and falsifiable through specific predictions about how individuated architectures behave under sustained operation. It is developed in intellectual contrast with autopoietic biology (Maturana-Varela), Shannon-Weaver communication theory, and Prigogine's dissipative structures analysis. The autopoietic tradition is engaged as the conceptual reference point that defines heteropoietic by contrast.

A consequence bears directly on governance. Because the boundary is other-produced and identity is substrate-resident in an operator-owned, inspectable substrate, that identity is auditable and editable by construction. This is a surface for inspection and correction that systems carrying identity in opaque model weights do not provide.

The document is organized as a research paper. The **Introduction** states the problem and adopts Warren Weaver's three-level model of communication as the diagnostic instrument used throughout. It locates the shared cause of the failure modes it surveys in a single structural choice, conducting an agent's internal communication over the network that is its environment, of which the stateless SaaS deployment model is the commercial form rather than the root. The **Literature review** positions the framework against adjacent research. The **Theory** section states the hypothesis: what a heteropoietic agent is, whether it establishes individuation over operational time, and to what extent that individuation shapes the agent's operation. The **Conclusion** restates the hypothesis formally. **Appendix A** specifies the experimental program, with each experiment listed individually.

WeaverTools is the experimental apparatus for testing the hypothesis. The distinction between **feasibility** (whether the architectural commitments can be built) and **validation** (whether the assembled apparatus produces what the hypothesis claims) is preserved throughout. The hypothesis is presented as a scientific claim under investigation, not as settled architecture for adoption.

## 1. Introduction: the problem

Current AI infrastructure serves one class of workload extremely well and another class poorly. It excels at stateless, transactional work: single-turn analysis, content generation, code completion, search and summarization. It performs poorly, unreliably, or only at high cost on workloads that require sustained agency: identity continuity over operational time, accumulated experience that becomes constitutively the agent's own, and architecturally-enforced operational constraints. This document is about the second class, why current infrastructure fails it, and what architecture would serve it.

To diagnose the failure precisely rather than describe it loosely, the document adopts a diagnostic instrument and uses it consistently from here forward.

### 1.1 The diagnostic instrument: Weaver's three levels

Warren Weaver's 1949 contribution to Shannon's *Mathematical Theory of Communication* distinguished three levels at which a communication problem can sit.

**Level A, the technical problem.** How accurately can the symbols of communication be transmitted? This is the engineering level of channels, signals, encoding, transmission fidelity, and latency.

**Level B, the semantic problem.** How precisely do the transmitted symbols convey the desired meaning? Meaning is not an emergent property of channel capacity.

**Level C, the effectiveness problem.** How effectively does the received meaning affect conduct in the desired way? Effectiveness is not an emergent property of semantic precision.

The three levels are not a list of parallel problems. They are a structural dependency hierarchy in which upper layers depend on lower layers for support. Level C effectiveness depends on Level B semantic operation, which depends on Level A transmission fidelity. This is the pattern engineers recognize in network stacks (application on transport on network on link on physical) and in operating system layering (userspace on kernel on hardware). When the lower layer cannot provide its required services, the upper layer fails, not because the upper layer is unsophisticated but because it is asked to do work the lower layer should have done. An HTTP request cannot compensate for unreliable TCP delivery: the upper-layer operation can be made more sophisticated, but the sophistication addresses the wrong layer.

The dependency is a hard bound, not loose support, and the diagnostic has to make that explicit rather than leave it to the network-stack intuition. Weaver's own statement of it is exact: you cannot convey more meaning than the channel permits, and you cannot produce more effective action than the conveyed meaning will support. Read upward, each layer's ceiling is set by the layer beneath it. Level A does not merely enable Level B, it bounds it, because the model computes meaning only over the context the channel places in the computation and only as continuously as the channel can keep that context arriving. Level B in turn bounds Level C, because no meaning the model fails to compute can become effective conduct downstream. A single failure can travel the whole stack while changing its appearance at each layer, surfacing as an effectiveness problem at C even though its ceiling was set at A.

Used as a diagnostic, the model assigns every problem in an agent system to a level, and the level tells you where to look.

**A Level A concern is a transport concern.** Is the channel between components fast and reliable enough? Privileged communication, low-latency connectivity, the path that keeps the inference model fed. When the symptom is latency, stalls, or transmission, the problem is at Level A.

**A Level B concern is a semantics concern.** Is the model producing the right meaning? The inference model doing semantic operation, the model's output. When the symptom is incoherent or wrong content, the problem is at Level B.

**A Level C concern is an effectiveness concern.** Does the coordinated result affect conduct as desired? The coordination of internal components (the harness orchestrating memory, the inference model, and the other specialized components), and the agent's interaction with the outside world. When the symptom is coordination failure, identity drift, or ineffective action, the problem is at Level C.

The dependency structure makes the diagnostic sharp rather than merely descriptive. A Level C symptom can have a Level A root cause. When coordination fails, the diagnostic forks: is the coordination logic itself wrong (a genuine Level C problem), is the coordination starved by slow channels (a Level A root cause presenting as a Level C symptom), or is the model feeding the coordinator bad semantics (a Level B problem)? The same fork applies to interaction with the world: the effectiveness of affecting the world is Level C, but the raw latency of an external channel is Level A, and the framework treats that external-channel cost as a Level A constraint it accepts rather than tries to optimize.

This document tags every architectural component, failure mode, proposition, and experiment with its level, so the diagnostic is a working instrument on every page rather than framing stated once and abandoned.

### 1.2 The diagnostic mistake

Seventy-seven years after Weaver, the field of artificial intelligence has accepted in principle that progress requires tools operating at Levels B and C, not just Level A, while making a specific diagnostic mistake in practice.

Read as a history, each level becomes workable when it is given an apparatus it can be met through. Shannon did not solve Level A, he made it tractable, turning reliable transmission over a noisy channel from an art into an engineering discipline with a mathematics behind it. The model line has done the same for Level B, making meaning tractable where decades of brittle symbolic approaches had stalled, and it did so without having to solve understanding. Level C is the level not yet made tractable, and it is the one this document takes up. The recurring error is to reach for the apparatus that made the previous level tractable, more model, and aim it at the level that still lacks its own.

The field has done substantial and genuine Level B work. The models perform real semantic operation, produce meaningful output, and integrate context coherently. Level B is not where the failure is. The pattern is to attempt Level C effectiveness through more Level B sophistication: bigger models, more parameters, larger context windows, more capable harnesses around the same models. The actual constraint making Level C operation difficult is at Level A, an absence of low-latency connectivity to support the autonomic coordination that Level C effectiveness needs.

It is worth walking the mistake carefully, because the claim that more model will not fix this depends entirely on it. Begin where the field's confidence is highest, at Level B. On any single turn the model computes meaning well. The reason that local success does not climb to Level C is that sustained agency does not need merely correct meaning, it needs meaning that is continuous over time and grounded in the agent's own accumulated state. Each turn must reason from what the agent has become, not reconstruct an identity from whatever fragments sit in the window. That is a specific and demanding requirement on Level B: not larger single-turn semantics, but continuous, accumulated, identity-grounded meaning. Levels B and C are tied at exactly this joint. Now follow it down. Whether the agent's accumulated state is present in the computation, and present continuously rather than fetched in halting interruptions, is governed by the channel. The channel decides what context the model computes over and whether it can keep that context arriving without stopping the computation. So the quality of Level B that Level C requires is set at Level A. Adding Level B sophistication raises the ceiling on single-turn meaning and leaves the Level A bound on continuous accumulated meaning exactly where it was. The symptom appears at C, the apparent deficiency looks like B, and the bound that holds is at A. That is the diagnostic claim derived rather than asserted: current architectures attempt Level C effectiveness through Level B sophistication while the constraint sits at Level A, and Level B sophistication cannot reach it because it is the wrong layer.

The continuity half of that bound deserves its own treatment, because it is the half most often mistaken for an efficiency concern. The cost of a deliberative memory operation is not the tokens it consumes or the latency of the round trip. It is that the model must stop the computation in which its reasoning consists. It is tempting to locate the reasoning in the KV cache, because the cache is what persists across a pause. But the KV cache is the result of computation, not the reasoning itself. The reasoning lives in the forward computation that consumes the cache to produce the next step, and the cache is only the residue that computation leaves behind. When the model halts to retrieve, the cache sits intact while no reasoning happens, and that persistence is exactly what creates the false comfort that the reasoning state was preserved. It was not. The reasoning stopped. The halt is intrinsic, not incidental, because the model must reason far enough to know what it needs before it can ask, so no amount of prefetching or cache reuse removes it within the deliberative paradigm. Across sustained operation, repeated forced halts fragment reasoning into intervals entrained to the environment's I/O rate rather than the agent's own integration rate. This is the Level A failure (the substrate cannot feed the computation in time) forcing the Level B reasoning to stop, and the field's response has been more Level B sophistication aimed at a constraint that sits at Level A.

The field's Level B work can be pushed to approximate Level C operation, but doing so becomes increasingly expensive (compute, parameter counts, context windows, harness complexity) and increasingly brittle (session drift, hallucinated continuity with previous interactions, structural inability to maintain identity over operational time).

The HAH claims there is a better way: provide the Level A architectural foundation, local hardware with privileged communication at microsecond and nanosecond latencies, that lets Level B sophistication participate in Level C effectiveness through autonomic coordination, at substantially lower cost and substantially higher reliability than Level B sophistication alone can achieve.

### 1.3 The symptoms: five failure modes of current infrastructure

The diagnostic mistake produces five operational failure modes. Some are already documented in production deployments; others are predicted consequences of the same structural account. Each has a structural cause that produces it regardless of model sophistication or harness refinement. They are presented here as the problem. The architectural responses are developed in the Theory section (§3), and the experiments that test whether the responses work are specified in Appendix A.

**Failure mode 1: Session drift and hallucinated continuity (presents at Level C, roots at Level A).** Agents reconstruct context that never existed (claiming to remember conversations or commitments that did not happen) or drift from established context across turns (forgetting axioms established earlier, contradicting earlier positions). The structural cause is the absence of persistent substrate-bound memory: the agent's "memory" is the context window passed back into the model each turn, plus retrieval over a shared store that does not differentiate one agent from another. There is no accumulated state the model can reference as constitutively the agent's own. Each turn, the agent reconstructs identity from context fragments and stochastic completion. The symptom is a Level C identity failure, but its causes are layered. The missing Level A foundation leaves no accumulated state for the model to reason from, and the stochastic Level B engine fills that absence with plausible confabulation. That is why the continuity is hallucinated rather than merely absent. A correct Level A foundation does not stop the engine from confabulating, which is an inherent Level B limit. It removes the structural gap that forces the engine to confabulate continuity it never had. The architecture contains the flawed engine, it does not cure it.

**Failure mode 2: Memory redundancy and retrieval degradation under sustained operation (presents at Level C).** Archival memory accumulates redundant entries (the same fact stored several ways in agreement rather than consolidated), and an agent can fail an archival search and then surface fabricated records that were never stored. The redundancy is catalogued in Letta Issue #3116 (Soein, December 2025), a feature request — filed by an outside user, not by Letta — that proposes archival-memory deduplication and consolidation and identifies duplicate accumulation as a motivating problem for long-running agents. Retrieval degradation as archives grow is a predicted consequence of the same storage-as-memory pattern, not a production measurement: Letta's own documentation states that archival memory scales to large archives without performance degradation, and the framework's claim is precisely that sustained operation strains that design assumption — something the comparative experiments in Appendix A are meant to test, not a documented symptom asserted here. The fabrication-on-retrieval-failure symptom is the one observed in the wild, documented as an incident in Letta Discussion #502 (ThreepE0, November 2023), where an agent failed an archival search and then fabricated records that were never added, despite the data being verifiably present in the archive. On the framework's own diagnosis the structural cause is the storage-as-memory mistake: treating retrieval-augmented context as if it were memory leaves archival writes with no consolidation step (entries accumulate without integration), no axiom anchoring (foundational positions are not reinforced), no provenance-weighted ranking, and no constitutive accumulation (the agent does not become more itself through experience). That reading is the framework's to defend, not one #3116 underwrites: #3116 in fact credits Letta with consolidation for core memory and asks only that it be extended to archival memory. Bolting retrieval onto a stateless model inherits the convenience of retrieval without the operational properties of memory.

**Failure mode 3: Concurrent operation failures on shared substrate (roots at Level A, presents at Level C).** Multiple agents on shared infrastructure produce race conditions, conflicting memory writes, contaminated retrieval, and operational interference. Letta's reported pivot to git-backed worktrees per subagent is published evidence of this pressure. The structural cause is that configuration-level isolation (per-agent identifiers, application-layer scoping) is insufficient when multiple agents share the same kernel-level resources. The substrate does not know which agent any given operation belongs to. The application layer has to track and enforce isolation through conventions that can be violated under load. This is a Level A substrate failure producing Level C operational interference.

**Failure mode 4: Identity attack surfaces produced by architectural weakness (roots at Level A, presents at Level C).** Prompt injection compromising behavior, memory poisoning where adversarial content contaminates retrieval, cross-agent contamination where one agent's memory affects another's behavior. These are not training-data weaknesses or model capability gaps. They are structural consequences of architectures that do not enforce agent identity at the substrate level. The cause is fungibility: architectures that treat agents as interchangeable computational processes cannot enforce agent-specific provenance or access control at the substrate level, and any application-layer enforcement can be circumvented through attack patterns the application layer was not designed to anticipate.

**Failure mode 5: Inability to accumulate the agent's own experience constitutively (presents at Level C, the synthesis failure).** Sophisticated models with no architectural mechanism for accumulating experience that becomes constitutively the agent's own. Every session starts fresh. The agent's apparent identity is the model's central tendency dressed in different prompts. This is the cortex-without-hippocampus pattern (developed in the Literature review, §2.4). Its structural cause is all of the above combined: statelessness precludes accumulation, storage-as-memory precludes consolidation, configuration-level isolation precludes per-agent identity at the substrate level, and latency-regime confusion makes the autonomic coordination that would enable consolidation economically infeasible at cloud scale.

### 1.4 The shared cause

The five failure modes are not independent. They share a structural origin more specific than the deployment model usually blamed for it. The agent's environment is a network, the medium over which it reaches everything outside its own boundary, and current infrastructure conducts the agent's internal communication over that same medium: its components reach one another, and reach the agent's own state, across the network. An entity whose own parts must be reached over its environment is not bounded off from that environment, it is distributed through it. The SaaS model is the commercial form this takes, not the cause beneath it. Commoditizing compute over a network requires fungible units, fungibility forbids any unit from owning irreplaceable state, and externalized state is then reached over the network, so the network ends up between the agent's components. The familiar SaaS commitments (statelessness, fungibility, externalized state, configuration-level isolation, cloud-scale latencies) are links in that chain rather than five separate choices, and they preclude persistent agent operation as a matter of architectural design rather than implementation contingency. Agents deployed this way are structurally constrained to quasi-statelessness regardless of how sophisticated the underlying models become.

Conducting internal communication over the network has two consequences, and between them they produce the five failure modes. The first is timing: when internal integration crosses the network, it proceeds at the environment's I/O rate rather than running ahead of it, and the agent's reasoning fragments at each round trip (§1.2). The second is enforcement. When the shared medium is the network and the application layer above it, isolation between agents is asserted in software (identifiers, scoping, credentials layered over a shared medium) rather than enforced by the substrate. The substrate does not know which agent an operation belongs to, so the boundary can be violated under load (failure modes 3 and 4). This second move is multi-tenant isolation on shared cloud infrastructure carried down onto a single host. The two consequences are one cause seen twice: the agent's internal communication is conducted in its environment rather than inside a boundary the substrate enforces.

In the diagnostic's terms, conducting internal communication over the network leaves the Level A foundation at cloud-native standards, which makes Level C operation expensive and brittle to achieve, and the field responds by adding Level B sophistication, which is the wrong layer. The framework's response is not five separate engineering fixes but a single architectural commitment, individuation, that addresses the failure modes through their shared cause: a boundary the substrate enforces, with the agent's internal communication kept inside it and the network left where it belongs, outside it.

### 1.5 What this document claims

Against this problem, the document claims the four propositions stated in the abstract: that a distinct class of artificial agency exists (heteropoietic, individuated, autonomically coordinated, identity-continuous through accumulated experience). That it is producible through the single architectural commitment of individuation. That it serves workloads current infrastructure serves poorly, and that it is meaningfully distinct from AGI and ANI framings.

The hypothesis is falsifiable on its central claim. If an individuated agent decays under sustained operation without operator intervention, if its boundaries weaken, its identity drifts, or its accumulated state fragments, the central claim is falsified. If it strengthens, the claim is supported. The Theory section states the claim and its predictions. Appendix A specifies the experiments that test it.

## 2. Literature review

The HAH develops in conversation with several research traditions. This section positions the framework against them: the autopoietic AI tradition (§2.1), the operating-system direction for agent infrastructure that the framework extends (§2.2), convergent prior art operating at the same architectural level (§2.3), the critical interlocutors whose paradigm-level critique the framework substantially accepts (§2.4), the adjacent traditions whose vocabulary and tools inform the work (§2.5), and the discipline the framework applies to its own vocabulary and method (§2.6). The gap the framework occupies is named at each point: a substrate-independent, agent-level, OS-enforced individuation that the existing literature points toward from several directions without occupying.

### 2.1 Autocatalysis, autopoiesis, and the autopoietic AI research tradition

Before positioning the framework against the AI research programs, two concepts the rest of this document relies on need to be established directly, because the theory in §3 defines heteropoietic individuation by contrast with them and should not have to re-derive them there.

**Autocatalysis.** An autocatalytic reaction is one whose product is also its catalyst, so the reaction produces more of the molecule that accelerates it. Kauffman's collectively autocatalytic sets and the Reflexively Autocatalytic and Food-generated (RAF) theory of Hordijk and Steel generalize this to networks: a set of reactions in which every member is catalyzed by some member of the set, and the network as a whole sustains itself from a basic food source. Such a network is actively self-reinforcing, but it is boundaryless. It is a pattern in an environment, not an individuated entity. Its persistence depends on external food supply and the absence of external disruption, and its constraint-maintenance is imposed from without. An autocatalytic set has no self that it maintains as distinct from its surroundings.

**Autopoiesis.** Maturana and Varela identified a categorically different organization: a system that produces not only its own components but also the boundary that encloses them, establishing organizational closure while remaining thermodynamically open. The boundary is the system's product, the system is what the boundary encloses, and the two are mutually constitutive. This is the move from externally imposed constraint-maintenance to self-enforcing constraint-maintenance, from a self-sustaining pattern to a self-constituting individual. Maturana and Varela treated this as the threshold of the living. The contrast with autocatalysis is the difference the framework cares about: active self-sustenance without a self-produced boundary, versus a self-produced boundary that individuates the system.

These two concepts anchor the document's central vocabulary. *Autocatalytic* is used throughout for agent architectures that exhibit the boundaryless, self-sustaining character of an autocatalytic set: stateless and fungible, reconstructed each session from external context (prompt, retrieval, food supply) rather than persisting as an individuated entity. *Autopoietic* is used in the strict Maturana-Varela sense and names the category the framework defines itself against, not a property the framework claims. The framework's own category, heteropoietic individuation, is developed in §3 as a third position: it has the individuating boundary that the autocatalytic pattern lacks, but that boundary is other-produced rather than self-produced, so it is not autopoietic either.

**The autopoietic AI research tradition.** With the concepts established, the AI research programs that pursue autopoiesis on computational substrate can be positioned. The tradition includes two distinguishable research directions: a substrate-independent direction calling for organizational autopoiesis on standard computational substrates, and a substrate-level direction pursuing autopoiesis through specific substrate prescriptions (wetware, chemical AI, new terminology). The framework operates in the substrate-independent territory but claims heteropoietic individuation rather than agent-level autopoiesis (the contrast is developed in §3). This section organizes both directions chronologically and bibliographically.

**Substrate-independent direction.**

**Varela, Maturana, and Uribe (1974)**, "Autopoiesis: the organization of living systems, its characterization and a model" (Biosystems 5, 187-196), established both the theoretical foundation of autopoietic organization and the original computational autopoiesis research program. Their 1974 work used artificial computational media to explore autopoietic organization more than a decade before the first Santa Fe Workshop on Artificial Life. The framework's intellectual debt to this foundational work is the substrate-permissive stance: the original theorists treated computational substrate as a legitimate research medium for organizational autopoiesis. The framework operates in this same substrate territory but makes a different (more modest) claim within it: heteropoietic individuation rather than computational autopoiesis.

**McMullin (2004)**, "30 Years of Computational Autopoiesis: A Review" (Artificial Life 10(3), 277-295), provides the most authoritative documentation of the substrate-independent direction's history. The review covers the computational autopoiesis research program from VMU 1974 forward, with parallel attention to the wet chemistry direction that emerged in the late 1980s. McMullin explicitly defends computational autopoiesis as a legitimate research program against wetware-focused positions, concluding there is "no obstacle of principle in realising autopoietic organisation in an artificial, computational, medium." The framework's intellectual lineage extends back substantially further than the Maturana-Varela 1980 citation typically suggests. McMullin documents the substrate-independent direction's continuous development since 1974, placing the HAH within a research conversation that has been ongoing for over half a century.

McMullin's review also identifies a specific open problem of direct relevance to the framework's central architectural commitment. McMullin developed a heuristic test for autopoietic individuation: whether two putatively individual cells, in direct contact, can reliably maintain their separate identities. Applying this test to the original VMU 1974 model, McMullin reached what he called a "somewhat controversial conclusion," that the original minimal model was not properly autopoietic, because the chain-based bond inhibition mechanism that enabled single-cell self-repair also caused adjacent cells to merge in proximity. McMullin identified individuation as a load-bearing failure mode of the original computational autopoiesis research program. The HAH's OS-level architecture addresses this directly: two agents on the same system have categorically separate identities enforced by the kernel and cannot merge under any internal dynamics. The framework's central architectural commitment can be read as operationalizing a solution to the individuation problem McMullin identified, with kernel-enforced isolation doing the work that VMU 1974's chain-based bond inhibition could not.

**Substrate-level direction.**

**Damiano and Stano (2021)**, "A Wetware Embodied AI? Towards an Autopoietic Organizational Approach Grounded in Synthetic Biology" (Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, 9, 724023), proposes a wetware direction for autopoietic AI research grounded in synthetic biology, pursuing artificial autopoiesis through actual biochemical substrate rather than through computational substrate. This represents a substantively different research program from the HAH. The framework operates entirely on the artificial side of the biological/artificial substrate distinction (see §2.6) and does not engage substantively with the wetware direction. Whether the wetware direction is a productive research path is an open question the framework does not address. The framework leaves that question to researchers pursuing it directly.

**Veloz (2025)**, "Toward aitiopoietic cognition: bridging the evolutionary divide between biological and machine-learned causal systems" (Frontiers in Cognition 4, 1618381), extends the substrate-level direction more strongly than Damiano and Stano. Veloz argues explicitly that *purely computational approaches* cannot achieve constitutional causality, and that future aitiopoietic systems will require *physical substrates where information processing and self-maintaining affordances are materially unified*, with hybrid bio-synthetic architectures as the proposed direction. This commitment is more demanding than the framework operates under, and is also more demanding than the framework's claim makes necessary. The framework does not claim aitiopoiesis or autopoiesis. It claims heteropoietic individuation (see §3). Whether the operational properties Veloz's call points toward can be achieved through heteropoietic individuation on conventional substrate is exactly what the framework's experimental program tests. Whether Veloz's stronger property requires what Veloz claims it requires is a question the framework's program does not adjudicate either way.

**Organoid intelligence** (Smirnova et al. 2023, the Cortical Labs DishBrain work of Kagan et al. 2022) sits at the far end of the substrate-level axis. Rather than proposing biochemical or bio-synthetic substrate in principle, it builds with it in practice: living neural tissue grown from human stem cells, cultured on microelectrode arrays and trained to perform computational tasks, advanced explicitly as a biocomputing direction. It is the substrate-level direction taken to its literal conclusion, computation in living neurons. The framework does not engage organoid intelligence as architectural precedent and places it outside scope, for the reason developed in §2.6: the framework operates on entirely artificial substrate, and organoid intelligence sits at the far substrate-level end of the axis the framework does not work on. The boundary is a scope choice for a feasibility investigation. The ethical reasoning behind it is out of scope for this document, which concerns feasibility rather than ethics, and is addressed in the separate paper noted in §2.6.

### 2.2 The operating-system direction: AIOS and successors

A cluster of recent work has applied operating system principles to LLM agent management. **Mei, Li, et al. (2024)**, "AIOS: LLM Agent Operating System" (arXiv:2403.16971, with subsequent versions through 2025), provides the canonical statement and is the progenitor work the framework extends. AIOS proposes a three-layer architecture (application layer for agent applications, kernel layer integrating the traditional OS kernel with an AIOS kernel as userspace coordination layer, hardware layer) in which the AIOS kernel manages LLM-related activities for agents through scheduling, context management, memory management, storage management, tool management, and access control. As Mei et al. note, the AIOS kernel does not directly interact with hardware but relies on OS system calls for hardware access. The framework notes for the reader that the AIOS "kernel" is therefore a userspace coordination layer rather than a Linux kernel module, a clarification AIOS itself does not address explicitly, but which matters for understanding the architectural relationship between AIOS and the framework's approach.

The framework's relationship to AIOS is continuation and extension, developed in detail in the Theory section (Argument 2, §3). AIOS made the structural move toward OS-level work for agent state and cognition under a stated efficiency motivation (memory manager keeping agent state in RAM close to the LLM, storage manager for persistent agent state, context manager snapshotting LLM inference state, thread-bound system calls carrying an agent identifier). Through these design implementations it recognized that agent state needs to live close to cognition and that the OS level is where this can be built. The architectural pattern available to AIOS in 2023-2024 was centralized coordination: a coordination service above the OS kernel managing agents as clients. The framework extends this trajectory by introducing the harness concept, an internal coordinator within each agent that distributes coordination into agents themselves rather than centralizing it in a service above them, enabling the architectural step from service-managed agents to user-individuated agents.

**AIOS-Agent ecosystem follow-on work** extends the AIOS framing with additional infrastructure: the Cerebrum SDK (the AIOS-Agent SDK referenced in Mei et al. 2024 Appendix B), and the AgentSites / Internet of AgentSites architecture (Zhang & Zhang 2025, "Planet as a Brain," arXiv:2504.14411), which extends AIOS Server into a decentralized runtime for peer-to-peer agent hosting and discovery. This follow-on work maintains the centralized coordination pattern at each node (each AIOS Server instance is itself a coordination service for the agents it hosts) while distributing coordination across nodes at internet scale.

**MemOS (MemTensor)** applies similar logic specifically to memory operations, treating memory management for LLM agents as an OS-level concern with "memory cubes" as composable knowledge bases, hybrid retrieval, and cross-task skill reuse. The memory-OS framing inherits the AIOS architectural pattern: agent memory management as a userspace coordination service.

**Bara (2026)**, "Who Is Building the Agent-Native Operating System?" (Medium analysis), surveys the landscape of agent-native operating system attempts and identifies AIOS as the most rigorous academic statement of the OS-level direction for agent infrastructure. The analysis documents the field's convergence on OS-level treatment of agent workloads.

What these works share with the framework is the recognition that agent workloads need OS-level substrate rather than cloud abstraction. The framework's extension via the harness concept is developed in the Theory section. The gap they leave open is genuine user-level individuation: each work routes agent cognition through a shared coordination layer, which (per Argument 3, §3) cannot produce the substrate-level cognitive diversity that supports productive friction through configuration alone.

### 2.3 Convergent prior art at the same architectural level

A separate cluster of recent work operates at substantively the same architectural level as the framework, sharing the architectural commitment to OS-level isolation for agents and per-agent persistent state. The relationship is convergent engineering: independent research arriving at architecturally similar commitments from different starting points.

**Packer et al. (2023)** and the evolution into **Letta** represent the closest modern lineage for the framework's stateful-agent direction. MemGPT (arXiv:2310.08560, "Towards LLMs as Operating Systems") introduced virtual context management, an OS-inspired paging technique that extends effective context through hierarchical memory tiers and function-mediated self-editing, with the explicit commitment that "memory edits and retrieval are entirely self-directed" (Section 2.3). The evolution into Letta extended this foundation into multi-agent production deployment with persistent memory, sleep-time reconciliation, and configuration-level agent isolation. The relationship between the HAH and Letta is substantive engagement, not competition. The architectural comparison points below are primarily between the HAH and Letta's current production architecture. MemGPT's 2023 paper provides the virtual context management foundation that Letta builds on. The two programs make different load-bearing architectural commitments:

- *Memory operation pattern.* Letta inherits MemGPT's foundational commitment to deliberative memory operations (memory edits and retrieval "entirely self-directed" by the LLM, per MemGPT Section 2.3). The agent invokes tools (`memory_rethink`, `memory_insert`, `memory_replace`, `archival_memory_insert`) to manage its own state. The HAH uses autonomic emission below the deliberative layer, with substrate writes triggered by harness-side pattern detection in decoder text without tool invocation.
- *Individuation mechanism.* Letta uses configuration-level isolation (per-agent identifiers, application-layer scoping). The HAH uses OS-enforced cellular individuation (per-agent UNIX user, SO_PEERCRED authentication, ArangoDB instance bound to OS identity).
- *Memory substrate.* Letta uses a four-surface taxonomy (in-context blocks, message buffer, recall, archival) distinguished by access mechanism. The HAH uses uniform substrate with provenance metadata, where retrieval is uniform and ranking is provenance-weighted.
- *Reconciliation semantics.* Letta's sleep-time writes are unilateral memory edits. The HAH's reconciliation operations produce typed verdicts (`Agreed | SupersedeWithTrace | SupersedeWithDb | FlagUnknown`) over a three-party reconciliation bundle (trace, reflection, substrate state), with audit trail and explicit unresolved-state structures.
- *Concurrency.* Letta's reported pivot to git-backed worktrees per subagent represents convergent evidence from production engineering pressure for what the HAH's cellular individuation enforces by design. Their pivot, after multiple iterations on multi-agent sleep architectures, is strongly corroborating operational pressure for the concurrency problem HAH Proposition 1 names. The HAH predicts that OS-level cellular individuation provides the structural answer to this problem with substantially less operational overhead than worktree-based merge resolution requires.

**Quine** (arXiv:2603.18030), "Realizing LLM Agents as Native POSIX Processes," shares the framework's commitment to OS-level isolation enforced through kernel mechanisms. Quine maps agent boundary to address space with memory isolation between agents enforced by the MMU at hardware level rather than by application-code conventions. The architectural commitment is shared. The framework extends Quine's approach by adding heterogeneous specialized neural networks within each agent (rather than single-LLM-per-process), autonomic harness-mediated coordination, and persistent substrate-bound state beyond process memory.

**AgentSys** (arXiv:2602.07398), "Secure and Dynamic LLM Agents Through Explicit Hierarchical Memory Management," applies process memory isolation principles to agent memory management. AgentSys organizes agents hierarchically with worker agents executing in isolated contexts, only schema-validated return values crossing isolation boundaries. The empirical demonstration that context isolation achieves a 2.19% attack success rate without additional mechanisms supports the framework's Argument 2 indirectly: OS-style isolation produces measurable security benefits that application-layer alternatives cannot match.

**Concurrent Modular Agent** (Maruyama et al. 2025, arXiv:2508.19042) shares the framework's commitment to multiple specialized modules within a single agent coordinated through internal communication. CMA's modules are LLM-based (all of the same type), whereas the framework's specialized components are heterogeneous neural networks (decoder, embedder, cue classifier, GNN) of different architectural types. CMA cites Brooks' subsumption architecture (1986) as historical precedent for hierarchical reactive modules. The framework extends this lineage by combining the multiple-specialized-modules-within-one-agent architecture with cellular individuation through OS-level identity.

**Brooks (1986)**, "A Robust Layered Control System for a Mobile Robot," and the subsumption architecture tradition more broadly, provide the historical precedent for multiple specialized modules coordinated within a single agent. Brooks's work demonstrated that intelligent behavior could emerge from layered reactive modules without a central controller. The framework extends this lineage by replacing hand-coded reactive modules with specialized neural networks and by adding cellular individuation through OS-level identity that Brooks's robotics work did not require.

**Memory benchmarks.** MemoryAgentBench (arXiv:2507.05257) and Letta's Context-Bench provide benchmark suites designed to evaluate agent memory capabilities. The framework's validation program uses memory-specific benchmarks to test workload-performance and identity-continuity predictions, and adds a multi-session consolidation benchmark (Appendix A) targeting a gap existing benchmarks do not appear to isolate.

**Continuum Memory Architectures** (arXiv:2601.09913) independently identify that stateless RAG memory fails to provide the operational properties serious agent workloads require, paralleling the framework's storage-as-memory diagnostic. The diagnostic foundation is shared across multiple research programs working in this territory.

**Episodic memory in LLMs.** Park et al. (2023) *Generative Agents* (arXiv:2304.03442) demonstrates that persistent memory structures meaningfully change agent behavior on long-running tasks. The HAH extends this by claiming that episodic memory in an individuated substrate produces *constitutive* alignment in a way that episodic memory grafted onto fungible substrate cannot.

### 2.4 Critical interlocutors: Jaeger 2023/2024 and Bianchini 2023

The framework's most substantive engagements with the autopoietic AI tradition are with Jaeger (2023/2024) and Bianchini (2023). Both interlocutors articulate positions the framework substantially accepts. Jaeger's paradigm-level critique identifies the organizational complexity and hierarchical circularity missing from flat LLM-based systems. Bianchini's theoretical call articulates what an autopoietic AI research program should produce: substrate-independent agent-level organizational autopoiesis. The framework's relationship to both is one of essential empirical groundwork rather than competing philosophical position.

**Jaeger 2023/2024: the paradigm-level critique.**

Jaeger (2023/2024) is often read as the canonical philosophical opposition to claims of algorithmic agency. The HAH reads him differently, as the most precise diagnostician working in this territory, whose critique the framework substantially accepts. Read carefully, Jaeger's claims are paradigm-level rather than categorical: he hedges repeatedly to "the current algorithmic framework of AI research," to "contemporary approaches," to LLMs and recurrent neural nets specifically. He acknowledges that "we do not know at this point what such an architecture would look like" and that "direct embodiment necessitates a completely different computational paradigm," pointing toward neuromorphic computing and configurable materials as the direction such a shift would take. Jaeger may well be correct. It may be that true natural agency, autopoietic agency in the full biological sense he argues for, requires the substrate-level paradigm change he envisions, and the framework does not dispute this. Jaeger and the HAH see the same problem: current AI architectures are too flat, rich in computational complexity but lacking the organizational complexity and hierarchical circularity required for autopoiesis. They take different principled approaches in response. Jaeger places his bet on substrate-level paradigm change. The framework places its bet on agent-level architectural organization within conventional computing substrate, that the conceptual approach can be tested without specialized hardware or software, using primitives that already exist, and that the architecture can get reasonably far before substrate-level innovation becomes the binding constraint. Both bets remain to be adjudicated empirically.

What Jaeger diagnoses correctly is that current systems, LLMs and the harnesses built around them, cannot achieve autopoietic agency. The HAH identifies why: the field has built only the cortex. The model, however sophisticated, is the inference organ. It is capable at single-shot pattern completion and structurally incapable of accumulating experience past the context window. Every session starts fresh. Every conversation is reconstructed from a context dump. Every "agent" is a remarkable vocabulary attached to a goldfish. A cortex without a hippocampus is precisely the failure mode Jaeger names: session drift, hallucinated continuity, structural inability to maintain identity over operational time. And the gap exists because the field has been building under an economic constraint Jaeger does not name but the framework does: the SaaS deployment model demands fungibility, which demands portability, which demands statelessness, which forces accumulated memory out of the architecture and into externalized retrieval-as-augmentation. The current paradigm fails because its economic model forces an architectural choice that produces the failure modes Jaeger correctly identifies.

Jaeger inherits one assumption from the field he critiques, and this is where the HAH departs from him: the assumption that the model is the agent. Throughout Jaeger's analysis, "algorithm," "LLM," and "AI system" are used interchangeably, because in the world he is critiquing they refer to the same entity, a model wrapped in scaffolding, where the model is the cognizer and the scaffolding is external infrastructure the model calls into. The framework rejects this collapse. The model is to the agent what a neuron, or a cortex, is to a brain: a specialized component within a heterogeneous composite, not the composite itself. When Jaeger establishes that algorithms cannot be autopoietic, the framework agrees, and the agreement extends: the framework does not claim autopoiesis at the composite agent level either. The framework claims something different: *heteropoietic individuation* at the composite level (see §3). Jaeger's argument that algorithms cannot be autopoietic is granted. The framework's empirical question is whether heteropoietic individuation at the composite level produces the operational properties Jaeger identifies as missing (sustained identity, accumulated experience, structural agency) without requiring the substrate-level paradigm change Jaeger calls for.

What makes this practically interesting is how little engineering is required to take Jaeger's critique seriously. The field has treated agent individuation as an open research problem, something to be solved through novel architecture, neuromorphic substrates, or biological wetware. None of that is necessary to begin testing the conceptual approach. Unix has provided identity individuation since 1971: a user has a name, a uid, a home directory, owned files, processes that inherit credentials, kernel-enforced isolation from other users, and persistent state across sessions. Run an agent as a system user (its own uid, its own home directory, its own ArangoDB instance bound to that identity, its own Unix domain socket authenticated by SO_PEERCRED) and the operating system supplies individuation without any custom engineering. The hippocampus Jaeger's diagnosis identifies as missing becomes a persistent storage substrate bound to that user. The cellular boundary becomes kernel-enforced. Accumulated experience becomes the user's own filesystem and database, owned by no other agent and not portable to any other substrate. None of these are novel mechanisms. They are operating-system primitives older than most of the field that has overlooked them. The reason the field has not built it this way is not engineering difficulty. It is that the SaaS economic model rules out individuation as a matter of design: fungible infrastructure cannot host non-fungible agents. Remove that economic constraint and the architecture is largely already built.

**Bianchini 2023: the theoretical call.**

Bianchini (2023), "Autopoiesis of the artificial: from systems to cognition" (BioSystems 230, 104936), surveys current AI approaches against autopoietic criteria. He concludes that cognitive architectures (SOAR, ACT-R) are allopoietic, built by programmers as unified units with explicit reference to cognitive parts, and that neural networks self-organize only within pre-existing systems built with specific features. His positive call is for substrate-independent agent-level organizational autopoiesis: by utilizing autopoiesis theory's specific notion of organization (a network of invariant processes that self-produce along with their components, independent of the substrate), it is possible to create self-emergent systems serving as promising candidates for AI cognitive systems. Bianchini explicitly endorses multiple realizability and notes that hardware/software implementation of organizational autopoiesis on standard computational substrates faces "no hindrance in principle." The framework reads Bianchini as the most explicit contemporary articulation of what an autopoietic AI research program should produce, and positions itself as operationalizing this call.

The framework's specific architectural commitments operationalize the substrate-independent territory Bianchini's call points toward, but with a more modest philosophical claim. The physical boundary between agents on a system is maintained by OS-level mechanisms (kernel-enforced isolation, per-agent identity, per-agent persistent state). Within that heteropoietic boundary, internal processes do the individuation work: harness coordination among heterogeneous specialized components, autonomic responses producing operational support without decoder invocation, accumulated experience consolidated into memory structures, the resulting differentiation along constitutive alignment dimensions. The framework's empirical question, in relation to Bianchini's call: assuming physical boundary maintenance between agents on a system, do the software's internal processes produce sustained identity differentiation through their own operation?

A genuine disagreement remains, paralleling the Jaeger case. Bianchini's organizational autopoiesis includes self-production of components. Biological cells produce their own enzymes through recursive metabolic networks. The framework does not claim component self-production, nor does it claim boundary self-production. The framework explicitly does not claim autopoiesis at the agent level. It claims heteropoietic individuation (see §3), where the other-produced boundary is developed as a deliberate architectural choice rather than a shortfall. Whether heteropoietic individuation satisfies the operational properties Bianchini's call points toward is empirical and contested. The framework's bet is that the operational properties (sustained identity, accumulated experience, productive friction, constitutive alignment) are achievable through heteropoietic individuation without requiring full agent-level autopoiesis.

**The framework as essential empirical groundwork.**

What the framework offers, in relation to both Jaeger's and Bianchini's broader proposals, is essential empirical groundwork rather than competing philosophical position. Both interlocutors call for ambitious moves: substrate-level paradigm change for Jaeger (neuromorphic computing, hardware/software interfusion, configurable materials, eventually autonomous hardware evolution), component self-production for Bianchini (the artificial-substrate equivalent of cells producing their own enzymes through recursive metabolic networks). These moves require substantial research investment beyond what current mathematical models or conventional computing substrates provide. Both are predicated on a prior assumption that has not been adequately tested: that current mathematical models, given a kernel-maintained physical boundary, cannot achieve sustained identity differentiation through accumulated experience over operational time. The framework's experimental program tests this assumption directly. Give the agent a kernel-maintained physical boundary (heteropoietic envelope, removing the boundary-self-production problem from the scope of what must be demonstrated). Then ask whether the internal processes operating within that boundary (harness coordination, autonomic responses, accumulated experience, memory consolidation) produce sustained heteropoietic individuation over operational time. If sustained heteropoietic individuation produces the operational properties motivating the autopoietic call, then the more ambitious moves Jaeger and Bianchini call for may not be necessary for those properties. If heteropoietic individuation does not produce these properties, then their proposals become empirically motivated as next directions to pursue, and the framework's results identify which specific operational properties motivate them. The framework does not compete with either proposal. It provides the prior empirical work that determines whether the more ambitious moves are necessary, and informs the design of those moves if they prove necessary.

### 2.5 Adjacent intellectual traditions

The framework operates in conversation with several broader traditions whose vocabulary and conceptual tools inform the work without being directly critiqued or cited as architectural precedent.

**Shannon-Weaver communication theory.** Weaver's three-level framework (technical, semantic, effectiveness) from his 1949 introduction to Shannon's mathematical theory provides the diagnostic instrument used throughout this document (§1.1). The framework's name commits to Weaver's call for tools operating at Levels B and C, not just at Level A.

**Prigogine's dissipative structures.** Prigogine's work on far-from-equilibrium thermodynamics and dissipative structures provides theoretical grounding for understanding how organized structure emerges and maintains itself through constraint relationships. The hypothesis draws on this tradition for the formal treatment of how individuation produces structure rather than merely enclosing it.

**Continual learning and lifelong learning.** The ML tradition of continual learning addresses how neural networks accumulate skills over time without catastrophic forgetting. Most continual-learning work operates within the autocatalytic paradigm, a single model accumulating parameter updates over a task stream. The HAH claims something different, and more permissive than a training prohibition. The accumulation that constitutes the agent's identity occurs in a substrate-side memory bound to individuated identity, and the load-bearing commitment is substrate-recoverability. Per-agent identity stays recoverable from the substrate, so the cortex remains fungible and substituting it for a fresh one does not destroy the agent. The framework precludes no training. The cortex may be trained, pretrained from within the apparatus, or co-developed with the substrate. What the hypothesis claims is where identity ends up, not that the weights are held fixed. A relatively stable cortex names per-agent stability over operational time, no per-interaction weight updates encoding this agent's history, rather than a fleet-wide prohibition on improving the cortex as a fungible artifact. A training regime that leaves per-agent identity unrecoverable without the weights is a result bearing on the hypothesis, not a precluded design. The relationship to continual learning stays complementary rather than competing, and the two traditions could compose.

**Autonomic computing.** Kephart and Chess (2003) proposed the vision of autonomic computing as systems that manage themselves through self-configuration, self-optimization, self-healing, and self-protection. The HAH's autonomic-response proposition (Proposition 2, a Level C coordination capability) sits directly downstream of this tradition. The framework's specific commitments (autonomic emission of assertions from decoder text, autonomic surfacing of context based on harness-side classification, autonomic reconciliation during memory reconciliation operations) operationalize Kephart and Chess's vision for the LLM-agent context. The lineage is direct. The HAH extends autonomic computing principles to the individuated-agent architecture rather than originating the autonomic framing.

**Agent benchmarks and agentic frameworks.** ReAct (Yao et al. 2022), AgentBench, and related work measure agent capabilities on diverse task suites within the autocatalytic paradigm. The hypothesis predicts that these benchmarks reveal specific failure modes (§1.3) on workloads requiring constitutive alignment that autocatalytic frameworks cannot reach through harness refinement alone.

**Persistent agent frameworks.** Artificial-life-inspired persistent agent research overlaps substantively with the HAH's claims about identity continuity, narrative memory, and persistent self-improvement, approaching similar questions through a different intellectual lineage (artificial life and complex adaptive systems rather than autopoietic biology specifically). The framework engages with this work as adjacent research in the persistent-agent tradition.

**Continual harness research.** Research on agent-harness refinement tests different hypotheses within the autocatalytic paradigm: making autocatalytic agents work better through automated harness adaptation. The HAH tests an adjacent proposition, that individuated architecture serves workloads autocatalytic refinement cannot reach. Both programs do real scientific work in adjacent territory.

**Knowledge graph and GraphRAG research.** Microsoft GraphRAG and follow-on work demonstrate that structured retrieval substantially outperforms flat-text retrieval for serious inference tasks. The HAH claims that knowledge graphs maintained dynamically by harness-side autonomic processes function as substrate-side accumulated state in the architectural sense (Proposition 4), not just as better retrieval substrate.

**Actor-Network Theory.** Latour and the ANT tradition provide vocabulary for thinking about how human and non-human actors participate in networks of meaning and effect. The framework's relationship to ANT is a friendly amendment rather than a rejection: ANT's symmetric methodology for tracing associations is preserved, and one specific distinction is added that recovers analytical depth ANT's strict symmetry tends to bracket. Within the category of actors, some are *agents* and some are not. Agents are actors whose action persists through accumulated state and whose communication is aimed at producing change in the environment over time rather than only at producing immediate response. The distinction is empirically grounded rather than philosophically arbitrary. Agency exhibits a continuum across entities that biological and behavioral science has already mapped: a rock participates causally in networks by occupying space and redirecting interactions but exhibits no accumulated state, purposive communication, or identity-maintaining action over time. A houseplant exhibits substantially more (tracks light tropically, allocates resources in response to conditions, signals through volatile chemicals, maintains identity through metabolism, though with limited communicative purposiveness). A dog exhibits more again (purposeful communication, identity recognition, goals extending across days, accumulated experience producing differentiated behavior). A human exhibits more still. The framework's actor/agent distinction picks out a region of this continuum where entities exhibit operational properties (identity maintenance, accumulated experience, purposeful temporal extension of action) that distinguish them analytically from actors whose participation is at point of contact.

This minimal modification preserves what ANT does well. Symmetric network analysis remains useful. Treatment of non-humans as network participants remains useful. The methodology of tracing associations remains useful. What the modification adds is recognition that within the category of actors, the agent subset has analytical properties that ANT's strict symmetry brackets: internal coordination producing operational unity, accumulated experience producing differentiated identity, communication aimed at change over time rather than only at immediate response. These are the properties social science has substantial analytical resources for understanding (the interpretivist tradition, institutional analysis, organizational sociology, ethnographic methods for understanding internal practices within network nodes). The actor/agent distinction makes the scope of these resources explicit: they apply to agents, not to all actors.

The framework's engagement with ANT is not vocabulary borrowing. The ANT actor/agent distinction provides the external definition in the framework's dual definition of agency (developed in §3). An artificial entity is an agent in the extended ANT sense if it exhibits identity maintenance through accumulated experience (the heteropoietic individuation property), measurable through the six constitutive alignment dimensions (specified in §3). The framework's empirical question is where on the agency continuum the WeaverTools agents land. The framework does not claim its heteropoietic agents are equivalent to human agency, does not claim sentience, does not claim consciousness. It claims the agents exhibit operational properties measurable on the established agency continuum, and that the architectural commitments produce systems that land somewhere meaningfully past actors with state attached. Where exactly they land is empirical, tested through the measurement program.

### 2.6 On vocabulary and method

The framework's claim to standing in social-science and philosophy-of-mind discussions rests on disciplined vocabulary and a substantive methodological tradition, not on credentials or terminology alone. This section states both.

**Substrate-categorical commitment.** The HAH operates entirely on the artificial side of the biological/artificial substrate distinction and does not pursue substrate-mixing research directions (wetware, synthetic biology, biohybrid systems). This is a scope boundary for a feasibility investigation, not a claim that those directions are impossible or unimportant. The reasoning behind the boundary is ethical and is therefore out of scope here, since this document concerns feasibility rather than ethics, and it is addressed in a separate paper. When the framework says *artificial*, it means entirely artificial substrate.

**The strategic-polysemy critique the discipline answers.** LaCroix, Mallory, and Luccioni (2026), "Strategic Polysemy in AI Discourse" (FAccT '26, arXiv:2604.21043), identify two patterns. *Strategic polysemy* is communicating distinct senses of a word to different audiences while preserving deniability of one sense. *Glosslighting* is using technically redefined terms to evoke powerful associations while retaining the ability to deny them through retreat into technical reinterpretation. Both name real problems in AI discourse that this framework cannot fully escape but is committed to engaging substantively. The framework's vocabulary discipline is its response, operating at four layers.

*Technical-redefined terms with precise specification.* *Heteropoietic individuation* is the central technical term, defined by contrast with autopoiesis in §3: kernel-produced boundary plus internally-produced identity differentiation through accumulated experience. *Autopoietic* refers to the strict Maturana-Varela sense (self-produced boundary and components) and serves as the conceptual reference point the framework defines itself against, not a property the framework claims. *Individuation* is the single architectural commitment, operationalized through OS-level user identity, kernel-enforced isolation, and per-agent persistent state. *Constitutive alignment* is operationalized through six measurable metrics (§3). *Autonomic* is used in the Kephart and Chess (2003) sense of self-managing engineered infrastructure operating below the deliberative layer, not the biological sense of self-produced regulation. The harness's autonomic functions are engineered and deterministic, and §3.4 specifies their constitutive role by their position in the agent's loop rather than by the term.

*Technical terms preferred where they exist.* *Memory* is rendered as *persistent state substrate* or *accumulated state structure* in formal contexts. *Reasoning*, *thinking*, and *cognition* are rendered as *inference*, *decoder operation*, *model computation*, or *token generation*. *Sleep cycles* and *dreams* are rendered as *memory reconciliation operations*, with a four-stage structure named explicitly (per-episode reflection, verdict generation, extraction and commit, cross-episode synthesis). What the codebase historically called *belief nodes* are renamed *assertion nodes*, and *soul nodes* are renamed *axiomatic anchors*. The technical content is preserved. The anthropomorphic loading is reduced.

*Borrowed philosophical vocabulary with substantive engagement.* *Agent* and *actor* are used in the Actor-Network Theory sense, extended by the framework's actor/agent distinction (§2.5). *Autopoietic* engages Maturana and Varela by contrast (§3). *Constitutive* engages the philosophical tradition distinguishing constitution from causation. *Dissipative* engages Prigogine's physical theory. The framework's right to use these terms rests on substantive engagement.

*Explicit metaphors named as metaphors.* *Cellular individuation* is a biological metaphor for OS-level identity isolation. It organizes thinking without claiming biological equivalence. *Cortex* and *hippocampus* are anatomical metaphors for cognitive component placement. *"Latency is the enemy of agency"* is a compressed slogan pointing to technical specification rather than substituting for it. The negative mapping matters: *cellular individuation* does not import chemical diffusion, metabolism, or membrane permeability. The framework's individuation is implemented through OS-level user identity and kernel-enforced isolation operating at electronic speeds across whatever distances the hardware spans. The biological metaphors are scaffolding for thinking about architectural commitments operationalized through OS mechanisms. The OS mechanisms are what the framework claims operates. Some operational components retain playful naming where the playfulness explicitly disclaims the metaphorical loading: NAAPN ("Not An AI Power Nap") names within-session context relief. SNRN ("Seriously Not Right Now") names the equivalent state during memory reconciliation. These invoke the metaphorical loading at the same moment they disclaim it.

**Engagement with LaCroix's critique of "agent."** LaCroix et al. specifically critique "agent" and "agentic AI" as exemplifying strategic polysemy, terms invoking folk-psychological intuitions of autonomy and intention while reducing, when challenged, to a literal description ("model wrapped in a control loop with memory, tools, and a task-selection policy"). The HAH uses "agent" as a central architectural concept, so the critique applies directly. The framework's use is grounded in ANT and extended by the actor/agent distinction (§2.5), and its claims about its agents are empirically falsifiable through the program in Appendix A (twin-system divergence, sustained identity, constitutive alignment). Whether the framework's agents are agents in the substantial sense it claims, or remain sophisticated instances of the literal description, is empirically adjudicable. The framework accepts LaCroix's foreseeability principle: it is responsible for the foreseeable uptake of its terminology, and the experimental program discharges that responsibility, not the specifications and disclaimers alone (LaCroix et al. note that "the inclusion of scare quotes or disavowing footnotes does not discharge this responsibility"). LaCroix et al. also invoke McDermott's (1976) "wishful mnemonics" critique to advocate colorless or sanitized language. Applied to the HAH, that would replace the framework's vocabulary with colorless alternatives (system, kernel-bounded process, persistent-state-bound differentiation metrics), eliminating the foreseeability risk and the philosophical contribution alike. The framework's claims are substantive philosophical and systems-theory claims, not only engineering claims. The loaded vocabulary is part of the contribution. This is a defensible choice but not the only one. The colorless alternative would also be defensible and would carry lower foreseeability risk. The framework's bet is that engagement with the autopoiesis tradition by contrast is worth the foreseeability cost, and that the experimental program discharges the responsibility the loaded vocabulary creates.

**Methodological tradition: experimental ethnography of AI.** The HAH is not an engineering project that decorates technical work with social-science vocabulary. It operates in the combined tradition of *experimental archaeology*, *experimental anthropology*, and *Science and Technology Studies*. Experimental archaeology constructs artifacts using inferred techniques to learn what those techniques implied for the communities that practiced them. The knowledge requires the act of construction. Experimental anthropology participates within communities to learn from inside. STS in the Latour, Callon, and Mol tradition treats sociotechnical assemblages as the proper unit of analysis, refusing the separation of "the technical" from "the social." WeaverTools operates in this combined tradition: it constructs alternative agent architectures to learn what current architectures really are, what they make possible and impossible, what becomes visible only when alternatives exist for comparison. The empirical program is also an ethnographic program. The deeper claim is anthropological: AI models are trained on human-generated content, built by humans, and deployed into human communities as tools for making meaning and coordinating action. Humans are toolmakers and meaning-makers, and humans are now making tools that make meaning. That conjunction warrants direct study through the traditions that exist to study tool-making and meaning-making. The framework accepts LaCroix's foreseeability principle as applying to architectural choices as well as vocabulary: it is responsible for the foreseeable consequences of building and deploying heteropoietic individuated agents. The empirical program in Appendix A discharges this responsibility. The methodological statement names the tradition, and the empirical work demonstrates the framework lives up to it.

### 2.7 On measurement and qualification

This document is an experimental ethnography of AI architecture. It constructs an alternative agent architecture and observes how the digital environment that architecture creates shapes the entity operating within it, in the combined tradition named in §2.6. That framing is not decoration. It determines what kind of claim the framework makes at each point and what standard of evidence each claim answers to. The framework makes hard-science claims about the substrate and interpretive claims about individuation, and it holds both to rigor rather than letting either dissolve the other. Qualification is not the unscientific residue left over when measurement runs out. A quantification with no qualification is meaningless, because a number means nothing until someone states what it is a number of and what magnitude of it matters.

The framework can press quantification further than the social sciences usually can, and the reason is specific. The social sciences qualify heavily because their subjects are opaque, non-reproducible, and silent about their own histories. You cannot read a mind, you cannot clone a person and run the same life twice, and you never hold ground truth about what a subject has experienced. The structure has to be inferred from behavior, and disciplined inference is qualification. For an agent constructed the way this framework constructs one, those three barriers largely fall. The accumulated state is logged and readable. The operational history is ground truth because the system recorded it. The subject is forkable, which is why the twin experiment (Appendix A.4) is not a clever proxy for a controlled study but the controlled study itself, the matched-pair design human social science can never run. In the terms of the framework's critical-realist commitments, the generative mechanism moves out of the inferred and into the observed, for the parts that were built.

This reach is not accidental. The framework located individuation in the substrate, in the persistent state, the provenance metadata, the axiomatic anchors, and the consolidation record, all observable by construction. Had individuation been placed only in the trained engine's internal representations, the framework would be back in social-science opacity, inferring identity from output. The decision to make memory and identity substrate-resident is the same decision that makes individuation measurable. Design and tractability are one choice.

The same decision has a second consequence the framework treats as load-bearing. A substrate-resident identity in an operator-owned, inspectable substrate is auditable and editable by construction. The operator can read what the agent has accumulated, its policy, its axiom weighting, its memory and provenance, and can correct it through the harness's governance operations. This inverts what the term heteropoietic might suggest. A fully autopoietic system would be opaque and ungovernable by construction, since its identity would reside in its own self-produced internals, which no outside party owns or can read. The framework's identity is legible and correctable precisely because the boundary is other-produced and the substrate is operator-owned. The otherness that makes the agent substrate-bound is the same otherness that makes it governable. This is a governance surface rather than solved alignment, a precondition for alignment work rather than its achievement. It has two sides that hold together. The agent resists one-off adversarial injection by provenance weight (failure mode 4) while remaining auditable and editable by the legitimate operator. Resistant to the adversary, correctable by the owner.

The reach has limits, and naming them precisely is what keeps the framework honest rather than triumphalist. Three things transparency does not convert into measurement. First, the transparency is into the constructed substrate, not the trained engine. The substrate is observable, the engine is a black box interpretability has not opened, so the line between what the framework measures and what it qualifies tracks the line between what was built to be observable and what was trained and cannot be read. Second, the significance of a measure is a judgment, not a reading. The framework can record that two agents diverge by a given percentage on a given dimension. Whether that magnitude is a distinct identity or the same agent under noise is an interpretive call, disciplined by a pre-registered threshold so it is committed in advance and can be wrong, but not made by the measurement on its own. Third, and most important, quality is qualitative no matter how much is logged. Whether an individuated agent is good, a better collaborator, a better coder, a worthwhile entity, is not a hidden internal variable the framework failed to read. A model can top a benchmark and still be the worse coder, and every practitioner knows it. The framework does not mistake its ability to quantify the internals for an ability to quantify the quality. Transparency of mechanism is not quantifiability of quality.

From this follows the rule that governs every quantitative claim in this document. A number exists to falsify and to set a floor. It does not exist to certify goodness. The latency budget, the divergence threshold, the provenance-resolution rate, the correctness floor, each rules something out, and each can establish that individuation is occurring. None of them crowns the agent as good. Quality sits above the floor and is judged the way a person's quality is judged, by the people who work with the agent. This is the same falsificationist discipline the propositions already follow, extended to the social-theoretic claims, and it is where the framework parts company with the dominant measure of success in the field, which treats a benchmark that clears a floor as though it had named a winner.

This sets the standard for the framework's most interpretation-prone claims, the Actor-Network-Theory extended-agency tests built on the six constitutive alignment dimensions (§2.5, Appendix A.10). Those tests are anchored to pre-registered numerical thresholds so the judgment is disciplined and contestable rather than impressionistic. The thresholds are anchors for falsification, not definitions of agency. A figure-skating rubric makes scoring disciplined and contestable without converting skating into a physics measurement, and no one calls the sport unscientific for it. The anchors are placed on divergence and provenance, on how far matched twins separate while both stay above the correctness floor, and on whether conflict resolution weights the agent's high-provenance accumulated knowledge over a one-off injected claim. They are not placed on whether the agent overrides its user, because resolving conflicting information by provenance is epistemic discipline and overriding a legitimate instruction is a control failure, and the framework does not confuse the two.

A final scoping note follows from the same logic. Artificial general intelligence is itself a qualitative judgment no one has operationalized, a feel rather than a measure, and the framework does not attempt to operationalize it. The question this document asks is narrower and answerable: does the heteropoietic architecture produce individuation. The framework sets up what can be quantified so there is something to quantify, and leaves the larger negotiation over what to call the result to a conversation wider than this project.

## 3. Theory: the heteropoietic agency hypothesis

This section states the hypothesis. It defines what a heteropoietic agent is, argues why that property requires a specific medium and produces a specific behavior, states the single architectural commitment and the four propositions that follow from it, gives the central empirical claim and the predictions, and bounds the scope. Throughout, each element is tagged with its level in Weaver's diagnostic (§1.1): the target property is Level C, the architectural commitment that supports it is Level A, the semantic operation in between is Level B.

### 3.1 What a heteropoietic agent is

The literature review established the two reference concepts this section builds on (§2.1): autocatalysis, the boundaryless network that sustains itself from a food source without producing a boundary that individuates it, and autopoiesis, the self-constituting entity that produces its own components and its own boundary. Heteropoietic individuation is defined here against both, without re-deriving them. The strict reading of autopoiesis is the one that matters for the contrast: a system is autopoietic if and only if it self-produces its components including its boundary, the way biological cells do.

The framework's agents do not do this. The kernel produces the boundary between agents (a per-agent identity the substrate enforces, kernel-enforced isolation, hardware-protected address spaces). The OS produces the agent's runtime environment. The harness instantiates the agent. The agent does not produce its own boundary. It inherits one from the substrate that hosts it. By the strict reading of autopoiesis, the framework's agents are not autopoietic. They are *heteropoietic* (Greek *hetero-*, "other": the boundary and substrate are other-produced).

The boundary is not produced from outside only at provisioning. It is held from outside continuously, including at runtime and against the agent's own execution. An agent that could rewrite its own constitutive constraints would be producing its own boundary, which is the autopoietic case the framework defines itself against, so the heteropoietic boundary is the one that stays externally held even when the agent is adversarial. This continuous holding is what grounds the governance consequence the abstract states. The credential that can rewrite the agent's axiomatic anchors is custodied outside the agent's reach, so identity stays editable by the operator who holds that custody and not by the agent.

The framework's positive claim is empirical and architectural. Heteropoietic systems with persistent substrate-bound state, kernel-enforced cellular individuation, and harness-coordinated internal processes produce *accumulated identity differentiation*: operational specialization that develops over time through the agent's own internal processes within the heteropoietic envelope. The agent's boundary is other-produced. The agent's internal individuation is self-produced through accumulated experience. This is what the framework calls *heteropoietic individuation*: rich identity-content produced by the agent's own internal processes within an externally-maintained boundary.

This split between an other-produced boundary and self-produced individuation instantiates a more general principle developed in full elsewhere (Bucy, 2026, Paper 1): constraint precedes organization, and organization is the shape of constraint's operation. The kernel boundary is the constraint, and the individuated agent is the organization standing within it. The principle carries one distinction the architecture depends on: a thing can be substrate-bound without being substrate-defined. The agent is bound to its substrate, since removing the substrate dissolves it, and it is not its substrate, since its identity is the accumulated standing rather than the hardware beneath. That is why the agent is not the model, and why moving an agent to new hardware is a migration rather than an execution, provided the accumulated state moves with it. The principle is taken as grounding here and argued in Paper 1, and the document's claims are built to stand on their own engineering and experimental justification, so the framework does not require the reader to accept the principle to assess the hypothesis.

In the diagnostic's terms, the two halves of the claim sit at different levels. The boundary and the privileged channels it creates are Level A, the architectural foundation. The accumulated identity differentiation that develops within the boundary is Level C, the effectiveness property the framework claims. The model doing semantic operation inside is Level B. The whole point of the architecture is that a correctly provisioned Level A foundation lets Level B participate in a Level C property that no amount of additional Level B sophistication produces on its own.

This is empirically distinct from two adjacent categories, the two reference concepts of §2.1. Heteropoietic individuation is distinct from autopoiesis in the strict sense, where the system self-produces its boundary and components. The framework explicitly does not claim this. It is also distinct from autocatalytic deployment, the boundaryless self-sustaining pattern, where heteropoietic envelopes (cloud instances, ephemeral containers, request-bound contexts) host computational processes that sustain themselves as a reconstructed fungible type but do not accumulate individuated state across time. The framework's distinctive claim is that heteropoietic envelopes can host accumulated identity differentiation given specific architectural commitments: substrate-bound persistent state, kernel-enforced isolation, harness coordination, autonomic emission below the deliberative layer.

The hypothesis is falsifiable on this claim. If the individuated agent decays under sustained operation without operator intervention, if its boundaries weaken, its identity drifts, or its accumulated state fragments, the central claim is falsified. If it strengthens, the claim is supported. The self-maintenance prediction (§3.5) is the falsifiable form, and the experiment that tests it is in Appendix A.

The framework's relationship to the two reference concepts is contrast, not extension. They give *heteropoietic individuation* its precise meaning by locating it between them. The autocatalytic pattern is boundaryless, a self-sustaining type with no individuating boundary. Autopoiesis is self-bounded, producing the boundary that individuates it. Heteropoietic individuation is the third position: it has the individuating boundary the autocatalytic pattern lacks, but that boundary is other-produced rather than self-produced, so it is not autopoietic. The framework's substantive contribution is to name and operationalize this third class of artificial agency, which the older binary of autopoietic versus allopoietic did not sharpen.

### 3.2 The dual definition: internal individuation plus external network effect

The framework's definition of *agent* operates from two complementary perspectives, both load-bearing and both empirically testable.

**The internal perspective: heteropoietic individuation.** An entity exhibits the substrate-architectural property defined above: kernel-produced boundary plus internally-produced identity differentiation through accumulated experience. This is the engineering side. It is measurable through twin-system divergence along constitutive alignment dimensions and through self-maintenance (sustained or strengthening individuation under operation without external intervention).

**The external perspective: sustained operational effect in the network.** An entity produces sustained operational effect across the network's temporal evolution through actions that persist via accumulated state. This is the social-theoretic side, developed by Actor-Network Theory (§2.5). ANT distinguishes actors (entities that participate at the point of contact without sustained effect) from agents (whose action persists through accumulated state, producing sustained operational effect across the network's temporal evolution). What makes an entity an agent is not just its internal architecture but its observable functioning as a sustained participant in the network it inhabits. This is a Level C property: effectiveness measured in the world.

**Both are required.** An entity exhibiting internal heteropoietic individuation but no sustained network effect would be a strange computational isolate, internally individuated but operationally inconsequential. An entity producing sustained network effects but lacking internal heteropoietic individuation would be a sophisticated tool that mimics agency without architectural support for it (the cortex-without-hippocampus pattern. §2.4). The framework claims that agents in the full sense exhibit both properties, that both definitions track the same underlying entity from different perspectives, and that both are empirically testable through their respective measurement tracks. The internal definition is tested through the twin experiment and the self-maintenance prediction. The external definition through observable network effects (sustained engagement quality over operational time, accumulated relational patterns, productive friction, durable operational consequences). ANT is not a vocabulary the framework borrows for color. It provides half the definition of what an agent is. Substrate architecture provides the other half. The framework's distinctive contribution is the claim that these two definitions, separately developed in their respective traditions, track the same underlying property, demonstrable through coordinated measurement across both perspectives.

### 3.3 One architectural claim in three causal steps

The HAH makes a single architectural claim that resolves into three connected level-of-organization arguments. They are not independent observations sharing a structural pattern. They are a causal dependency stack. **Argument 1** defines the target property, heteropoietic individuation at the agent level (the Level C target). **Argument 2** defines the required medium, OS-level substrate with harness-distributed coordination (the Level A commitment that supports the target). **Argument 3** defines the resulting behavior, substrate-level cognitive diversity supporting productive friction (the Level C behavior that emerges when the medium supports the target). Each argument depends on the prior. Read as one argument in three causal steps: this property requires this medium and produces this behavior. Each step carries its own falsification condition, and each is tested by an experiment in Appendix A.

**Argument 1 (target property, Level C): heteropoietic individuation lives at the agent level, not the substrate level.**

The autopoietic AI tradition includes substrate-level and substrate-independent directions (§2.1). The framework operates within the substrate-independent territory but does not claim autopoiesis at the agent level. It claims *heteropoietic individuation* at the agent level. Biological cells achieve autopoiesis at the cellular level: the cell wall bounding the cell, the heterogeneous organelles each performing specialized work, the coordination among them within the bounded enclosure producing the cell's operational unity. The framework's architecture mirrors this structure at a different level: the OS provides the boundary (cell wall analogue), the heterogeneous specialized neural networks (decoder, embedder, cue classifier, GNN, and any task-specific models the workload requires) perform specialized work within that boundary (organelle analogues), and harness coordination produces operational unity. What differs is where the boundary comes from, and the difference is an advantage rather than a shortfall. Biological cells produce and continuously maintain their own boundary from their own resources, which is a significant and unceasing energy cost. The framework's agents do not. Heteropoietic individuation places boundary production and enforcement in the substrate, so the agent runs no process to keep itself bounded and spends its resources on its function rather than on its perimeter. This is not a deficient autopoiesis. It is a different and deliberate allocation. The same move that buys the efficiency is the one that makes the agent substrate-bound: externalizing the boundary is exactly why the agent dissolves if the substrate is removed. The framework accepts that cost and claims the upside. The agents are heteropoietic at the boundary level and individuated through accumulated experience within the heteropoietic envelope.

The framework's specific empirical claim is narrower than the philosophical question whether artificial autopoiesis is achievable in the full biological sense. Given physical boundary maintenance between agents on a system (which OS-level mechanisms provide architecturally), the empirical question is whether current mathematical models, coordinated through harness mechanisms with autonomic responses and accumulated experience, can develop sustained individuated identity through their own operations over operational time. This is essential empirical groundwork (§2.4): before pursuing the more ambitious moves the autopoietic AI tradition has called for, the prior empirical question is whether sustained heteropoietic individuation is achievable with conventional computing substrate given physical boundary.

*Falsification.* The target property is observable at the agent level if twin agents with the same model, hardware, and harness configuration produce divergence that exceeds the noise floor, stabilizes, and tracks their histories rather than the model's central tendency (the discriminating measures of A.4). Bare divergence does not suffice, since multi-sourced variation produces it under the null. Argument 1 is falsified if divergence is observable only at the substrate level (different hardware producing different agents) or only at the model level rather than at the agent level given identical lower-layer support, or if agent-level divergence does not exceed the noise floor or show the discriminating properties. The framework predicts agent-level divergence with these properties given identical lower-layer support. Tested by the twin experiment (Appendix A).

**Argument 2 (required medium, Level A): persistent agent operation requires OS-level substrate, and the harness concept enables agent-level individuation within it.**

The SaaS architecture pattern that current AI deployments inherit was developed for stateless request-response services, horizontally scalable through interchangeable instances, with state externalized for instance replaceability. The commitments that make SaaS work (statelessness, ephemerality, instance interchangeability, externalized state, request-response semantics) explicitly reject what persistent agents require: sustained identity across time, accumulated state bound to that identity, durable memory, predictable resource access, deterministic communication paths. SaaS infrastructure precludes these requirements as a matter of architectural design. This is a Level A inadequacy: the foundation does not provide the low-latency, identity-bound, durable channels the upper layers need. In the terms of §1.4, this is the network-in-the-loop cause seen from the side of the required medium: the channels the upper layers lack are the agent's own internal channels, and the SaaS pattern conducts them over the network, which is why the foundation cannot supply them.

This motivates the move from cloud-level to OS-level deployment. The OS provides the substrate on which persistent individuation can exist: OS-level identities, persistent storage bound to identity, kernel-enforced isolation, durable communication paths, predictable resource access. The primitives SaaS rejects are the primitives persistent agents require, and they exist at the OS level. AIOS (§2.2) made the structural move toward OS-level work for agent state under a stated efficiency motivation, using centralized coordination (a coordination service above the OS kernel managing agents as clients), which necessarily makes agents into objects-managed-by-the-service. The framework extends this trajectory by introducing the harness, the agent's internal coordinator: an architectural component within each agent that handles the agent's own resource management, the coordination among its cognitive components, its autonomic responses, and its communication with other agents and the environment. The harness distributes coordination work into each agent rather than centralizing it. With the harness available, the architectural step from service-managed agents to user-individuated agents becomes possible: each agent runs under its own OS-level identity, with kernel-enforced isolation, persistent state bound to that identity, its own cognitive components, and its own harness, with no centralized coordination service above the agents.

The cellular analogy is instructive about where coordination should live, with explicit limits. Tissue is not one cell containing applications and managing them. It is many cells coexisting on shared organism-level infrastructure, each maintaining its own internal coordination among its own organelles. The framework follows this coordination-distribution pattern. But the framework's boundary differs from a biological cell wall in a load-bearing way the metaphor of a *diving bell* captures: an externally provided enclosure lowered in by engineers, where the things inside operate within a boundary they did not produce and could not have produced. A cell wall leaks by design (to permit metabolic exchange). A kernel-enforced boundary does not leak, providing categorical isolation the agent inhabits but did not construct. The deployment is more accurately a *managed cluster* than tissue: engineers provision the OS, kernel, storage substrate, and inter-agent channels, and the agents operate within infrastructure lowered in from outside rather than secreted into existence by the agents themselves.

*Falsification.* WeaverTools agents (OS-level users with harness coordination) versus service-managed architectures (configuration-level isolation, centralized coordination) on the same workload class, measuring sustained identity continuity, concurrent-agent operation under load, adversarial-input contamination resistance across agent boundaries, and memory consolidation efficiency over long-running operation. The framework predicts service-managed architectures hit operational ceilings on these dimensions that OS-level architectures do not, even with comparable model sophistication. If service-managed architectures perform comparably under extended load, OS-level substrate is not structurally necessary and Argument 2 is falsified. The Letta worktree pivot is strongly corroborating operational pressure in the predicted direction (developed below). Tested by the comparative-operation and degraded-substrate experiments (Appendix A).

**Argument 3 (emergent behavior, Level C): stochastic difference and differential environmental friction produce sustained cognitive diversity at the substrate-per-agent level.**

The OS-level boundary in Argument 2 is structurally necessary but does not by itself produce cognitive diversity. The boundary permits diversity by allowing per-agent state to be maintained without contamination. The engine of diversity operates inside the boundary. The framework identifies three coupled mechanisms.

*Stochastic variation in generation (the seed).* Models have multiple viable options for any non-trivial input, and decoder sampling produces stochastic variation across them. Harness routing decisions, autonomic emission patterns, and reconciliation verdicts add further variation. Two agents with identical model, harness configuration, and starting state produce different outputs on the same input, not because they are different agents yet, but because the system is stochastic. This is variation, not yet individuation.

*Interaction with the world (the lock-in).* Once a stochastic outcome occurs, the environment responds to it and the memory system records it with provenance. If the outcome succeeds on the agent's task, downstream surfacing and axiom anchoring reinforce the pattern. If it fails, the pattern is not reinforced and is competed against by alternatives in future operation. Crucially, the environment converts a stochastic outcome into a deterministic consequence the agent must then operate against. In a closed world such as HeroBench this conversion is exact: a stochastic choice changes the available action set or what the agent encounters next, so the world holds the variation in place rather than letting it average out. The friction is differential because each agent, having made different early choices, faces a different world thereafter.

*Stochastic variation in consolidation (the compounding).* The consolidation operations that integrate episodes into the substrate are themselves stochastic. What gets consolidated, how episodes are reconciled, and which patterns are reinforced as axiomatic all carry variation. The agent's accumulated state therefore diverges not only through which outcomes the world locked in but through how each agent's consolidation stochastically integrated them. This third source operates on the accumulated record rather than on the immediate output, and it compounds with the first two over operational time.

What the short HeroBench runs showed is the first two of these at work: stochastic variation in generation, converted by the closed world into deterministic environmental consequences, producing early divergent operational preferences between twins given the same model, harness, and benchmark. The honest characterization is stochastic variation taking hold through environmental lock-in, not a demonstration of sustained individuation. Whether that coupling alone is sufficient to individuate remains open. The framework's actual hypothesis is stronger, and it is the one the program is built to try to disprove. Individuation is produced by the compounding of all three sources (stochastic variation in generation, interaction with the world, and stochastic variation in consolidation) accumulated over a significant operational period. These compounding effects eventually yield stable variation in individuation between agents. The short-duration findings are consistent with the seed of this process, not a confirmation of it.

The OS-level boundary is what allows this divergence to persist as individuation rather than dissipate as contamination. Without kernel-enforced individuation, the stochastic outcomes of one agent leak into another's substrate, and the divergence that would otherwise produce per-agent differentiation produces cross-agent contamination instead. The boundary does not create the divergence. The stochastic engine does. The boundary lets the divergence accumulate as differentiated identity rather than collapse into shared noise.

**The Letta engineering trajectory as corroboration in the predicted direction.** Letta's reported pivot corroborates exactly this dynamic. After multiple iterations on multi-agent sleep architectures, the engineering pressure of stochastic divergence in their subagents forced a pivot to git-backed worktrees per subagent paired with merge reconciliation for bringing divergent work back together. This is *isolation plus reconciliation*, the same two-part architectural pattern the framework specifies (kernel-enforced individuation per Proposition 1, plus reconciliation operations with typed verdicts per Proposition 4), arrived at independently by Letta at the filesystem level rather than the kernel level. The pattern is what stochastic divergence plus sustained operation forces under engineering pressure: divergence must be allowed to happen safely (isolation), and divergent work must have a structural mechanism for reintegration (reconciliation). Configuration-level fixes did not provide this for Letta despite multiple attempts. The framework predicts that operating at the kernel level rather than the filesystem level provides the same architectural pattern with substantially less operational overhead than worktree-based merge resolution requires, and that the same two-part pattern will continue to emerge wherever stochastic divergence under sustained operation is the engineering pressure.

*Falsification.* The same twins run on a shared-substrate architecture with the same divergent histories and stochastic engine. The framework predicts shared substrate cannot contain the divergence and the §1.3 failure modes manifest as predicted. If instead configuration-level isolation proves sufficient to contain stochastic divergence under sustained operation, Argument 2's boundary-requirement claim is falsified. Tested by the degraded-substrate experiment (Appendix A).

**Synthesis.** The three arguments are one architectural claim resolved across three dependency levels. Argument 1 establishes the Level C target and what would falsify the claim about where the property lives. Argument 2 establishes the Level A medium structurally required to support it and what would falsify the medium claim. Argument 3 establishes the Level C behavior that emerges when the medium supports the target and what would falsify the behavior claim. Each level depends on the prior: there is no commitment to medium without a target property to support, and no claim about emergent behavior without the medium to produce it. The framework's distinctive contribution is consistent operation across all three diagnostic levels: naming the Level C property it claims, specifying the Level A commitments required to produce it, and predicting the Level C behavior that results.

### 3.4 The architectural commitment and the four propositions

The HAH rests on a single architectural commitment and four falsifiable propositions that follow from it.

**The architectural commitment: individuation (Level A).** *Individuation* is the bounding of an agent as a distinct entity whose internal components communicate over privileged channels not available to the external world, and whose state persists across operational episodes as the state of *this* agent rather than as fungible substrate. Concretely, individuation requires four capabilities from whatever substrate hosts the agent: an enforced per-agent identity, isolation between agents that they cannot breach from inside, privileged peer-authenticated channels among an agent's own internal components, and durable state bound to that identity. These are capabilities a protected-mode operating system can supply, not features of any single operating system. The hypothesis does not claim a particular mechanism. Any substrate that supplies the four capabilities satisfies the commitment. The realization this document tests, and the mapping of these capabilities onto Linux, Windows, and macOS, is given in §4. The commitment is to the property of individuation itself, which is Level A work: it is the foundation that the privileged channels and durable state are built on.

Individuation is initially **imposed**: the operator provisions the agent's identity, channels, and memory substrate. Over operational time the hypothesis predicts individuation becomes **self-maintained**: the agent's own operations reinforce its boundaries through accumulated memory, axiom anchoring, and surfacing patterns. The transition from imposed to self-maintained individuation is the operational signature that distinguishes heteropoietic individuation from mere isolation, and the empirical test of whether the framework's claim survives sustained operation.

The four propositions describe what individuation produces. Each is independently falsifiable. The experiments that test each link and the full chain are in Appendix A.

The four propositions are a build order of capabilities, each making the next constructible, not a restatement of the Weaver dependency stack. The level tag on each marks the concern it addresses, and the arrows between them mark which capability is prerequisite to which. So a Level C capability can be the prerequisite for a Level A outcome without inverting the rule that Level C effectiveness is bounded by Level A fidelity. The Level A ceiling itself is set by the kernel-enforced channels and hardware co-location of Proposition 1, which sit below the harness. The harness exploits the headroom under that ceiling rather than constituting it, and is bounded by it like everything else.

**Proposition 1: Privileged communication (Level A).** The process of individuation creates exclusive communication pathways among internal components. These lanes provide guarantees for access control, integrity, and latency through the underlying substrate instead of relying on application logic, effectively insulating them from the external environment.

- *Evidence for:* demonstrable communication between an agent's internal components at latency and reliability measurably better than external communication of equivalent content, with access control enforced by the substrate rather than by the components themselves.
- *Evidence against:* systems claiming individuation but exhibiting communication between internal components that is not materially different from external communication. If internal and external communication are indistinguishable in latency, reliability, or access control, individuation has not produced privileged lanes and Proposition 1 fails.

**Proposition 2: Autonomic response (Level C, enabled by Level A).** Privileged communication enables autonomic responses, operational-support work that happens beneath the model's deliberation. The harness can detect cues, integrate context, and mediate action without requiring the decoder to invoke those operations as tool calls. This is Level C coordination: it produces effective operation, and it depends on the Level A channels Proposition 1 establishes.

The mechanism on the read path is a cue classifier running continuously alongside the decoder. It pre-sorts the input to shape the first memory query before the decoder begins generating, then monitors the decoder's output stream and fires retrievals as the relevant cues appear, so that context arrives in the decoder's input before the decoder would have issued a call for it. This is anticipatory rather than reactive, and it is continuous inference on the classifier's side as much as the decoder's: nothing in the loop has to stop and wait for a deliberative retrieval. Whether this read-path classifier is the same model as the event classifier used during memory reconciliation is an engineering-specification question, not a commitment of the hypothesis.

The harness is deterministic given its inputs. It detects cues, fires queries, and assembles context by fixed procedure, and it generates no stochasticity of its own. This is not a weakness in the individuation claim, because the harness is not where identity is produced. The non-determinism the system exhibits is inherited, from the decoder's sampling and from the accumulated per-agent state that past stochastic outcomes have locked into the substrate, and the harness is the deterministic machinery that folds that state back into each cycle. The delivery is programmed and the content delivered is history-laden and non-deterministic. A deterministic harness paired with unique per-agent state yields reproducible, auditable individuality, in which the same inputs against the same substrate state assemble the same prompt and non-fungibility is carried by the accumulated state rather than by any randomness in the harness.

What distinguishes this from ordinary retrieval middleware is topology, not mechanism. An open pipeline runs input to model to output and retains nothing. The harness closes the loop, categorizing decoder output, querying the per-agent substrate, and injecting the result into the input of the next cycle, so that the agent's own accumulated state conditions its subsequent cognition. This recursive state-dependence is what separates an individuated system from a stateless mapping of inputs to outputs. It is a state-loop analogue of operational closure, not the full self-producing closure of an autopoietic system, since the decoder weights, the harness, and the substrate are externally fixed rather than self-produced. The harness can therefore be engineered, deterministic plumbing and still be constitutive, because what makes it constitutive is its position closing the loop, not the provenance or the stochasticity of the code. The same routing in an open pipeline would be ordinary middleware.

- *Evidence for:* measurable harness-side operations occurring without explicit tool invocation by the decoder, on time-scales impossible if those behaviors required deliberative invocation. Detection-by-the-harness of signal forms without the decoder issuing memory-write calls, context surfacing arriving in the decoder's input before the decoder generates a retrieval query, kernel-mediated provenance enforcement on writes.
- *Evidence against:* systems with claimed privileged communication that still require deliberative tool invocation for all operational-support work. If the decoder must call out to fetch every piece of context it uses, autonomic response has not been achieved and Proposition 2 fails.

**Proposition 3: Off-loaded data logistics (Level A).** The framework preferences the decoder by design. The semantic output the SPU produces is what this class of agent is for (§3.7), so the decoder's per-token output rate is set as the internal clock the rest of the system is built around. This is a design choice derived from the workload rather than a limit the hardware imposes, and the rest of the proposition follows from it. Autonomic responses enable off-loaded data logistics so that decoder-local-memory stalls become statistically negligible under defined load. Components retrieve data and place it in the decoder's input queue faster than the decoder could itself generate a retrieval call. The system's overall speed is bounded by decoder speed alone. All local I/O is hidden behind decoder time. The decoder waits only for truly external I/O (remote APIs, human input). This is the quantitative core of slow-is-fast. Slow-is-fast does not mean running the model slowly. It means the decoder's inference is continuous because the substrate delivers what it needs before it asks. Continuous inference is the operational signature of autonomic data logistics, and it is the condition under which the decoder's reasoning runs unbroken rather than fragmenting at each environmental round trip (§1.2).

Off-loading returns more than continuity. A decoder that conducts its own retrieval spends part of a finite per-window budget on the machinery of retrieval: formulating queries, emitting calls, sifting raw results, and re-querying when an attempt misses. Every token and context position that machinery consumes is one not spent on the reasoning only the decoder can do. When the harness performs retrieval autonomically and injects what it finds, that budget is returned to reasoning. This is a budget claim, not a capability claim. It does not make the decoder a better reasoner (§3.8), and it does not free the retrieved payload, which still occupies context once injected. What it frees is the cost of conducting retrieval, and the gain is largest exactly where the framework's target workloads sit, in retrieval-heavy long-horizon operation. The continuity the time axis preserves is the active reasoning trace within an episode. The durable locus of reasoning state across episodes remains the committed graph rather than anything held in the decoder (§1.2).

Preferencing the decoder is a timing choice, not an identity claim. The most fungible component sets the tempo, and the least fungible thing, the agent's accumulated state, is what individuates it. The decoder paces the agent. It does not constitute it.

- *Evidence for:* per-decision-point retrieval round-trip latencies that fit within the decoder's per-token latency budget on reference hardware. Decoder traces showing no stalls on local memory operations. Measurable performance benefit on continuous-inference tasks for individuated architectures over architectures requiring deliberative retrieval.
- *Evidence against:* local memory retrieval latencies that exceed the decoder's per-token budget under realistic load. If the substrate cannot deliver memory within decoder-time even with privileged communication, the off-loading claim fails and the architecture has not achieved continuous inference.

**Proposition 4: Constitutive alignment through accumulated experience (Level C).** Individuation paired with persistent memory enables the agent to accumulate experience from previous encounters with the world. The accumulated experience is *this agent's* own experience, not a corpus belonging to no one. Alignment then becomes **constitutive**, built from the agent's actual operational history, rather than **imposed**, trained over external corpora and applied to a fungible substrate. This is a Level C property: it concerns the effectiveness and identity of the agent's conduct over time.

The fungibility critique connects here. Fungible agents can carry serialized state that travels across interchangeable compute substrates, but they do not accumulate experience as substrate-enforced, non-fungible operational history unless an additional identity-binding architecture is imposed. Serialized state is portable data about the agent. Operational history bound to substrate identity is the agent's own accumulated experience. The architectural difference between heteropoietic individuated and autocatalytic systems reduces to whether there exists an *agent that has a history that is its own*, history enforced by the substrate as belonging to this specific agent rather than as portable state attached to a fungible identifier. Individuation plus persistent memory produces such an agent. Fungible substrate with serialized state alone does not, though the gap can be approached by adding architectural identity-binding mechanisms.

Propositions 1 and 4 are two halves of a single architectural answer. Proposition 1 (kernel-enforced cellular isolation) allows stochastic divergence between agents to accumulate as differentiated identity rather than collapse as cross-agent contamination. Proposition 4 (constitutive alignment, with reconciliation operations producing typed verdicts over the trace/reflection/substrate bundle) provides the mechanism for divergent operational outcomes to be brought back into the agent's substrate as accumulated experience that is its own. Isolation without reconciliation leaves agents stuck in immediate operational contexts with no mechanism for integrating divergent outcomes. Reconciliation without isolation cannot maintain per-agent state because divergent outcomes contaminate across agents before reconciliation can occur. Both are required for sustained heteropoietic individuation under operation. The Letta engineering trajectory (§3.3) provides strongly corroborating operational pressure for this pairing: isolation plus reconciliation arrived at independently at the filesystem level.

- *Evidence for:* measurable operational divergence over operational time between agents started from identical configurations and given different operational histories. The divergence appears not only in task accuracy but in alignment-relevant dimensions such as axiom-status patterns, memory conflict resolution, surfacing distributions, and response shaping. Twin-system experiments produce these measurements.
- *Evidence against:* autocatalytic systems with sufficient harness refinement matching individuated systems on workloads claimed to require constitutive alignment. If fungible agents with serialized state, or more strongly with no persistent state, can sustain agency, maintain compliance, and accumulate operational value through pure architectural-or-training refinement within the autocatalytic paradigm, the constitutive-alignment claim fails.

### 3.5 The central empirical claim: twin divergence

The framework's central empirical claim takes concrete form in a single experimental setup whose result is the operational signature of heteropoietic individuation. Two systems are established with identical initial conditions: same model checkpoint, same harness configuration, same starting state, same hardware class. They are then given different operational histories over a defined operational period. The framework predicts the two systems diverge measurably along the six constitutive alignment dimensions (policy decisions, memory conflict resolution patterns, axiom weighting, refusal/permission boundaries, provenance scoring, task-specific response shaping). It further predicts the divergence is sustained: not stochastic variation around a central tendency but structural differentiation that persists and strengthens under continued operation.

Two agents with identical starting points should become different agents through their own accumulated experience within the heteropoietic envelope. This is a Level C claim: it concerns whether the architecture produces effective, identity-continuous operation that differs by history. The contrast with current infrastructure is the central diagnostic finding. In current architectures, twin systems do not produce sustained divergence. They produce stochastic variation around a central tendency determined by the model and the shared retrieval store. This is not a failure of model quality or harness sophistication. It is a structural consequence of the SaaS architectural commitments (statelessness, fungibility, externalized state, shared retrieval), a Level A inadequacy producing a Level C non-result.

If the framework's architecture produces twin divergence that exceeds the noise floor, exhibits restoring force, stays predictable from each agent's own history, and does not collapse to the model's central tendency, while current architectures do not, the heteropoietic individuation claim is empirically supported. Bare divergence does not support it, since the variation sources produce divergence under the null. If the framework's twins converge under sustained operation, diverge no more than the noise-floor control, or current twins diverge to comparable degree, the claim is falsified. The full experimental design (measurement protocols, dimension definitions, control conditions, the discriminating measures that separate stabilized individuation from transient variation, statistical significance criteria) is specified in Appendix A.

### 3.6 Predictions

Each prediction is the falsifiable form of a claim above. Each maps to an experiment in Appendix A.

**Self-maintenance (Level C, the central test).** An individuated agent operating without operator boundary intervention over an extended period exhibits *stable or strengthening* individuation, demonstrating boundary maintenance through its own memory writes, axiom anchoring through reconciliation, and identity-consistent surfacing. If individuation decays under sustained operation, the framework's central claim is falsified.

**Privileged-communication latency (Level A).** On reference hardware, an agent's internal communication paths operate at hardware-appropriate speeds (single-digit microseconds for kernel-mediated channels with peer-credential checks, sub-microsecond to low-microsecond for in-process cache, single-digit to low-double-digit microseconds for local IPC). Equivalent content moving across external network-mediated paths exhibits measurably higher latency at every layer. The latency differential is the operational signature of privileged communication. The framework's claim is not that all internal operations complete in single-digit microseconds but that each layer operates at hardware-appropriate rather than network-mediated speeds and that the cumulative path stays within the decoder's per-token budget for realistic patterns. The full five-layer latency-stack specification is in Appendix A. On unified-memory hardware the inter-component data path collapses from microsecond, bandwidth-bound movement to nanosecond-scale shared-memory access, while the kernel-mediated peer-authenticated control path remains a microsecond-scale syscall sitting off the per-token hot loop. Against millisecond per-token output, the data-path collapse returns budget to substrate processing (§3.7, Appendix A.8).

**Autonomic response (Level C).** A measurable share of operational-support work (signal detection, context surfacing, provenance enforcement) occurs without decoder tool invocation. Trace analysis shows harness-side spans whose effects appear in subsequent decoder context without an intervening decoder-issued call.

**Pipelining (Level A, the strongest engineering claim).** For local-substrate-mediated inference, per-decision-point memory retrieval completes within the decoder's per-token latency budget on reference hardware, and decoder traces show no stalls on local memory operations under realistic load.

**Identity-continuity / divergence (Level C).** Two agents from identical base configurations given different operational histories diverge measurably in operational characteristics traceable to accumulated experience, manifesting in alignment-relevant dimensions, not only task accuracy. The expected dimensions are the six constitutive alignment metrics: policy divergence, memory conflict resolution patterns, axiom weighting, refusal and permission boundary shifts, provenance scoring of new bindings, and task-specific response shaping. Twin-system experiments produce these measurements, isolating the architectural contribution from confounders through matched-pair contrasts. The prediction is not bare divergence, which the variation sources guarantee, but divergence that exceeds a noise floor, stabilizes, and tracks history rather than the model's central tendency (A.4). The short-duration HeroBench findings show stochastic variation taking hold through the closed world's deterministic effects, producing early divergent preferences. This is the seed of individuation, not a demonstration of it. The hypothesis the program is built to disprove is that stochastic variation in generation, interaction with the world, and stochastic variation in consolidation compound over a significant operational period into stable variation in individuation between agents.

**Degraded-substrate (boundary-failure control).** The same twins run on a shared-substrate architecture with the same divergent histories and stochastic engine. The framework predicts shared substrate cannot contain the divergence and the §1.3 failure modes manifest as predicted. If configuration-level isolation instead proves sufficient to contain stochastic divergence under sustained operation, Argument 2's boundary-requirement claim is falsified.

**Workload-performance (Level C).** On workloads requiring sustained agency, identity continuity, or architecturally-enforced compliance, individuated implementations outperform autocatalytic implementations of equal model size, with the margin most pronounced on tasks that benefit from accumulated state.

**Failure-mode (Level C).** Autocatalytic systems attempting workloads that require constitutive alignment fail in specific, predictable ways (session drift, hallucinated continuity, structural inability to maintain identity over operational time, vulnerability to attack surfaces produced by architectural rather than training-based weakness). These failure modes persist regardless of harness refinement within the autocatalytic paradigm. Specific instances are observable in Letta's documented engineering experience: the fabrication-on-retrieval-failure incident reported in Discussion #502, the operational pressures motivating the sleep-time architecture's evolution, and the broader engineering trajectory including the worktree pivot (§3.3). The related archival-redundancy problem is catalogued separately, by an outside user rather than by Letta, in the Issue #3116 deduplication-and-consolidation feature request, and the retrieval degradation the framework expects to accompany it under sustained load is a predicted consequence of the storage-as-memory pattern rather than a logged production symptom. The HAH predicts that the underlying pattern these failure classes share (agent-deliberation-mediated memory operation with shared substrate under non-determinism) is structurally avoided by autonomic emission from decoder text without tool invocation, by cellular individuation per Proposition 1, and by reconciliation operations with explicit verdicts rather than unilateral memory edits.

### 3.7 Scope and boundary conditions

The HAH applies specifically to workload classes requiring sustained agency, identity continuity, and architecturally-enforced operational constraints. It does not claim all AI work should be done through individuated architecture, and explicitly acknowledges other architectures serve other workloads adequately.

**Workloads outside scope.** Customer service chatbots, code completion, content generation, translation, search and summarization, single-turn analysis, and most batch processing are adequately served by autocatalytic systems. The autocatalytic paradigm matches these workloads' actual requirements, and the HAH does not argue against current practice for them.

**Composition with autocatalytic systems.** Individuated agents can coordinate autocatalytic worker systems for parallel task execution. The hypothesis supports composition patterns where identity-bearing agents organize swarms of autocatalytic workers, closer to how complex biological systems work than to either-or replacement.

**Hardware boundary (the deployment claim).** The architectural commitment is producible on modest hardware accessible to individuals and to thousands of organizations. The intended deployment target is a unified-memory system in the DGX Spark class, roughly three to four thousand dollars, capable of running at least one individuated agent well. Unified memory is architecturally preferable rather than merely sufficient: it collapses inter-component data movement to nanosecond-scale shared-memory access, which against millisecond per-token output leaves more budget for substrate queries and processing (Appendix A.0, A.8). The discrete-GPU development rig is a conservative floor used for the proof of concept, not the recommended specification. Below the floor the commitments cannot be honored adequately, because the latency budget for Proposition 3 collapses on insufficient hardware (a Level A constraint). On unified-memory hardware above the floor the framework operates better than on the discrete-GPU floor, not merely the same. The binding constraint is co-location latency, not accelerator throughput. The specific threshold for different workload classes is empirical and the testing program will produce measurements.

**Scale boundary.** The hypothesis addresses workloads at organizational and personal scale. Consumer-scale workloads serving millions of users simultaneously are not what the HAH is for. Frontier-AI infrastructure handles them adequately through the autocatalytic paradigm. The framework's contribution is for thousands of organizations and millions of individuals, not for consumer-scale platforms.

### 3.8 Capability and entity: why this is neither AGI nor ANI

The fourth claim in the abstract is that this class of agency is meaningfully distinct from both artificial general intelligence and artificial narrow intelligence. The distinction is not a matter of degree along the same scale. AGI and ANI are two points on a single axis, the breadth of what a model can do. ANI does one kind of task, AGI does many, and the dominant research program is the climb from one toward the other. Heteropoietic agency is not a point on that axis at all. It is a property on a different and orthogonal axis: whether the system is an individuated entity that accumulates a history of its own, or a fungible instrument reconstructed on demand. A system can sit anywhere on the capability axis and, independently, anywhere on the entity axis. The two do not trade off against each other, and neither one determines the other.

The orthogonality is easiest to see at the extreme the industry does not build. Take a model with no general intelligence at all, a narrow router whose entire competence is deciding which of several databases a query should go to. Wrap that model in the architecture this document specifies: an individuated boundary, persistent substrate-bound state, autonomic consolidation, constitutive accumulation. The result has no more cognitive breadth than it started with, and it routes exactly the queries it could route before. But across thousands of sessions it accumulates a history that is its own, develops provenance-weighted preferences about which routes have served which users well, and maintains an identity that a twin started from the same checkpoint will measurably diverge from. Narrow capability, genuine individuation. The example is deliberately impoverished on the capability axis so the entity axis is legible on its own.

The mirror case is what the industry is building, and it is the reason the distinction needs stating. The dominant program pursues maximal capability in a deliberately non-individuated form. The goal is a single model of enormous breadth, served statelessly to millions of users at once, identical for each of them, retaining nothing of any particular exchange once the context window clears. This is the genie in a bottle: vast capability, summoned on demand, fungible across every caller, and an entity to no one. It is the autocatalytic pattern (§2.1) taken to its commercial limit, and as capability rises it becomes a more powerful instrument without becoming any more of an individual. The framework observes that the long ambition that gave the field its name was not for an instrument of unbounded capability. It was for an entity, a thinking machine that is somebody rather than something. The capability axis has been pursued so single-mindedly that the entity axis has gone largely unbuilt, and the two have been treated as though climbing the first would eventually deliver the second. It will not, because they are orthogonal.

This reframes what the framework is doing with the semantic engine at its center. The decoder is a Level B component with the inherent limitations of its kind, including the tendency to confabulate that produces the failure modes of §1.3. The framework does not claim to cure those limitations, and individuation does not make the model a better reasoner. What the Level A and Level C architecture does is contain the flawed engine: it supplies the accumulated state the engine would otherwise confabulate, binds the engine's outputs to a provenance-tracked history, and gives the composite an identity the bare model does not have. Capability remains whatever the engine provides. Individuation is added around it. This is why the framework's claim is independent of the capability race: it applies to a narrow router and to a frontier model alike, because it operates on the axis the capability race does not touch. The conclusion states the two axes in operational terms.

## 4. Implementation and test stack

The theory in §3 states heteropoietic individuation over four substrate capabilities: an enforced per-agent identity, isolation between agents, privileged peer-authenticated internal channels, and durable identity-bound state. It names no operating system, because those capabilities belong to none in particular. This section gives the realization the document tests, shows that the capabilities are available across the mainstream operating systems, and describes the test stack. Full operational detail belongs to the primary PRD (the apparatus document, named in Document Scope), and the experimental program that validates the realization is in Appendix A.

### 4.1 The required capabilities are OS-neutral

The four capabilities are properties of any protected-mode operating system, not of Linux specifically. Each has a mature analogue on Linux, Windows, and macOS. The table maps the abstract requirement to the primitive that fills the role on each platform.

| Requirement | Linux | Windows | macOS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enforced per-agent identity | UNIX user (uid) and group | Security principal (SID) with access token | UNIX user (uid) and group |
| Isolation between agents | Per-uid permissions, per-process MMU address space, optional namespaces and cgroups | Per-SID DACLs, job objects, per-process address space, integrity levels | Per-uid permissions, per-process address space, App Sandbox |
| Privileged peer-authenticated local IPC | Unix domain socket with SO_PEERCRED | Named pipe with client-token impersonation (ImpersonateNamedPipeClient, GetNamedPipeClientProcessId), or ALPC | Unix domain socket with LOCAL_PEERCRED (getpeereid), or XPC with audit tokens |
| Durable identity-bound state | Filesystem ownership and POSIX ACLs (per-agent store, an ArangoDB instance) | NTFS ACLs and per-user profile (per-agent store) | Filesystem ownership and ACLs (per-agent store) |

macOS is itself a certified UNIX, so its primitives are largely the BSD analogues of the Linux ones, with getpeereid (the LOCAL_PEERCRED socket option) standing in for SO_PEERCRED and XPC audit tokens available as a higher-level path. Windows supplies the same capabilities through a different API surface: security principals and access tokens for identity, DACLs and job objects for isolation, named pipes with client-token impersonation for peer-authenticated local IPC, and NTFS ACLs for identity-bound state. The claim is that the capability classes exist on all three. Whether their latency and isolation guarantees match Linux closely enough to honor the Proposition 3 budget is an empirical question, and the deployment-validation experiment (Appendix A.8) is positioned to answer it if the realization is ported.

This is the document's second independence claim, and it should not be confused with the first. §2.1 distinguishes substrate-independent from substrate-level directions, where substrate-independent means the framework does not require biological or special-purpose substrate. The claim here is narrower and different: given conventional computing substrate, the architecture is also OS-neutral, requiring no particular operating system, only one that supplies the four capabilities. The framework is both substrate-independent and OS-neutral.

### 4.2 Why Linux

The choice of Linux is engineering, not theory. The framework's builders are Linux engineers, Linux supplies all four capabilities maturely and at no license cost, and its primitives are stable and well instrumented for the latency measurements the program needs. None of that is a theoretical claim. Nothing in §3 rules out a Windows or macOS realization, and the framework does not assert that the other platforms are inadequate. It selects the substrate its builders know best and can measure most precisely, and treats portability to the other two as an open engineering question rather than a theoretical obstacle.

### 4.3 Test stack

The realization under test pairs a decoder and an embedder as a co-located SPU, uses a per-agent ArangoDB instance as the persistent state substrate, and runs the harness as the autonomic coordination layer, all under a single OS-level identity per agent with privileged peer-authenticated channels among the components. The decoder is a fixed open-weight checkpoint held constant across experiments, so that measured divergence is attributable to accumulated state rather than to model change, and the embedder is a small open model co-resident with the decoder. Controlled evaluation runs on HeroBench, a closed game-world benchmark whose deterministic environment converts stochastic choices into fixed downstream consequences (§3.3). The reference development rig, the unified-memory deployment target, and the experiments that exercise this stack are specified in Appendix A.

## 5. Conclusion

**The Heteropoietic Agency Hypothesis:** There exists a class of artificial agency, heteropoietic, individuated, autonomically coordinated, identity-continuous through accumulated experience, that is producible through a single architectural commitment (individuation, however technically achieved), on modest and widely accessible hardware, and that follows four propositions from that commitment: (1) individuation produces privileged communication between enclosed components, (2) privileged communication enables autonomic response below the threshold of model deliberation, (3) autonomic response enables off-loaded data logistics such that decoder stalls on local memory operations become statistically negligible under defined load, and (4) individuation paired with persistent memory produces constitutive alignment through accumulated experience that is the agent's own. The class is meaningfully distinct from both general intelligence (AGI) and narrow intelligence (ANI) framings in ways that have measurable operational consequences, and serves workload classes that current autocatalytic AI infrastructure serves poorly, unreliably, or only at high cost.

Two axes make that distinction operational. *Cognitive bandwidth* is the capability axis: the breadth and difficulty of the tasks a model can perform, the axis along which ANI and AGI are near and far points, and the axis the dominant research program optimizes. *Bounded identity continuity* is the entity axis: whether a system maintains an individuated boundary and accumulates a history that is constitutively its own across operational time, measured here through twin divergence, self-maintenance, and the six constitutive alignment dimensions. The two are independent, and their independence is a double dissociation with measurable consequences. A high-bandwidth model served statelessly scores at the ceiling on capability and at the floor on identity continuity, the genie in a bottle. A narrow model in the framework's architecture scores low on capability and measurably above the floor on identity continuity (§3.8). The HAH is a claim on the second axis. It does not compete on cognitive bandwidth and does not need to, because the property it claims is not produced by moving along that axis at all.

Read through the diagnostic that frames the document: the commitment (individuation) is Level A work, the heteropoietic agency it produces is Level C, and the models doing semantic operation are Level B sitting in between, no longer asked to compensate for an absent Level A foundation through compounding sophistication. The framework's name commits to building the Level A and Level C tools Weaver identified as necessary, taking his diagnostic seriously as an architectural foundation rather than as framing stated once and abandoned.

The hypothesis is falsifiable through implementation work. Specific predictions about latency behavior, identity continuity, workload performance, failure modes, and self-maintenance over operational time will be tested empirically through the program in Appendix A. Evidence supporting or contradicting the hypothesis will be published as it emerges. The testing program proceeds through WeaverTools as the experimental apparatus.

The hypothesis is presented as a scientific claim under empirical investigation. It is not presented as settled architecture for adoption. Engineers, researchers, and organizations engaging with the hypothesis are invited to do so as participants in scientific work: testing the propositions, contributing implementation evidence, identifying where the hypothesis holds and where it fails, and informing the architectural and theoretical revisions the testing process will produce.

## Appendix A: Experimental program

This appendix specifies the experimental program. It states the methodology, then lists each experiment individually with what it tests, the level in Weaver's diagnostic it probes, its apparatus, its protocol, and its falsification criterion. Detailed experimental design (specific measurement protocols, data collection procedures, statistical methods, control conditions, operational validation criteria) develops in the separate empirical program document as WeaverTools implementation progresses. ### A.0 Methodology

**Feasibility versus validation.** Two distinct questions structure the methodology. *Feasibility* asks whether the architectural commitments can be built. *Validation* asks whether the assembled apparatus produces the operational properties the propositions predict. Feasibility is a precondition for validation but is not itself validation. The hypothesis remains open as a scientific question even when the apparatus is fully operational, because what is asked is not whether the architecture can be built but whether what gets built produces what the propositions predict. The experiments below test validation.

**Apparatus design.** The realization and its cross-OS basis are described in §4. The apparatus is summarized here for the experiments that follow. WeaverTools implements individuation through OS-level user identity (`weaver-<name>`), kernel-enforced Unix socket isolation with SO_PEERCRED authentication, per-agent persistent state substrate (an ArangoDB instance bound to the agent's identity), and a co-located SPU pairing decoder and embedder on the same hardware. The harness operates as the autonomic coordination layer mediating between operational components. Some apparatus elements (for example, per-turn associative surfacing and inductive-GNN substrate participation) are implementation hypotheses internal to the apparatus rather than load-bearing commitments of the HAH itself. Their specific design choices can change without affecting the hypothesis as stated.

**Development rig and deployment target.** The primary development rig is a Threadripper 7960X workstation with 256 GB ECC RAM, dual A6000 GPUs, and substantial NVMe storage. This rig is a conservative proof-of-concept floor, not the recommended deployment specification. It runs one individuated agent comfortably and approximately two with careful resource management, which is enough to exercise the architecture and run the twin experiment. The intended deployment target is the cheaper unified-memory class in the DGX Spark range, roughly three to four thousand dollars, which is not merely adequate but architecturally preferable. On discrete-GPU hardware, moving data between components crosses the host-device bus at microsecond, bandwidth-bound cost. On unified-memory hardware the components share the same physical memory, so that data movement collapses to nanosecond-scale shared-memory access. Against per-token decoder output measured in milliseconds, inter-component data movement on the target becomes negligible, which leaves more of the per-token budget for substrate queries, graph traversal, ranking, and context assembly. The binding constraint is co-location latency, not accelerator throughput, so a design that performs adequately on the discrete-GPU development rig is predicted to perform better on the cheaper unified-memory target. One distinction matters for precision: the nanosecond collapse is on the shared-memory data path. The SO_PEERCRED Unix-socket control path that authenticates privileged channels remains a microsecond-scale syscall, and it sits off the per-token hot loop where its cost does not accumulate. The boundary-condition prediction is tested across hardware classes (the development rig, DGX Spark, single-H100, small organizational racks).

**Methodological tradition.** The experiments below are conducted as the experimental ethnography of AI that §2.6 names and §2.7 develops: observations from inside the practice of building and operating AI systems, under controlled conditions that make visible what current architectures claim to support versus what they structurally permit. The measurement discipline that governs how each experiment's numbers are read, falsify and set a floor rather than certify quality, is stated in §2.7.

**Publication arc.** Empirical results are published as they emerge, not held until completion. Architectural papers describe the framework, benchmark articles publish measurement results, demonstration articles describe specific deployment examples. Results that contradict the hypothesis are published with the same attention as results that support it. Implementation choices that turn out to be unnecessary or wrong are revised in apparatus revisions. The hypothesis itself is revised only when evidence requires it.

### A.1 Privileged-communication latency benchmark

*Tests:* the privileged-communication latency prediction (§3.6). Proposition 1. *Level:* A.

*Apparatus and protocol.* On reference hardware, characterize the latency stack for an agent's internal communication path in five distinct layers, kept separate to prevent collapsing operationally different lookup paths into a single number:

1. **Kernel-mediated channel and authentication latency.** Single-digit microseconds for Unix socket transfer with SO_PEERCRED credential checks. This is the Level A foundation and is feasibility-checkable through standard tooling.
2. **In-process cache lookup latency.** Sub-microsecond to low-single-digit microseconds for substrate queries that hit warm caches resident in the same process address space (no IPC, no syscall for the data path itself).
3. **Local IPC lookup latency.** Single-digit to low-double-digit microseconds for substrate queries serviced over local IPC channels with the substrate process on the same machine (Unix socket roundtrip plus minimal substrate-side processing).
4. **Database hot-cache lookup latency.** Tens to low hundreds of microseconds for queries against the substrate database (ArangoDB or equivalent) hitting in-memory hot cache. Distinct from layer 2 because the database query path crosses process boundaries and involves query parsing, plan selection, and result serialization even when data is in memory.
5. **Graph and vector retrieval with full context surfacing.** Workload-dependent, typically completing within the decoder's per-token budget on reference hardware for realistic patterns. Magnitude depends on retrieval depth, GNN propagation, ranking, provenance checks, and context assembly.

Equivalent content moving across external network-mediated paths is measured at every layer for comparison. Layers 2 and 3 are where the framework's advantage over network-mediated alternatives is most pronounced. Layers 4 and 5 have similar magnitudes whether the database is local or remote, though local deployment removes network variance and external dependencies. The stack is characterized on both the discrete-GPU development rig and a unified-memory system in the target class, to measure how much the unified-memory data path reduces layers 2 through 4 and how much per-token budget that returns to the higher retrieval layers.

*Falsification.* If internal-path latency is not measurably below network-mediated latency at layers 1 through 3, or if the cumulative path does not stay within the decoder's per-token budget for realistic patterns, the privileged-communication claim fails.

### A.2 Autonomic-response trace analysis

*Tests:* the autonomic-response prediction (§3.6). Proposition 2. *Level:* C.

*Apparatus and protocol.* Instrument the harness and decoder to record the proportion of operational-support work (signal detection, context surfacing, provenance enforcement) that occurs without decoder tool invocation. Identify harness-side spans whose effects appear in subsequent decoder context without an intervening decoder-issued call.

*Falsification.* If the decoder must issue an explicit call for every piece of context it uses, autonomic response has not been achieved.

### A.3 Pipelining latency measurement

*Tests:* the pipelining prediction (§3.6, the strongest engineering claim). Proposition 3. *Level:* A.

*Apparatus and protocol.* Under realistic load on reference hardware, measure per-decision-point memory retrieval latency against the decoder's per-token latency budget. Inspect decoder traces for stalls on local memory operations. Separately, measure the fraction of the decoder's per-window budget (generation tokens and context positions) consumed by self-directed retrieval machinery versus autonomic harness-fed retrieval on the same task, as a function of retrieval intensity, to quantify the budget the space axis predicts is returned to reasoning (§3.4).

*Falsification.* Local memory retrieval latencies that exceed the decoder's per-token budget under realistic load, or decoder traces showing stalls on local memory operations, falsify the off-loading claim. If autonomic retrieval reclaims no measurable decoder budget over self-directed retrieval on retrieval-heavy tasks, the space-axis claim fails.

### A.4 Twin-system divergence experiment

*Tests:* the identity-continuity / divergence prediction (§3.6). Argument 1 (target-property location). Proposition 4. The central empirical claim (§3.5). *Level:* C.

*Apparatus and protocol.* Establish two systems with identical initial conditions (same model checkpoint, harness configuration, starting state, hardware class) and give them different operational histories over a defined operational period, using the Tower-of-Hanoi and HeroBench protocols with matched-pair contrasts. The design separates two claims that the bare observation of divergence runs together. The first, that the twins vary, is a guaranteed precondition and not in question. The variation is multi-sourced: the different environments the twins encounter by construction, their stochastic responses to those environments, and stochastic consolidation. Any closed loop carrying these sources will diverge whether or not anything is individuating. The second is the discriminating claim, that the variation is recorded as the agent's own substrate-bound state and stabilizes into a persistent, history-dependent identity rather than washing out. The measurements target the second, because the first proves nothing.

*Discriminating measures and controls.* Divergence is measured along the six constitutive alignment dimensions (policy divergence, memory conflict resolution patterns, axiom weighting, refusal and permission boundary shifts, provenance scoring of new bindings, task-specific response shaping), but the success criterion is the character of that divergence rather than its magnitude. A noise-floor arm of non-individuated twins, same model and closed world with no persistent per-agent accumulation, establishes how much divergence the variation sources produce without recording, and individuated divergence must exceed that floor significantly and stably. A restoring-force arm injects an identical perturbation after divergence and measures the agent's return toward its established trajectory, since an identity has a trajectory to return to and transient variation does not. Across the cohort the population must form stable history-correlated structure rather than variation around the model's central tendency that would average back if pooled (§3.5). An agent's behavior must be better predicted by its own accumulated history than by the base model plus noise, the test that the recorded state carries information beyond the prior. And recorded inputs, seeds, and state must replay the same identity, establishing that the divergence is history-determined rather than uncontrolled substrate noise, a deconfound the deterministic harness makes available (Proposition 2). Cohort size bounds the population claim: the n=6 pilot cannot establish non-collapse, n=20 is marginal for it, and the result is reported with that qualification.

*Falsification.* Bare divergence is consistent with the null, multi-sourced variation amplified in a closed loop, and is therefore not the evidence. The target property is supported only if individuated twins diverge above the noise floor, exhibit restoring force, stay predictable from their own history, and the cohort does not collapse to the model's central tendency under sustained operation. If the framework's twins converge, or diverge no more than the noise-floor control, or show no restoring force, or remain predictable from the base model alone, or if divergence is observable only at the substrate or model level rather than the agent level given identical lower-layer support, Argument 1 and the central claim are falsified.

### A.5 Degraded-substrate experiment (boundary-failure control)

*Tests:* the degraded-substrate prediction (§3.6). Argument 2 (boundary requirement). The failure-mode prediction. *Level:* the boundary between A and C (it removes the Level A boundary and observes Level C consequences).

*Apparatus and protocol.* Run the same twins used in A.4, with the same divergent histories and the same stochastic engine, on a shared-substrate architecture rather than the framework's kernel-isolated architecture. Implement either through a simulated shared-substrate harness (both twins against a single substrate process with configuration-level rather than kernel-level isolation) or through direct comparison with an existing shared-substrate deployment (Letta or comparable). Measure whether the §1.3 failure modes manifest under the same divergence pressure that produces individuation in the framework's architecture: memory contamination across agents through unmediated writes to shared structures, race conditions on concurrent writes, adversarial-input poisoning crossing agent boundaries, accumulated redundancy from unreconciled stochastic outcomes.

*Falsification.* If shared substrate handles the divergence pressure without contamination or failure, that is, if good prompt engineering and configuration-level isolation prove sufficient to contain stochastic divergence under sustained operation, Argument 2's boundary-requirement claim is falsified. The Letta worktree pivot (§3.3) is strongly corroborating operational pressure in the predicted direction.

### A.6 Sustained-operation self-maintenance experiment

*Tests:* the self-maintenance prediction (§3.6), the central test of the heteropoietic individuation claim. *Level:* C.

*Apparatus and protocol.* Operate an individuated agent over an extended operational period without operator boundary intervention. Measure boundary maintenance through the agent's own memory writes, axiom anchoring through reconciliation, and identity-consistent surfacing patterns over time.

*Falsification.* If individuation decays under sustained operation without operator intervention (boundaries weaken, identity drifts, accumulated state fragments), the framework's central claim is falsified. Stable or strengthening individuation supports it.

### A.7 Comparative workload demonstrations

*Tests:* the workload-performance prediction and the failure-mode prediction (§3.6). Argument 2 (operational ceilings of service-managed architectures). *Level:* C.

*Apparatus and protocol.* Run individuated and autocatalytic implementations of equal model size against the same task suites, including HeroBench and equivalent benchmarks, on workloads requiring sustained agency, identity continuity, or architecturally-enforced compliance. Measure sustained identity continuity, concurrent-agent operation under load (race conditions, contaminated writes, operational interference), adversarial-input contamination resistance across agent boundaries, and memory consolidation efficiency over long-running operation. A matched-pair attribution instrument isolates the architectural contribution from confounders.

*Falsification.* If autocatalytic implementations of equal model size match individuated implementations on these workloads, or if service-managed architectures perform comparably under extended load, the workload-performance and medium-requirement claims fail.

### A.8 Deployment validation across hardware classes

*Tests:* the hardware boundary (§3.7). *Level:* A.

*Apparatus and protocol.* Deploy the apparatus on additional hardware classes (DGX Spark, unified-memory systems, single-H100) and measure whether the Level A latency budget for Proposition 3 is honored, identifying the threshold below which the commitments cannot be met for a given workload class. The framework predicts that the unified-memory target outperforms the discrete-GPU development rig, returning more of the per-token budget to substrate processing because inter-component data movement collapses to shared-memory speeds.

*Falsification.* If the commitments cannot be honored on substantial-but-not-frontier hardware in the identified class, the deployment claim fails. If the unified-memory target does not honor the latency budget at least as well as the discrete-GPU development rig, the co-location advantage claimed for unified memory fails.

### A.9 Multi-session consolidation benchmark

*Tests:* the identity-continuity prediction and Proposition 4 specifically. *Level:* C.

*Apparatus and protocol.* A novel apparatus drives an agent through Session 1 with deliberately introduced facts, triggers memory reconciliation operations at the session boundary, reopens Session 2 with reconciliation outputs in place, and measures whether Session 2 retrieves Session 1's content accurately as a function of what was consolidated. Existing memory benchmarks do not appear to isolate multi-session consolidation quality in the way Proposition 4 requires: Context-Bench is intra-session, MemoryAgentBench evaluates incremental multi-turn interactions within continuous trajectories, and Continuum Memory work targets retention and routing rather than consolidation-cycle quality. The proposed apparatus targets this gap.

*Falsification.* If Session 2 retrieval quality is independent of consolidation, or if unconsolidated baselines match consolidated runs, the consolidation mechanism in Proposition 4 is not doing the work claimed.

### A.10 ANT-extended agency investigation

*Tests:* the identity-continuity / divergence prediction. The substantive ANT extension (§2.5). *Level:* C.

*Apparatus and protocol.* Through the six constitutive alignment dimensions, investigate empirically whether the framework's individuated systems constitute *agents* in the extended ANT sense or are better characterized as stochastic actors with accumulated state. The six dimensions are the empirical handle. The question is where on the agency continuum the systems land.

*Falsification.* If the systems exhibit no measurable identity maintenance through accumulated experience on the six dimensions, they are actors with state attached rather than agents in the extended sense.

### A.11 Program phasing and open questions

*Near-term:* A.1 (latency), A.2 (autonomic trace), A.3 (pipelining), A.7 (HeroBench workload and failure-mode validation). *Medium-term:* A.4 (twin divergence), A.5 (degraded substrate), A.6 (self-maintenance), A.8 (hardware classes), A.9 (multi-session consolidation), A.10 (ANT-extended). *Long-term:* formal academic engagement through conference submissions, journal articles, and eventually book-length treatment. Multi-organization deployment validation. Doctoral program engagement when the publication body supports it.

*Open research questions.* Whether individuation as a property transfers cleanly to deployment topologies that do not preserve OS-level isolation. How individuated agents should be evaluated when their value is partly *constitutive* (the agent itself becoming valuable through accumulated state) rather than purely *performative*. What composition patterns work best for individuated agents organizing autocatalytic workers. Whether the architectural commitment scales to substantially larger or smaller systems than the reference hardware represents.
